**Focus:** Two IELTS-style descriptions, accurate tense choices, natural vocabulary, and linking words for conclusions, emphasis, examples, conditions, and time.

## 1. Warm-up: Weather Preferences

**Practice conversation:** Describe preferred weather and use future expressions to discuss an expected change in temperature.

**Added practice sentences:**

- **It's still hot, but the temperature is expected to drop in a few days.**
- **I prefer cool weather because I feel more comfortable outdoors.**
- **I enjoy watching the rain, especially when I have nowhere to rush to.**

**Useful distinction:** **The weather is normal for this time of year** describes what is typical. **The weather is mild** describes weather that is not very hot or cold. They do not mean the same thing.

## 2. A Peaceful Place: A Wetland Park

### Reported details

- **Location:** the wetland park in the city, **within easy reach**.
- **Description:** A park with waterways, trees and places to walk.
- **First visit:** In **[year]**, when the learner started working in the city.
- **Routine:** Usually visits on **some evenings**, often with her friend.
- **Activities:** Renting a boat, watching birds, and looking at waterways, traditional villages, and different types of trees.
- **Feelings:** Happy and comfortable because it is relatively uncrowded during her visits.

### Added cue card and structure

Describe a peaceful place you like to visit. Explain **where it is**, **when you first went there**, **what you do there**, and **why it feels peaceful**.

**Structure:** **Location → First visit → Present routine → Activities → Feelings**.

### Added model answer

> A peaceful place I enjoy visiting is the wetland park in the city. It's a convenient distance from home, so it's easy to get there. I first visited it in [year], when I started working in the city. Since then, it has become one of my favourite places to relax. I usually go there on some evenings with my friend. We can rent a boat and enjoy the waterways, watch birds, and see traditional villages and different types of trees. When we visit, it usually isn't too crowded, which makes the experience especially relaxing. Overall, I like it because it gives me a break from the noise and pace of city life.

### Vocabulary and grammar

| Expression | How to use it |
| --- | --- |
| **rent a boat** | Pay to use it temporarily. **Buy a boat** means purchase it, so this is a meaning correction rather than a faulty sentence structure. |
| **a tourist attraction** | A place that visitors come to see. **A tourist destination** can refer to a larger area or city. |
| **It isn't too crowded.** | More natural than making a place “have people.” Also: **There aren't many people there.** |
| **watch birds / go birdwatching** | Observe birds. **Look at birds** is grammatical but less specific. |
| **different types of trees** | Clear everyday wording for tree variety. |
| **a convenient distance from home** | Distance pattern: **distance + from**. |
| **on some evenings** | A recurring routine; **last Sunday evening** identifies a finished occasion. |

**Tense changes are appropriate:** **I first visited it in [year]** is past simple; **I usually go there** is present simple; **It has become a favourite place** connects the past to the present.

## 3. A Favourite Photograph: A School Photo

### Reported details

- Stored on the learner's phone for **several years**.
- Taken at the **high school gate** by the person working at the entrance.
- Shows a group of friends.
- They were close friends at school and have remained friends.
- They meet occasionally, although changing commitments have made arranging meetings harder.

The summary calls it a graduation photo but does not establish the exact day it was taken. Do not automatically add “on graduation day.”

### Added cue card and structure

Describe a photograph you like. Explain **what it shows**, **when and where it was taken**, **who took it**, and **why it matters to you**.

**Structure:** **Identify photo → Past scene → Friendship now → Personal meaning**.

### Added model answer

> One of my favourite photos is a picture of my high school friends and me. I've kept it on my phone for several years. It was taken at our school gate by the person working at the entrance. There are four of us in the photo, and we were very close at school. We've remained friends since then and meet occasionally. Recently, our commitments have changed, so it has become harder to find a time when everyone is free. This photo matters to me because it reminds me of our school days and a friendship that has lasted for years. To sum up, it captures an important part of my life.

### Useful tense patterns

| Intended meaning | Sentence |
| --- | --- |
| A finished event | **The photo was taken at our school gate.** |
| A past relationship description | **We were best friends in high school.** |
| Friendship continuing now | **We have been close friends since high school.** |
| Keeping something until now | **I've kept this photo on my phone for several years.** |
| A recurring current event | **We meet occasionally.** |
| A recent change with a current result | **It has become harder to arrange a meeting.** |

**Since + starting point:** since high school, since [year]. **For + duration:** for several years, for several months.

**Gate versus door:** A **gate** controls access through a fence or boundary; a **door** usually leads into a building or room. **School gate** fits an outdoor school entrance, but **school door** is possible if an actual building door is meant. **Security guard** is appropriate if that was the person's job; **doorkeeper** is possible but less specific to a typical school security role.

**Speaking length:** Clarity matters more than padding. For an IELTS Part 2 simulation, still practise developing an answer towards the requested **one to two minutes**, using relevant detail rather than repetition.

## 4. Linking Words: Five Practical Groups

Choose a few expressions to use comfortably. A connector should express a real relationship between ideas, not decorate every sentence.

### A. Conclusions and final points

| Expression | Added example | Usage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **In conclusion** | **In conclusion,** spending time in nature helps me relax. | Formal conclusion to an argument or presentation. |
| **In summary** | **In summary,** the photo reminds me of school and lasting friendship. | Briefly restates main points. |
| **Overall** | **Overall,** the wetland park is a relaxing place to visit. | Gives a general assessment; useful in speech. |
| **As a final point** | **As a final point,** it is easy to reach from my home. | Adds a last point; does not necessarily summarise earlier points. |

### B. Emphasis and clarification

| Expression | Added example | Usage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Indeed** | The photo is meaningful. **Indeed,** I've kept it for several years. | Confirms or strengthens a point; fairly formal. |
| **In fact** | I still see my school friends. **In fact,** we usually meet when our schedules allow. | Adds a stronger detail or corrects an assumption. |
| **Particularly** | I **particularly** enjoy watching birds. | Highlights one activity or aspect. |
| **Obviously** | **Obviously,** renting a boat costs less than buying one. | Marks something as clear; can sound dismissive if used carelessly. |
| **Unquestionably** | This is **unquestionably** one of my most treasured photos. | Strong certainty; use sparingly rather than overstating debatable claims. |

### C. Examples and exact identification

| Expression | Added example | Usage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **For instance** | There is plenty to see. **For instance,** you can watch birds near the water. | Introduces an example, often a clause. |
| **Such as** | I enjoy peaceful activities **such as walking and birdwatching**. | Follow with example items; no extra **as** or **for example** is needed. |
| **Namely** | The photo shows four people, **namely my three friends and me**. | Identifies the exact people or things, not merely possible examples. |
| **As an illustration** | Nature can improve my mood. **As an illustration,** a quiet walk often helps me feel calmer. | Formal introduction to an example. |

**Electricity / illiteracy / illiterate:** These were reportedly discussed during the explanation, but the summary does not establish that the learner herself confused them.

- **Electricity**: electrical energy — **The boat has an electric motor powered by electricity.**
- **Illiteracy**: inability to read and write — **The programme aims to reduce adult illiteracy.**
- **Illiterate**: adjective — **The organisation supports adults who are illiterate.** Use sensitive, specific wording when describing people.

### D. Conditions, precautions, and hypothetical situations

| Expression | Added example | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Provided that** | We can rent a boat **provided that the service is open**. | Only if; a required condition. |
| **As long as** | I'll enjoy the visit **as long as it isn't too crowded**. | On condition that, in this example. It can also describe duration in other contexts. |
| **In case** | I'll take an umbrella **in case it rains**. | A precaution against a possibility. It is not simply another word for **if**. |
| **Even if** | I'll keep the photo **even if I change my phone**. | The result remains true whether or not the condition happens. |
| **Supposing that** | **Supposing that everyone is free next weekend,** where should we meet? | Introduces an imagined situation. **Suppose…** is often simpler in conversation. |

**Compare:** **I'll take an umbrella if it rains** makes taking it conditional on rain. **I'll take an umbrella in case it rains** means taking it beforehand as a precaution.

For likely future conditions, use **present simple**, not ordinary future **will**, after these conditional expressions: **provided that the weather is good**, not **provided that the weather will be good**.

### E. Time and sequence

| Expression | Added example | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **First** | **First,** we walk to the entrance. | Starting step. |
| **Next** | **Next,** we check whether boats are available. | Following step. |
| **Then** | **Then** we rent a boat. | What happens next. |
| **After that** | **After that,** we walk around the village. | A later stage. |
| **Meanwhile** | I take photos. **Meanwhile,** my friend watches the birds. | Activities happening at the same time. |
| **Immediately** | When it started raining, we **immediately** went indoors. | Without delay; not automatically a connector between stages. |

### Added connected example

> **First,** we walk through the gate. **Then** we head towards the water. We enjoy several activities, **such as** birdwatching and taking photos. I **particularly** like the quieter paths. We stay outside **as long as** the weather is comfortable, but I bring an umbrella **in case** it rains. **Overall,** it is a simple way to relax.

**Punctuation reminder:** Use **The park is nearby. In addition, it is peaceful.** Do not join independent sentences with just a comma and a connector such as **however** or **therefore**.

## 5. The learner's Personal Error Review

| Reported wording | Correction or clarification | Main learning point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “it don't have so many people” | **It doesn't get too crowded.** / **There aren't many people there.** | Singular **it** → **doesn't**. A crowd-based expression is more natural here. |
| “I didn't go there this Sunday.” | **Correct if that visit opportunity is in the past.** | If this Sunday is still upcoming, use **I'm not going there this Sunday**. **I haven't been there today** may fit an unfinished day. The phrase alone does not prove a tense error. |
| “buy a boat” | **rent a boat** | A vocabulary correction if temporary use is intended. Buying is grammatically possible but means purchasing. |
| “school door” | **school gate**, if referring to the outdoor entrance | Context-dependent vocabulary, not a universal grammar error. |
| “tourist see” / “top tourist place” | **a tourist attraction** / **a major tourist attraction** | **Tourist see** may be a transcription or vocabulary problem. **Tourist place** is understandable but less precise. |
| “The picture… stored there for several years.” | **The picture has been stored on my phone for several years.** | A complete passive predicate needs **has been stored**. The fragment may omit words in transcription. More conversational: **I've kept it on my phone…** |
| “We are best friends in high school.” | **We were best friends in high school.** / **We've been best friends since high school.** | Choose a past school relationship or a continuing relationship. The second adds continuity to now. |
| “Let me started to answer…” | **Let me start answering…** / **Let me answer…** | **Let + object + base verb**. **Let me start to answer** is also grammatical. |

**No confirmed personal pronunciation error can be established for electricity/illiteracy from the supplied summary alone.** A word explained by the teacher should not automatically be counted as an error made by the learner.

### Three patterns to practise first

1. **It doesn't + base verb:** It doesn't get crowded. It doesn't cost much.
2. **Have/has + past participle:** I've kept the photo. We've remained friends. The photo has been stored on my phone.
3. **Let me + base verb:** Let me think. Let me explain. Let me start again.

### C. Speaking practice

Record each answer for **one to two minutes**. Prepare keywords rather than full sentences.

1. **A peaceful place:** Include one first-visit detail, one present habit, and one example of an activity.

Try **one example marker**, **one time marker**, and **one concluding phrase** in each answer. Do not force every category into every response.

**Recording checklist:**
