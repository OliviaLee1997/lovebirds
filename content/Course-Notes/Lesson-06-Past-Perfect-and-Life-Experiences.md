## 1. Past Perfect: Look Back from a Past Moment

### A. Core Meaning and Structure

The **past perfect** describes something that happened **before a past reference point**. That reference point may be another event, a stated time, or a moment already established in the conversation.

**Structure: subject + had + past participle.** Use **had** with every subject.

| Form | Pattern | Edited example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Affirmative | subject + **had + past participle** | I **had repaired** my motorcycle before the ride. |
| Negative | subject + **had not / hadn't + past participle** | I **hadn't finished** the repair when my friend arrived. |
| Question | **Had + subject + past participle?** | **Had** you **checked** the tyres before you left? |
| Short answer | Yes, subject + had. / No, subject + hadn't. | Yes, I **had**. / No, I **hadn't**. |

**Contraction:** **I'd repaired** = **I had repaired**. Compare **I'd repair it if I could**, where **I'd** means **I would** and is followed by a base verb.

### B. Earlier Event and Later Reference Point

> **I had repaired my motorcycle before I went for a ride around the city.**

**Order:** repair completed → ride began → present moment.

The past perfect looks back at the repair **from the time of the ride**. The later event is often in the simple past, but this is not a requirement for every past perfect sentence:

> **By eight o'clock, I had finished the repair.**

Here, **eight o'clock** supplies the past reference point. A second action does not need to be stated.

### C. No Time Gap Is Required

The source summaries suggest using the past perfect only when actions are separated by a clear time interval or linked by cause and effect. **Neither a time gap nor causality is required.**

> **I had just sat down when the phone rang.**

The events can be only seconds apart. The key is **looking back from a past point**, not measuring the interval between actions.

Similarly, closely connected events can use the past perfect if that perspective matters:

> **After I had washed my face, I brushed my teeth.**

This is grammatical. For a straightforward sequence, **I washed my face and brushed my teeth** is usually simpler and more natural.

### D. When the Simple Past Is Enough

**Before** and **after** often make the order clear without the past perfect.

| Sentence | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| I **repaired** my motorcycle before I **went** for a ride. | Reports the sequence; **before** establishes the order. |
| I **had repaired** my motorcycle before I **went** for a ride. | Emphasises that the repair was already complete before the ride. |
| I **woke up**, **washed** my face, and **left** home. | Moves forward through a sequence of completed events. |

**Do not put every earlier action into the past perfect.** Use it when the earlier perspective helps your listener follow the story or understand what was already true.

### E. When the Choice Changes the Meaning

> **When I arrived, the lesson started.**  
> **When I arrived, the lesson had started.**

- **Started:** The lesson began at or around my arrival.
- **Had started:** The lesson began **before** I arrived.

**Added cause-and-background example:**

> I couldn't join the ride because **I had forgotten my helmet**.

The earlier event explains the later situation here, but that explanatory relationship is **one use**, not a universal requirement.

### F. Present Perfect vs. Past Perfect

| Tense | Reference point | Edited example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Present perfect**: have/has + past participle | Now | I **have repaired** my motorcycle, so it is ready now. |
| **Past perfect**: had + past participle | A past moment | I **had repaired** my motorcycle, so it was ready when my friend arrived. |
| **Simple past**: past form | A finished past event or period | I **repaired** my motorcycle yesterday. |

For the school example, a clear past perfect version is:

> **I had finished high school before I started university.**

**I finished high school before I started university** is also correct. **Had been to high school** is possible when discussing attendance, but **had finished high school** more precisely expresses completion.

### G. Related Review: Perfect Continuous Forms

**Reported warm-up:** the learner described five days of ongoing rain using **It has been raining**. More precisely, this is the **present perfect continuous**.

- **It has been raining for five days.** — Looks back over a period leading up to **now**; the rain may still be continuing or have only recently stopped.
- **It had been raining for five days when we went for our ride.** — Looks back over a period leading up to **a past ride**.

The second sentence is an **added contrast example**, not a confirmed account of the timing of the learner's ride.

### H. Past Participles to Practise

| Base form | Simple past | Past participle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| be | was / were | **been** |
| go | went | **gone** |
| do | did | **done** |
| see | saw | **seen** |
| take | took | **taken** |
| forget | forgot | **forgotten** |
| ride | rode | **ridden** |
| write | wrote | **written** |
| repair | repaired | **repaired** |
| finish | finished | **finished** |

After **had**, use the past participle: **had gone**, not **had went**.

## 3. Remember, Remind & Recall

The main difference is **who remembers and what causes the remembering**, not whether remembering is voluntary. A memory can come to you without deliberate effort; a place, object, or smell can remind you of something.

| Verb | Meaning | Common patterns | Edited example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **remember** | Have or bring something back into your memory | remember something; remember doing; remember to do; remember that… | I **remember riding** to school. |
| **remind** | Cause someone to remember or think of something | remind someone **of/about** something; remind someone **to do** something; remind someone **that**… | Please **remind me to check** the tyres. |
| **recall** | Remember or bring a memory back; often more formal | recall something; recall doing; recall that… | I **recall learning** to ride on my own. |

### A. Remember Doing vs. Remember To Do

- **I remember locking the door.** — I have a memory of the completed action.
- **I remembered to lock the door.** — I did not forget to perform the action.

### B. Remind Someone

> **This bicycle reminds me of my school days.**  
> **Please remind me about the meeting.**  
> **Please remind me to bring my helmet.**

Include the person in these patterns: **remind me**, **remind her**, **remind us**. Avoid “Please remind to bring my helmet.”

### C. Recall

> **I can't recall his name.**  
> **I recall meeting him at university.**

For remembering an experience, use **recall + -ing**, not **recall to do**.

## 4. Vocabulary & Pronunciation

| Word or phrase | Meaning or usage | Edited example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **era** | A period characterised by major developments or a particular feature | Computer skills are essential in **the digital era**. |
| **school days / school years** | Natural expressions for the period when you attended school | I rode a bicycle during **my school years**. |
| **wartime / the post-war era** | The period of a war / the period after a war | The country changed during **the post-war era**. |
| **balloon** | An inflatable decoration or toy | We bought candles and **balloons**. |
| **bullet** | A projectile fired from a gun | **Bullet** does not mean a party decoration. |
| **temper** | A tendency to become angry; one's anger in common expressions | I try to **control my temper**. |
| **temple** | A religious building; also the side of the head | We visited an old **temple**. |
| **mechanic** | A person who repairs and maintains machinery or vehicles | I'd like to become a **motorcycle mechanic**. |
| **portable** | Easy to carry | I prefer laptops because they are **portable**. |
| **rote memorisation** | Learning through repetition, often without understanding | We relied too much on **rote memorisation**. |
| **self-employed** | Working for yourself | I'd like to be **self-employed** and run a motorcycle shop. |
| **work–life balance** | A manageable balance between work and personal life | I want a better **work–life balance**. |

**Era clarification:** **School era** is understandable in some contexts, but **school days** is more natural for personal memories. **Era** normally describes a broader historical, social, or technological period. **War era** also needs context; **wartime** or the name of a particular war is often clearer.

**Career distinction:** A **mechanic** repairs and maintains vehicles or machines. An **engineer** often designs, develops, or analyses systems, although roles can overlap. A shop owner is typically **self-employed**; **freelancer** more often describes someone doing projects or assignments for different clients.

## 5. Everyday Speaking Topics

The following model answers are **edited study versions based on the reported discussion**, not quotations. Use only details that match your own experience when practising.

### A. Bicycles and the Weekend

**Reported discussion:** the learner went for a city ride over the weekend and mentioned five days of rain. She had a bicycle in middle school, taught herself to ride, and used it for school transport and exercise rather than shopping. The type of vehicle used for the weekend ride is not fully clear from the summary.

> I **had a bicycle when I was in middle school**. I **taught myself to ride** and mainly used it to get to school and exercise. I didn't usually use it for shopping.

> I **encourage people to cycle more** because it is good exercise and can help people manage their weight. Cycling is also **good for the environment** because riding a conventional bicycle produces no exhaust emissions.

**Added precision:** “Produces no pollution” is too broad if it includes manufacturing and the bicycle's full life cycle. **Produces no exhaust emissions while riding** expresses the intended environmental benefit more accurately.

### B. Birthdays

**Practice template:** **My birthday is on [date].** The date is a placeholder, not personal information.

Possible celebration ideas include inviting friends, buying a cake, or going to a café. Use **on + date**, **keen on + noun/-ing**, and **invite someone to a party**.

### C. Computers

> I use a computer every day **for programming, watching videos, and chatting**. I prefer laptops because they're portable, although desktop computers are often better suited to gaming. I think computer skills are very important today.

**Keep parallel forms:** **for programming, watching, and chatting**, or **to program, watch videos, and chat**.

### B. Edited Speaking Answer

> When I was in middle school, my school day **ran from [start time] to [end time]**, including meal breaks. There were **many students in my class**. **Added extension:** **I wish we had had…** expresses regret about a past situation. The first **had** is the auxiliary; the second is the past participle of **have**. This is a related use of the past perfect, not a reported classroom requirement.

> Regular speaking practice is valuable. It can help younger people learn Mandarin **while keeping their local language varieties alive**.

### C. Comparing Language Difficulty Carefully

Both languages require learning new pronunciation, vocabulary, and grammar.

**Edited opinion:** **Some Japanese characters look familiar to me, which may help me get started, but I would still need to learn their readings and usage.**

## 9. Review Exercises — Added Study Material

### A. Practise the Past Perfect Form

Use **had + past participle** in each blank. The requested form practises an earlier event viewed from a past point; it is not a claim that simple past is impossible in every sentence.

1. By the time my friend arrived, I ______ the motorcycle. *(repair)*
2. I couldn't join the ride because I ______ my helmet. *(forget)*
3. The lesson ______ before I entered the classroom. *(start)*
4. I ______ high school before I started university. *(finish)*
5. She ______ a motorcycle before that day. *(never / ride)*
6. ______ you ______ the tyres before you left? *(check)*
7. We ______ the work when the teacher came back. *(not finish)*
8. I ______ down when the phone rang. *(just / sit)*

**Answers:** 1. **had repaired**; 2. **had forgotten**; 3. **had started**; 4. **had finished**; 5. **had never ridden**; 6. **Had … checked**; 7. **hadn't finished**; 8. **had just sat**.

### B. Explain the Difference

1. When I arrived, my friend **left**. / When I arrived, my friend **had left**.
2. I **have repaired** the bike. / I **had repaired** the bike before the ride.
3. I **remember locking** the door. / I **remembered to lock** the door.
4. I **remember my school days**. / This photo **reminds me of my school days**.

**Suggested answers:**

1. In the first, the departure occurred at or around my arrival. In the second, my friend left earlier.
2. The first connects the completed repair to now. The second places it before a past ride.
3. The first describes a memory of locking the door. The second says I did not forget to do it.
4. In the first, I have the memory. In the second, the photo brings the memory to mind.

### C. Correct the Sentences

2. I had went home before it started raining.

**Suggested answers:**

### D. Check the Grammar Rules

Decide whether each statement is true or false.

1. The past perfect requires a long gap between two events.
2. The earlier event must always cause the later event.
3. “I had just finished when she called” can describe events seconds apart.
4. Before and after can make the order clear enough for the simple past.
5. A past reference point can be a time rather than a second stated action.
6. Only another person can remind you of something.

**Answers:** 1. **False**; 2. **False**; 3. **True**; 4. **True**; 5. **True**; 6. **False** — a photo, place, sound, or other cue can also remind you.

### E. Short Story: Background Before a Past Event

**Added fictional story, not a record of the learner's weekend:**

> Last Sunday, I **went** for a motorcycle ride with a friend. I **had checked** the tyres and **had filled** the tank before we left. When we **reached** a café, I **realised** that I **had forgotten** my wallet. My friend **paid** for my coffee, and I **sent** her the money later.

**Review:** The simple past moves the story forward. **Had checked** and **had filled** look back to preparations before departure. **Had forgotten** looks back from the moment of realisation. None of these choices depends on a minimum time interval.
