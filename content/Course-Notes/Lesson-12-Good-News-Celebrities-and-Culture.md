# Lesson 12 — Good News, Celebrities & Culture

**Privacy note:** Personal and third-party details have been removed or generalised. First-person stories and altered quotations are anonymised study examples, not a biographical record or verbatim transcript.

**Focus:** Sharing good news, explaining opinions about fame, and discussing cultural preservation.

## 1. Sharing Good News

### Anonymised Practice Scenario

- A friend receives an offer for a course they want to take.
- The speaker feels surprised and happy.
- The answer explains the news, the effort behind it, and the speaker's reaction.

This is a replacement study scenario; family relationships and private plans are omitted.

### Added speaking framework: News → Details → Reaction → Reason

1. **News:** What did you hear, and when?
2. **Details:** Who is involved, and what will happen?
3. **Reaction:** How did you feel?
4. **Reason:** Why does this news matter to you?

**Added model answer:**

> Last week, I received some good news. A friend told me that she had been accepted onto a course she wanted to take. I was surprised at first, but I felt happy for her. She had spent time preparing, so I was glad to hear that her effort had paid off. When she told me, I thought the opportunity would help her develop her interests. I'm looking forward to hearing how she gets on.

### Tense choices: past events and current beliefs

| Meaning | Useful sentence | Why it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Finished event | **I received some good news last week.** | A finished past time takes the past simple. |
| A plan reported in the past | **She told me she was planning to get married.** | Normal backshift after **told**. |
| A plan that is still current | **She told me she is planning to get married.** | Present tense can remain when the plan is still true. |
| A belief held at that time | **I thought she would lead a happy life.** | **Would** expresses the future viewed from the past. |
| A belief held now | **I think she will have a happy life.** | Present belief + future prediction. |

**Tense consistency means matching the time you intend**, rather than keeping every verb in the same tense.

**Relationship vocabulary:** **Boyfriend** can describe a male romantic partner, including in a serious relationship. **Partner** is broader and often sounds more mature; **life partner** emphasizes long-term commitment. **Fiancé** means a man who is engaged to be married; use it only if engagement is confirmed. **A good match** and **compatible** describe people who suit each other.

## 2. Celebrity Culture

### Reported views

- Scientific recognition depends on **interest, passion, hard work, and resilience**.
- The learner would prefer a calm, private life to fame and public criticism.
- Fame can bring intrusive photography, unwanted attention, and less privacy.
- Celebrities and their families deserve privacy, especially children.
- The discussion contrasted recognition for expertise with attention based on appearance, entertainment, and social media visibility.
- The learner expressed concern that young people might imitate celebrities' behavior.

### Added clarification: make opinions precise

Avoid treating all past or present celebrities as one group. Dancing can require considerable professional skill, and tattoos do not inherently indicate harmful behavior. A clearer argument distinguishes **artistic skill**, **popularity**, and **harmful conduct**, such as promoting smoking to children.

**Celebrity** usually suggests public fame in entertainment or media. For science, **a renowned scientist**, **a leading researcher**, or **a respected expert** may be more precise. Winning a prize and becoming a celebrity are different outcomes.

### Added speaking framework: Opinion → Reason → Example → Qualification

**Question: What are the disadvantages of being famous?**

> The biggest disadvantage is the loss of privacy. Famous people may be photographed even when they are spending time with their families. For example, their children might receive attention they never chose. Although celebrities have public responsibilities, their families still deserve personal space.

**Question: Are modern celebrities different from those in the past?**

> I think social media has changed how people become famous. Some people gain attention through short videos or their appearance, while others become known for professional achievements. However, talented entertainers and respected scientists still exist today. I would judge celebrities by their work and behavior rather than by the period they live in.

**Useful phrases:** **gain public recognition**, **be in the public eye**, **respect someone's privacy**, **set a good example**, **have a positive influence on**, **the downside of fame**, **balance public responsibilities with private life**.

## 3. Cultural Preservation & Identity

### Reported discussion

- Culture and language help create a **sense of belonging**.
- China has **fifty-six officially recognized ethnic groups**.
- The learner identified **harmony** as an important Chinese cultural value and linked it to Taoist philosophy.
- Cultural preservation should be selective: maintain valuable traditions while reconsidering harmful practices.
- Overseas communities, including Chinatowns, can help maintain traditions.
- Schools and governments can support cultural education and people who pass on traditions.
- International celebrations and historical contact can influence local customs.

### Added clarification: shared identity and diversity

**A common language can support communication without replacing other languages.** China has many languages and cultural traditions; Mandarin serves as a common language, but describing the country as having only one language would obscure that diversity.

**Harmony** appears in several Chinese philosophical traditions, including Confucianism and Taoism. It should not be attributed exclusively to one tradition.

**Eclectic** means drawing ideas or styles from several sources. For the specific idea of retaining beneficial traditions, **selective preservation** or **preserving valuable traditions while reconsidering harmful practices** is clearer.

### Added model answer: Should we preserve every tradition?

> I believe we should preserve traditions that give people a sense of belonging, such as local languages, festivals, and traditional crafts. However, preserving culture does not mean keeping every practice unchanged. We can maintain valuable traditions while reconsidering practices that harm people. Schools can help by teaching local history, and communities can give young people opportunities to learn from older generations.

### Morocco and cross-cultural influences

**Reported topics:** Spanish and French historical influence on Moroccan architecture and language; Tangier's proximity to Spain; handmade traditional clothing; daily prayer and Ramadan fasting.

**Added factual and wording clarifications:**

- The often quoted **14-kilometre distance** refers to the narrowest part of the **Strait of Gibraltar**, rather than a precise Tangier-to-Spain distance. Say: **Northern Morocco is separated from Spain by the Strait of Gibraltar.**
- Morocco experienced French and Spanish protectorates; Tangier also had a distinct international history. **Historical contact and colonial influence** is useful wording without reducing every cultural feature to one cause.
- Traditional clothing is worn in everyday life as well as on special occasions. Handmade garments **can be expensive**, but cost and use vary.
- **Five daily prayers** and **fasting during Ramadan** are among the five pillars of Islam. Religious accommodations depend on circumstances; “urgent cases allow flexibility” is too vague to explain the rules accurately.

**Useful vocabulary:** **cultural heritage**, **national identity**, **a sense of belonging**, **pass traditions on to the next generation**, **traditional craftsmanship**, **cultural exchange**, **historical influence**, **the national curriculum**.

## 4. Grammar & Expression Review

The left column contains wording reported in the supplied error analysis. Some fragments may reflect transcription problems; corrections follow the likely intended meaning.

| Reported wording | Corrected or more natural expression | Learning point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “I slept for all day and I just wake up.” | **I slept all day and just woke up.** | **All day**, without **for**; past form **woke**. **I've just woken up** is also natural for a recent event. |
| “received a good news” / “it's a good news” | **received some good news** / **it's good news** | **News** is uncountable and takes a singular verb. Also: **a piece of good news**. |
| “my friend told me that he'll be… He's planning…” | **My friend told me that she was planning…** | If referring to the friend, use **she**; check the referent before correcting a pronoun. |
| “take her friend to home” | **bring her friend home** | **Home** is an adverb here: **go home**, **come home**, **bring someone home**. |
| “She has been live with her friend…” | **She has been living with her friend…** | Present perfect continuous: **has/have been + -ing**. |
| “The convenient partner for her.” | **He seems like a good match for her.** | **Convenient** means practical or easy to arrange; **compatible** means well suited. |
| “so glad to hearing that” | **I was so glad to hear that.** | **Glad + to + base verb**. **I felt so glad to hear that** is also correct. |
| “I held over a lot of bad news.” | **I've heard a lot of bad news recently.** | **Hear news**; **hold over** does not mean hear. |
| “in this film?” | **in this field?** | **Field** = area of study; **film** = movie. This is vocabulary or pronunciation, not an article error. |
| “interested on Nobel Prize” | **interested in the Nobel Prize** | **Interested in + noun/-ing**. For ambition: **interested in winning a Nobel Prize**. |
| “won a Fields Prize” / “field model price” | **won the Fields Medal** | Learn the full award name: **Fields Medal**. Distinguish **prize** from **price**. |
| “their interest and their passion is…” | **Interest and passion are important for success in science.** | Two subjects joined by **and** normally take **are**. Simplify the sentence before expanding it. |
| Unclear explanation of “downside” | **A downside is a disadvantage or negative aspect.** | Example: **One downside of fame is the loss of privacy.** |
| “You have no enough personal life.” | **You don't have enough privacy.** | **Not enough + noun**; **no enough** is incorrect. **Privacy** differs from **personal life**. |
| “in a very early age” | **at a very early age** | Fixed phrase: **at an early age**. |
| “good culture and bad culture” | **valuable traditions and harmful practices** | Name specific aspects rather than judging an entire culture as good or bad. |
| “Forty six… fifty six ethnic groups” | **fifty-six ethnic groups** | **Fifty-six** is the intended number; a corrected false start is not a grammar error. |
| “my country is divide was divided…” | **My country was divided into several smaller states.** | Passive: **was/were + past participle**. Historical accuracy depends on which period is meant. |
| “a concept from a Tao Religion” | **a concept associated with Taoist philosophy** | **Taoism**, **Taoist philosophy**, or **the Taoist tradition**. |

### Three patterns to practise first

1. **Uncountable nouns:** some good **news**, useful **advice**, enough **privacy**, important **information**. Do not use **a/an** directly with these nouns.
2. **Verb forms:** **has been living**, **glad to hear**, **just woke up**. Practise whole phrases rather than isolated rules.
3. **Natural combinations:** **interested in**, **at an early age**, **bring someone home**, **a good match**, **the downside of fame**.

### B. Choose the form that matches the meaning

1. Last week, I **received / have received** good news.
2. I still believe they **will / would** be happy together.
3. At that moment, I thought they **will / would** be happy together.
4. This news **is / are** exciting.
5. She has been **live / living** there since [month].
6. We should give young people **advice / an advice**.

### C. Speaking drills

Prepare four keywords, then speak for **60–90 seconds**.

- **Good news:** What happened? When did you hear it? How did you feel? Why did it matter?
- **Fame:** Would you like to be famous? Give a reason, an example, and one qualification.
- **Culture:** Choose one tradition worth preserving. Explain its value and suggest a practical way to pass it on.

**Recording checklist:**

- [ ] I distinguish past events from current beliefs.
- [ ] I say **some good news**, without **a**.
- [ ] I use **has been + -ing** and **glad to + base verb**.
- [ ] I give an example rather than repeating my opinion.
- [ ] I qualify broad claims with **some**, **can**, or **in my experience**.
