## 2. The Main Pronunciation Principle

**English spelling does not map one letter to one sound.** Some letters have no separate sound; some letter groups represent one sound; and some words have more than one accepted pronunciation.

- **Silent letter:** The **k** in **knife** does not produce /k/.
- **Letter group representing one sound:** **tch** in **watch** represents /tʃ/.
- **Pronunciation variant:** **Often** is pronounced both with and without /t/.

Use patterns to make a useful first guess, then check unfamiliar words in a dictionary with audio. The summary's estimate that “about 20%” are exceptions is **not supported by a source** and should not be memorised as a statistic.

**Notation:** Letters are written normally; sounds appear between slashes, such as **/ŋ/**. IPA examples below use common dictionary pronunciations; vowels and some consonants vary by accent.

## 3. Silent-Letter Patterns — Corrected Reference

| Letter | Useful pattern and examples | Limits and important contrasts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **G** | No /ɡ/ in **gnome, sign, design, campaign, foreigner**. Initial **gn-** is often /n/ in familiar words such as **gnome**. | **G before N is not always silent.** Compare **sign** /saɪn/ with **signal** /ˈsɪɡnəl/. **Magnet** also contains /ɡn/. |
| **U** | In **guitar, guess, guide**, the written **u** has no separate vowel or /w/ sound. **Biscuit** is /ˈbɪskɪt/; **tongue** is /tʌŋ/. | **GU/QU is not a general silent-U rule.** Compare **guitar** with **penguin**, which has /ɡw/. **Quick** and **queen** have /kw/; **unique** ends in /k/. Learn the whole spelling pattern. |
| **C** | No separate /k/ in **scene** /siːn/ and **muscle** /ˈmʌsəl/. | **SC does not always mean /s/.** **Scan** and **school** contain /sk/. **Conscious** has /ʃ/: UK /ˈkɒnʃəs/, US /ˈkɑːnʃəs/. |
| **L** | No /l/ in common pronunciations of **talk, walk, calm, half, would, could, should**. | These are useful word families, not universal rules for every **alk, alm, alf, ould** sequence. **Always** has /l/, and **shoulder** has /l/ even though **should** does not. **Almond** has variants with and without /l/. |
| **P** | Initial **p** is silent in words such as **psychology, pneumonia, pterodactyl**. It is also silent in **raspberry** and **cupboard**. | The initial pattern applies to these familiar Greek-derived words, not every occurrence of **ps, pn, pt**. **P before B** is not a general deletion rule: **upbeat** retains /p/ in its dictionary pronunciation. |
| **B** | Final **-mb** often has no /b/: **bomb, climb, comb, thumb, crumb, dumb, succumb**. **Debt, doubt, subtle** also have no /b/. | **MB/BT anywhere** does not guarantee silence. **Member** and **timber** have /b/; **obtain** also has /b/. Learn **debt/doubt/subtle** as specific words and related forms. |
| **T** | No /t/ in common pronunciations of **listen, castle, whistle, wrestle**. | **Often** has accepted forms with and without /t/. **Left, gift, after** retain /t/ in their dictionary pronunciations, so **ft** is not a general silent-T pattern. **Watch** and **butcher** both contain /tʃ/. |
| **H** | No /h/ in **honest, hour, honour/honor**. **What** and **where** commonly begin with /w/. | Some accents preserve a **wh** sound, often written /ʍ/. In **who**, the sound is /h/ and there is no /w/. **WH** is not simply “always remove H.” |
| **K** | Initial **kn-** normally has no /k/ in familiar words such as **know, knife, knee, knock**. | This is a pattern for the start of these words, not every K–N sequence. **Acknowledge**, for example, retains /k/. |
| **W** | Initial **wr-** has no /w/ in **write, wrong, wrist, wrap, wrestle**. | **Wrestle** has neither /w/ nor /t/: /ˈresəl/. Do not pronounce it by sounding out each written consonant. |

### Useful Word-Level Checks

- **Raspberry:** UK /ˈrɑːzbəri/, US /ˈræzberi/ — no /p/; the sound before /b/ is /z/.
- **Cupboard:** UK /ˈkʌbəd/, US /ˈkʌbɚd/ — no /p/. It does not sound like carefully saying **cup** and **board** separately.
- **Succumb:** /səˈkʌm/ — stress the second syllable and omit final /b/.
- **Often:** UK /ˈɒfən/ or /ˈɒftən/; US /ˈɑːfən/ or /ˈɑːftən/. Both the /t/ and no-/t/ versions are accepted.
- **Almond:** Cambridge gives UK /ˈɑːmənd/ and US /ˈɑːlmənd/, with further variants in its American entry. Do not treat one vowel or the absence of /l/ as universal.

## 4. Three Important Corrections to the Supplied Rules

### A. The -ing Ending Normally Has /ŋ/, Not /n/

The usual dictionary pronunciation of **-ing** ends in **/ɪŋ/**. **/ŋ/** is a nasal consonant made with the back of the tongue raised towards the soft palate. It differs from **/n/**, which uses the front of the tongue against the ridge behind the upper teeth.

| Word | Common pronunciation | What to notice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **sin** | /sɪn/ | Ends in /n/. |
| **sing** | /sɪŋ/ | Ends in /ŋ/; no extra /ɡ/. |
| **singing** | /ˈsɪŋɪŋ/ | Both **ng** groups are /ŋ/ in this pronunciation. |
| **writing** | /ˈraɪtɪŋ/ | Final /ŋ/; initial **w** is silent. |
| **singer** | UK /ˈsɪŋə/, US /ˈsɪŋɚ/ | No separate /ɡ/ in these dictionary forms. |
| **finger** | UK /ˈfɪŋɡə/, US /ˈfɪŋɡɚ/ | Contains /ŋɡ/, so **ng** does not always exclude /ɡ/. |

Some informal and regional pronunciations use **-in' /ɪn/**. That is a genuine variation, but it is not the general dictionary pronunciation of **-ing**. For this drill, practise **/ɪŋ/** without adding a hard /ɡ/.

### B. U Does Not Follow a Universal “Consonant = /n/” Rule

The symbol **/n/** is a consonant sound, not the vowel in **cup** or **sun**. The intended symbol in the summary was probably **/ʌ/**, but this cannot be verified without the original material.

- **Cup /kʌp/, sun /sʌn/, butter** commonly use /ʌ/ in the reference accents considered here.
- **Music, huge, cute** commonly contain /juː/.
- **Put** and **full** contain /ʊ/ in common dictionary pronunciations.
- **Rule, rude, flute** have /uː/ without /j/ in common dictionary pronunciations.

Spelling patterns such as a final silent **e** can be helpful, but the following letters alone do not determine every U pronunciation. These vowel patterns are a separate issue from whether a written **u** is silent.

### C. TCH Represents /tʃ/ in Both Watch and Butcher

**/tʃ/** is one affricate consonant: a brief closure followed by a “sh”-like release.

- **Watch:** UK /wɒtʃ/, US /wɑːtʃ/.
- **Butcher:** UK /ˈbʊtʃə/, US /ˈbʊtʃɚ/.

Do not reduce these words to a simple /ʃ/ sound, and do not add an extra /t/ before /tʃ/. The useful rule is **tch → /tʃ/**; **butcher** is not an exception to that pattern.

## 5. Reported Language Issues — Corrections and Uncertainty

| Reported wording and time | Correction or interpretation | Learning point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “I received a my a picture” | **I received a picture. / I received my picture.** Choose according to the intended meaning. | An article and possessive do not normally both determine the same noun this way. However, the fragment may record a restart rather than a stable grammar error. |
| “Give mea call”; repeated “Give me a…” | **Give me a call** is already correct. | **Me a** can run together in connected speech. The spelling **mea** may be the transcriber's choice; unfinished repetitions may be pronunciation practice. Neither proves incorrect spoken grammar. |
| “The next event will in the [month]” | **My next event will be in [month].** | Use **will + be** here and **in [month]**, without **the** before the ordinary month name. |
| “Its gonna be…” | In standard writing: **It's going to be…** | **It's/its** is a spelling distinction that cannot be diagnosed from sound alone. **Gonna** is a common informal spoken form, not automatically an error. |
| “three combined” | For three letter groupings: **three combinations**. The tutor reportedly supplied this noun. | **Combination** is the relevant countable noun. **Combined** can be grammatical in other contexts, so the correction depends on the intended phrase. |
| “two customers” | In the discussion of **mb**, the intended term is **two consonants**. | This may be word choice, pronunciation, or transcription confusion. Audio would be needed to determine which. |
| Unclear fragment around “work building” | No reliable correction can be assigned. | Do not invent a complete sentence from an unintelligible transcript fragment. |
| “Suck come” while practising **succumb** | Practise **succumb /səˈkʌm/**. | This appears to concern word pronunciation or syllable practice, not an attempted complete sentence. It should not be labelled a sentence-grammar error. |
| “I had a work the whole day” | **I worked all day. / I worked the whole day. / I had work all day.** | **Work** is uncountable when referring to employment or activity. **The whole day** is grammatical. Use **a job** or **a piece of work** when a countable expression is needed. |

### Vocabulary to Keep Separate

- **A vowel:** a vowel letter or sound, depending on context.
- **A consonant:** a consonant letter or sound. The letters **m** and **b** are consonant letters.
- **A customer:** a person who buys goods or services.
- **A combination:** things joined together, such as a group of letters.

The spelling **mb** contains **two consonant letters**, but **climb** ends in only the sound **/m/**. Keep the number of letters separate from the number of sounds.

## 6. Read-Aloud Practice — Added Study Material

Read each sentence slowly once, then at a comfortable conversational speed. Focus on the highlighted words without overpronouncing the rest of the sentence.

1. I **know** how to use this **knife**.
2. Please **write** your name under the **sign**.
3. We **climbed** up to the old **castle**.
4. The **psychology** book is in the **cupboard**.
5. I was **singing** while my friend was **writing**.
6. Please **listen** while I explain the **design**.
7. I **often** buy **almonds** and **raspberries**.
8. The **butcher** looked at his **watch**.

For sentence 7, choose accepted dictionary variants rather than trying to remove every consonant discussed in the lesson.

**Contrast drill:** **sin → sing; singer → finger; sign → signal; should → shoulder**. Identify the sound difference before repeating the pair.

## 7. Review Exercises — Added Study Material

### A. Identify the Sound or Silent Letter

1. Which letter has no sound in **knife**?
2. Which two letters have no separate sound in **wrestle**?
3. Does **singing** normally end in /n/, /ŋ/, or /ŋɡ/ in its dictionary form?
4. What sound does **tch** represent in **watch**?
5. Does **conscious** contain /s/ or /ʃ/ in the middle?
6. Is pronouncing /t/ in **often** necessarily wrong?
7. Does **quick** contain /kw/ or just /k/ at the beginning?
8. Which word has /ɡ/: **sign** or **signal**?

### B. Correct or Clarify the Sentences

1. My next holiday will in the [month].
2. I had a work the whole day.
3. There are two customers in the spelling **mb**. *(You mean letter types.)*
4. The transcript writes **mea**, so the speaker must have said **me a** incorrectly.

### Suggested Answers

**A:** 1. **K**. 2. **W and T**. 3. **/ŋ/**. 4. **/tʃ/**. 5. **/ʃ/**. 6. **No; both forms are accepted.** 7. **/kw/**. 8. **Signal**.

**B:**

1. **My next holiday will be in [month].**
2. **I worked all day / the whole day.** Alternatively: **I had work all day**, if describing the day's work obligations.
3. **There are two consonant letters in the spelling mb.**
4. **The transcript alone does not establish a pronunciation error.** Connected speech may explain the spelling.
