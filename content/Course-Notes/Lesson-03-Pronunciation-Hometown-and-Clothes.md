## 1. Pronunciation: Common Sound–Spelling Patterns

**Learn spelling patterns as useful clues, not rules without exceptions.** A pair of vowel letters is not necessarily a diphthong: **/aʊ/** and **/ɔɪ/** glide between vowel positions, whereas **/ɔː/** and **/uː/** are traditionally classified as long monophthongs in British teaching materials.

### A. Vowel Patterns

| Spelling | Common sound | Examples | Clarification / added exceptions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **au / aw** | **/ɔː/** | pause, sauce, Paul; saw, hawk, claw | Common in the British reference accent; **aunt /ɑːnt/** is an exception. |
| **ou / ow** | **/aʊ/** | loud, about, couch; cow, clown, crown | Other sounds occur: **soup /suːp/**, **young /jʌŋ/**, **snow /snəʊ/**. |
| **oi / oy** | **/ɔɪ/** | oil, coin, boil, point; boy, toy | A reliable pattern in these everyday examples. |
| **oo** | **/uː/** | boot, moon, soon, cool | Compare **book /bʊk/** and **blood /blʌd/**. |
| **ew** | **/uː/** or **/juː/** | chew /tʃuː/, drew /druː/ | Added British example: **few /fjuː/** includes /j/. |
| **ue** | **/uː/** or **/juː/** | Added examples: blue /bluː/, cue /kjuː/ | The sound depends on the word and accent. |
| **ui** | Often **/uː/** in the examples here | Added examples: fruit /fruːt/, suit /suːt/ | Compare **build /bɪld/**; **suit /sjuːt/** also occurs in British English. |

**Correction to the supplied summary:** **Two /tuː/** and **cool /kuːl/** contain the sound /uː/, but they do not illustrate the spellings **ew, ue, or ui**. **Cool** illustrates **oo**; the **w** in **two** is silent.

## 2. Homophones: Same Sound, Different Meaning

**Homophones** have the same pronunciation in a particular accent but different meanings and often different spellings. Use the **sentence context** to choose the correct word.

| Words | British reference pronunciation | Meaning distinction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **male / mail** | /meɪl/ | Male: a sex category; mail: letters or post. |
| **sea / see** | /siː/ | Sea: a large body of salt water; see: perceive with your eyes. |
| **pair / pear** | /peə/ | Pair: two things together; pear: a fruit. |
| **right / write** | /raɪt/ | Right: correct or a direction; write: produce written words. |
| **here / hear** | /hɪə/ | Here: in this place; hear: perceive sound. |
| **by / buy / bye** | /baɪ/ | By: a preposition; buy: purchase; bye: goodbye. |
| **flower / flour** | /ˈflaʊə/ | Flower: part of a plant; flour: an ingredient for baking. |
| **eight / ate** | /eɪt/ for both when ate uses this pronunciation | Eight: the number 8; ate: the past of eat. **Ate /et/** is also used in British English. |
| **meat / meet** | /miːt/ | Meat: food from animals; meet: encounter someone. |
| **son / sun** | /sʌn/ | Son: a male child; sun: the star that gives Earth light. |
| **one / won** | /wʌn/ | One: the number 1; won: the past of win. |
| **weight / wait** | /weɪt/ | Weight: how heavy something is; wait: stay until something happens. |

**Accent correction: aunt / ant.** These are homophones in accents that pronounce both as **/ænt/**. In the British reference pronunciation used here, **aunt /ɑːnt/** and **ant /ænt/** are different. British accents vary, so neither pattern represents every British speaker.

**Added model sentence:** I want to **buy** a **pair** of shoes **by** the end of the week.

## 6. Grammar: Irregular Past-Tense Verbs

The lesson reviewed the following **simple past** forms. Example sentences are added practice.

| Base form | Simple past | Added example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| eat | **ate** | I **ate** fish for dinner yesterday. |
| drink | **drank** | I **drank** some water after class. |
| buy | **bought** | I **bought** car insurance recently. |
| fly | **flew** | We **flew** to another city last year. |
| spend | **spent** | I **spent** an hour shopping online. |
| drive | **drove** | I **drove** to work yesterday. |
| cut | **cut** | I **cut** the label off the shirt. |
| catch | **caught** | I **caught** the bus this morning. |
| sing | **sang** | She **sang** at the wedding. |
| break | **broke** | I **broke** a glass yesterday. |

### Questions & Negatives — Added Explanation

Use **did / didn't + base verb**, even when the affirmative uses an irregular past form.

> I **bought** a shirt yesterday.  
> **Did** you **buy** it online?  
> I **didn't buy** it in a shop.

Use **spend + time/money + on + noun** or **spend + time + -ing**:

> I **spent £30 on a shirt**.  
> I **spent an hour comparing prices**.

## 7. Grammar Corrections from the Supplied Transcript Analysis

> **Source and scope:** The recorded expressions and timestamps below come from the additional transcript analysis supplied by the learner. They have not been checked against the original recording. Corrections and explanations are edited study material; uncertain wording is identified explicitly. Examples from verb practice do not establish that the learner actually made the trips or performed the actions described.

### A. Past Tense & Purpose

| Recorded expression | Corrected expression | Explanation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “I just go outside for eat.” | I just **went outside to eat**. | For a completed past outing, use **go → went**. Express purpose with **to + base verb**, not “for eat.” British English also commonly uses **I've just gone out to eat** for a very recent action when the speaker is still out; choose the form that fits the situation. |
| “I catched…” | I **caught**… | **Catch → caught** is irregular; do not add **-ed**. The supplied expression is a fragment. |
| “I fly to London.” (context: last month) | I **flew** to London **last month**. | A finished past time such as **last month** calls for the simple past: **fly → flew**. |
| “I drink a coconut water.” (context: yesterday) | I **drank coconut water yesterday**. | Use **drink → drank** for the past action. **Coconut water** is normally uncountable; use no article, **some**, or a container phrase. |
| “I just say at home.” | I just **stayed at home**. | **Stay** is the likely intended verb in the supplied weekend context, but this wording needs confirmation from the recording. For a completed past event, use **stay → stayed**. **Say** means to express something in words. |

**Time-marker check:** **Yesterday** and **last month** identify finished past time. **Just** alone does not determine the tense; both the simple past and present perfect can be appropriate depending on context and usage.

### B. Subject–Verb Agreement & Countable Nouns

| Recorded expression | Corrected expression | Explanation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “…only big city have… job opportunity.” | Only **big cities have job opportunities**. / Only **a big city has job opportunities**. | Use **plural subject + have** or **singular subject + has**. A singular countable noun needs a determiner: **a big city**, **a job opportunity**. |

**Meaning clarification:** These corrections preserve the supplied claim, but **only** makes it absolute. To express the earlier lesson summary's comparison more precisely, say: **Big cities offer more job opportunities in my field.**

### C. Prepositions & Word Choice

| Recorded expression | Recommended expression | Explanation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “My hometown is famous as white wine.” | My hometown is **famous for baijiu**, a Chinese distilled spirit. | Use **famous for + product, feature, or achievement**. Use **famous as + role or identity**, as in **She is famous as a singer**. The earlier lesson notes identify the local product as **baijiu**, which is not white wine. |

**Added comparison:** My hometown is **famous for baijiu production**. / The city is **famous as a centre of baijiu production**.

### D. Articles, Sentence Structure & Transcription

| Recorded expression | Recommended expression | Explanation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “I drank a coconut water.” | I drank **some coconut water**. / I drank **a glass of coconut water**. | This is an alternate wording supplied for the same timestamp as the **drink** example above, not a confirmed separate error. **Water** is normally uncountable. In ordering contexts, **a coconut water** can mean one serving, but **some coconut water** is clearer for this exercise. |
| “It's difficult to pronounce it. my hometown is uh uh[hometown]。” | My hometown is **[hometown]**. **The name is difficult to pronounce**. | This revision makes the reference explicit. Capitalisation and punctuation are transcription issues, not spoken grammar errors; **uh** is a hesitation. **It's difficult to pronounce it** can be grammatical when the first **it** is a dummy subject and the second refers to the name. If the first **it** already refers to the name, use **It's difficult to pronounce**. |

### E. Focused Review — Added Study Material

- **Irregular past forms:** **go → went**, **catch → caught**, **fly → flew**, **drink → drank**, **break → broke**.

- **Time and tense:** Check **yesterday**, **last month**, and the wider context before choosing a tense.
- **Useful patterns:** **go out to eat**, **stay at home**, **be famous for**, and **a glass of coconut water**.
- **Agreement:** Practise **a city has** alongside **cities have**.
