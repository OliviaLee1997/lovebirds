## 1. Present Continuous: Now and Future Arrangements

### A. Form

| Form | Pattern | Edited example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Affirmative | **subject + am/is/are + -ing** | AI **is making** some tasks easier. |
| Negative | **subject + am/is/are + not + -ing** | I **am not working** right now. |
| Question | **Am/Is/Are + subject + -ing?** | **Are** you **working** right now? |

**Do not omit am/is/are:** “AI making life better” is not a complete standard sentence. Say **AI is making life better**.

### B. Actions Happening Now or Around Now

> At the moment, **we are talking**.  
> **I am working on** a project this week.

The activity may be happening **at this moment** or during a **temporary period around now**. The second sentence does not require the speaker to be working on the project at the exact moment of speaking.

### C. Future Arrangements

> **I am flying to London next week.**  
> **I am meeting my friend tomorrow evening.**  
> **I am going camping with my friend this weekend.**

The present continuous can describe a **future arrangement**, especially when practical details have been settled. A booked flight is a useful example, but buying a ticket is not a grammatical requirement.

**The future meaning must be clear from context; a future time expression does not have to appear in every sentence.**

> A: What are you doing tomorrow evening?  
> B: **I'm having dinner with a friend.**

The question already establishes the future time. Without that context, “I'm having dinner” would normally describe an action happening now.

### D. Present Continuous, Going To, Will & Present Simple

The supplied analysis presents “planned = present continuous; spontaneous = will” as an absolute rule. It is a useful starting contrast, but **planned events do not always require the present continuous, and will is not limited to spontaneous decisions**.

| Intended meaning | Common form | Edited example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A settled personal arrangement | **Present continuous** | I **am meeting** my teacher at eight tomorrow. |
| An intention already formed | **be going to + base verb** | I **am going to read** more short stories. |
| A decision made as you speak | **will + base verb** | That T-shirt looks nice. I **will buy** it. |
| A prediction or belief about the future | **will + base verb** | AI **will change** many jobs. |
| A prediction based on present evidence | **be going to + base verb** | Look at those dark clouds. It **is going to rain**. |
| A promise or offer | **will + base verb** | I **will send** you the handouts. |
| A timetable or scheduled event | **Present simple** | The match **starts** at eight tomorrow. |

These forms overlap. Choose the form that best expresses **how you are presenting the future event**.

**The football example:**

- **Barcelona is playing against Madrid tomorrow.** — Presents the match as an arrangement.
- **Barcelona plays against Madrid tomorrow.** — Presents it as a scheduled fixture.
- **Barcelona will play against Madrid tomorrow.** — Also grammatical, for example as a neutral announcement about the future.

The last sentence should **not** be memorised as a grammar error. Here, “Madrid” is retained from the supplied wording; the precise team is not established.

## 2. Past Continuous & Simple Past: Tell a Clear Story

### A. Forms

- **Past continuous:** subject + **was/were + -ing**.
- **Simple past:** subject + **past form**.

Use **was** with I/he/she/it and **were** with you/we/they.

| Relationship | Typical pattern | Edited example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| An event occurs during an action in progress | Past continuous + simple past | I **was reading** when someone **called** me. |
| Two activities are in progress at the same time | Past continuous + past continuous | I **was showing** a video while they **were listening**. |
| Completed events occur in sequence | Simple past + simple past | I **saw** a stone and **picked** it up. |

### B. Background and Main Event

> **I was reading a book when my phone rang.**

**Was reading** supplies the background activity already in progress. **Rang** introduces an event during that activity. The call may interrupt the reading, but **the grammar alone does not prove that the reading stopped**.

### C. Two Simultaneous Activities

> **While I was explaining the problem, my colleague was taking notes.**

Both activities are presented as ongoing during the same period. **While** makes their overlap clear; **and** can also join the clauses when the context establishes simultaneity.

### D. A Sequence of Completed Events

> **I saw a stone, picked it up, and put it beside the path.**

The simple past moves the story through completed events.

**Added clarification: “long action / short action” is a classroom shortcut, not a literal duration rule.** A long activity can use the simple past: **I worked for eight hours yesterday.** The key is whether you present an event as **in progress at a past moment** or as **a completed whole**. Neither **when** nor **while** mechanically determines a tense.

## 3. Speaking Topics & Edited Model Answers

The following answers are study versions based on the reported discussion. They are **not quotations**, and the opinions are the learner's reported views rather than independently established facts.

### A. Work Skills and AI

**Anonymised practice context:** Describing technical project work, explaining how a tool helps, and giving opinions about technology.

**Question: What skills have helped you most at work?**

> **Communication skills have helped me the most at work.** I work in technical project work, mainly improving a project, and **I've been working in this field for a year**. Clear communication helps me explain problems and understand what other people need.

**Question: How is AI changing work?**

> AI **makes my work easier**. It can transcribe meetings, summarise discussions, and translate emails. However, I think it **may replace some front-end and back-end engineering roles** in the future. It creates opportunities, but it also raises concerns about job losses.

**Question: Can AI replace teachers?**

> In my opinion, AI can help with routine tasks such as marking homework, but it **cannot fully replace teachers**. Human teachers provide emotional support and understand students' individual needs. I see AI as a useful tool in education.

**Useful distinction:** **May/might replace** expresses a possibility; **will replace** presents a stronger prediction. Choose the strength that matches your actual view.

### B. Travel and Lifestyle Preferences

**Reported discussion:** the learner preferred city breaks to beach holidays because of concerns about dirty sand and litter. She preferred living in her home country, citing safety, rapid development, and opportunities. She recalled a mountain trip in another region in temperatures reportedly below −40°C.

> I **prefer city breaks to beach holidays** because I don't like dirty sand or litter on the beach.

> I **would rather stay in my home country** because I feel safe here and see plenty of opportunities as the country develops.

> I once went mountain climbing in another region. The temperature was **below minus forty degrees Celsius**, and **I was freezing**. The experience made me think about how differently people live.

The temperature is retained as a reported experience, not a verified weather observation.

### C. Personal Strengths and City Life

**Reported discussion:** the learner described herself as enthusiastic, friendly, and easy-going. She had lived in a city for some time and felt that local government staff worked more efficiently than those in her hometown. She suggested speed limits for electric bicycles.

> One of my strengths is that **I'm friendly and easy-going**. I enjoy meeting people, which helps me **make friends**.

> **I've lived here for some time.** In my experience, the local government staff are efficient and hard-working.

> I think the city should **set and enforce speed limits for electric bikes** because some riders travel too fast and put other road users at risk.

**A trait is not exactly a skill:** **Being friendly** is a personal quality; **building relationships** is a related skill.

### D. Restaurants and Food

> When I choose a restaurant, I care most about **a quiet atmosphere and good food**. I don't like crowded places or spicy dishes. I **prefer sweet foods**.

**Useful alternative:** **I have a sweet tooth** means that you particularly enjoy sweet foods.

### E. Weekend Arrangements

**Reported plan:** the learner planned to take her bird outside and go camping by the river with her friend. The summary says **bird**, while the supplied quotation says **birds**; the number is not confirmed.

**Edited arrangement example, using the summary's singular wording:**

> **I'm taking my bird outside this weekend, and I'm going camping by the river with my friend.**

If this is an intention without settled arrangements, **I'm going to take my bird outside** is also natural.

## 4. Personal Error Log

### A. Tense and Verb Structure

| Reported expression | Edited revision | Learning point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “I will have a dinner.” | **I'm having dinner tonight.** / **I'm going to have dinner.** | For a routine meal, normally omit **a**. An arrangement or intention fits these revisions, but **I'll have dinner** can also be grammatical. **A dinner** is possible for a special event. |
| “I'll take my birds outside... I'll go camping...” | **I'm taking my birds outside… I'm going camping…** | Natural for settled weekend arrangements; **will** is not inherently incorrect. Retains the quotation's plural **birds**. |
| “Barcelona will play against Madrid tomorrow.” | **Barcelona is playing against Madrid tomorrow.** | A change of emphasis to an arrangement, not a mandatory grammar correction. |
| “AI making life better.” | **AI is making life better.** | Include **is** in the present continuous. |
| “It growing rapidly.” | **It is growing rapidly.** | **Is** is the missing auxiliary in **is growing**. |
| “I have been lived there for five years.” | **I have lived there for five years.** / **I have been living there for five years.** | Use **have + past participle** or **have been + -ing**. **Live**, meaning reside, does not take this passive form. |
| “It make me makes me work my work easily.” | **It makes my work easier.** / **It makes me work more easily.** | Use one complete **make** pattern; **it** takes **makes**. |

### B. Nouns, Agreement and Quantity

| Reported expression | Edited revision | Learning point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “I think itsa communication skill.” | **Communication skills have helped me most at work.** | **Communication skills** is the usual general phrase. **A communication skill** can refer to one specific skill; the transcription alone does not establish an article error. |
| “Some job is replaced by AI.” | **Some jobs are being replaced by AI.** / **Some jobs may be replaced by AI in the future.** | Use plural **jobs** for the intended general meaning. Choose current change or future possibility according to your meaning. |
| “We have many chance.” | **We have many chances.** / **There are many opportunities here.** | **Many + plural countable noun**. **Opportunities** is natural for life and career possibilities. |
| “Below forty degree.” | **Below minus forty degrees Celsius.** | Use plural **degrees** and preserve the negative temperature. **Below forty** only means less than +40, not less than −40. |
| “A sweet food.” | **I like sweet food.** / **I like sweet foods.** | For a general preference, omit **a**. **Foods** refers to types; **a sweet food** can be grammatical when identifying one kind. |

**Passive forms for AI discussion — added reference:**

- **Some jobs are replaced by AI.** — A general statement about replacement.
- **Some jobs are being replaced by AI.** — A process happening now.
- **Some jobs may be replaced by AI.** — A possibility, including a future possibility in context.

### C. Vocabulary, Adjectives and Natural Phrasing

| Reported expression | Edited revision | Learning point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “I was frozen.” | **I was freezing.** | **Freezing** is the usual conversational choice for feeling very cold. **Frozen** can describe a frozen state, immobility, or figuratively extreme cold; it is not always wrong. |
| “They are many hard working.” | **They are very hard-working.** | **Very** modifies the adjective; **many** modifies plural countable nouns. |
| “Improve the efficiency of the work.” | **Improve work efficiency.** / **It helps me work more efficiently.** | The original phrase is grammatical in context. These are more concise alternatives; **make my work easier** has a related but different meaning. |
| “The sound is not clean.” | **The sand isn't clean.** / **The beach is dirty.** | **Sand**, not **sound**, is the intended word. Audio would be needed to distinguish a speaking error from a transcription error. |
| “I'm use enthusiastic to everyone.” | **I'm friendly to everyone.** / **I'm a friendly and easy-going person.** | Use **be + adjective**. **Enthusiastic about something** expresses excitement or interest; it is not an exact synonym for friendly. |

**Make patterns — added reference:**

| Pattern | Example |
| --- | --- |
| **make + object + adjective** | AI **makes my work easier**. |
| **make + person + base verb** | The interruption **made me lose** my place. |
| **help + person + (to) + base verb** | AI **helps me work more efficiently**. |

In the active pattern, say **makes me work**, not **makes me to work**. For assistance rather than compulsion, **helps me work** is often the more natural choice.

## 5. Vocabulary & Pronunciation Review

| Word or phrase | Meaning or usage | Edited example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **freezing** | Extremely cold; also used about how a person feels | I'm freezing. Let's go inside. |
| **frozen** | Turned to ice, or unable to move; also figurative | The lake is frozen. / I was frozen with fear. |
| **hopeful** | Feeling hope about an outcome | I'm hopeful that things will improve. |
| **optimistic** | Expecting good outcomes | I'm optimistic about the future. |
| **hopeless** | Without hope, or having little prospect of success | I felt hopeless. / The situation seemed hopeless. |
| **pessimistic** | Expecting bad outcomes | He's pessimistic about the job market. |
| **enthusiastic about** | Excited or interested in something | I'm enthusiastic about learning English. |
| **friendly to** | Kind and pleasant towards someone | She's friendly to new colleagues. |
| **easy-going** | Relaxed and not easily upset | My colleague is easy-going. |
| **city break** | A short holiday in a city | I'd like to go on a city break. |
| **litter** | Rubbish left in a public place; usually uncountable | There was litter on the beach. |
| **mark/grade homework** | Assess students' work | AI can help teachers mark homework. |
| **work efficiently** | Work with little wasted time or effort | These tools help us work efficiently. |
| **make friends** | Form friendships | Joining a club helps me make friends. |

**Hopeful and optimistic overlap, but hopeless is stronger than simply pessimistic.** Someone can expect a poor result without feeling entirely without hope.

**Added pronunciation practice:**

- **sand /sænd/** versus **sound /saʊnd/**: “The **sand** is dirty.” / “The **sound** is clear.”
- **degree /dɪˈɡriː/** → **degrees /dɪˈɡriːz/**: keep the final **/z/** in “forty degrees.”
- Practise the complete phrases **many chances**, **communication skills**, and **make friends**, keeping the plural endings audible.

## 7. Review Exercises — Added Study Material

### A. Choose a Form That Matches the Meaning

Complete each sentence using the requested form. These prompts practise particular meanings; other forms may be grammatical in other contexts.

1. Right now, we ______ about AI. *(talk; present continuous)*
2. The table is booked. I ______ dinner with a friend tomorrow. *(have; present continuous for an arrangement)*
3. That T-shirt looks nice. I think I ______ it. *(buy; will for a decision now)*
4. I believe AI ______ many jobs in the future. *(change; will for a prediction)*
5. I have decided to read more, but I haven't chosen the stories yet. I ______ more short stories. *(read; going to for an intention)*
6. According to the timetable, the train ______ at nine tomorrow. *(leave; present simple)*

### C. Check the Rule

Decide whether each statement is true or false, and explain why.

1. Every planned future action must use the present continuous.
2. Will can express a prediction as well as a spontaneous decision.
3. Every present continuous sentence about the future must contain a future time phrase.
4. A long action always requires the past continuous.
5. “I was reading when the phone rang” proves that I stopped reading.
6. “I like sweet foods” can refer to a preference for different kinds of sweet food.

**Answers:**

1. **False.** Going to, present simple, and will can also describe future situations, with different emphasis and context.
2. **True.** It can also express promises and offers.
3. **False.** The future time can be established elsewhere in the conversation.
4. **False.** “I worked all day yesterday” presents a long activity as a completed whole.
5. **False.** It locates the ringing during the reading; stopping is not guaranteed.
6. **True.** Food is often uncountable, but foods can refer to types.

### D. Short Story: Notice the Tense Changes

**Added fictional practice story, not an account of the learner's weekend:**

> Last Saturday, we **went** camping by the river. While my friend **was putting up** the tent, I **was unpacking** our bags. Suddenly, my phone **rang**. I **answered** it and **spoke** to a friend. Later, we **sat** beside the river and **watched** the sunset.

**Review:** **Went, rang, answered, spoke, sat**, and **watched** present completed events or periods. **Was putting up** and **was unpacking** present overlapping activities in progress. The simple past does not mean every event was brief.
