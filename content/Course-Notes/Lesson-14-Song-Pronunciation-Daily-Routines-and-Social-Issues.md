## 1. Connected Speech Through Music

### The main lesson

Common connected-speech patterns can make listening easier. Prioritise recognising them before deliberately producing them. Clear pronunciation is useful; natural speech also involves stress, rhythm, linking, and appropriate weak forms. **Naturalness does not require maximum speed or indiscriminate consonant deletion.**

| Process | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Assimilation** | A sound changes under the influence of a nearby sound. | /n/ may become [m] before /p/. |
| **Elision** | A sound is omitted. | /d/ may disappear from a weak form of **and**. |
| **Weak form** | An unstressed word uses a reduced pronunciation. | **A** is commonly /ə/; **and** may be /ən/. |
| **Linking** | Sounds connect across a word boundary without a pause. | The /k/ in **like a** can link into the following vowel. |
| **Flapping** | /t/ or /d/ can have a brief tongue-tip contact, [ɾ], in suitable environments. | The /t/ in American **beautiful** is commonly flapped. |
| **Unreleased stop** | A closure is retained without a separately audible release burst. | Some final /t/ sounds before another consonant. |

### Reassessing the reported song examples

These are brief phrase references from the supplied material, not a verified transcription of the performance. Singing can stretch vowels, shift stress, and obscure consonants through melody and accompaniment.

| Reported phrase or observation | Accurate learning point |
| --- | --- |
| **like a diamond**: /k/ and /d/ seemed absent | **Like a** can link as /laɪk ə/ without deleting /k/. The initial /d/ of **diamond** is not routinely deleted by a general English rule. A weakly heard consonant is not proof of its absence. |
| **you and I** | **And** may have a weak form such as /ən/, with /d/ absent. This is reduction/elision, not necessarily assimilation. |
| **beautiful** | American /t/ commonly becomes [ɾ], a tap rather than the usual English /r/. British pronunciation also varies; it is not always a fully released [t]. |
| **in the sky** | **The** normally has weak /ðə/ here. Do not replace it with **a** because the consonant is hard to hear. The /k/ is inside **sky**, not a separate sound before that word; there is no general rule to delete it. |
| **we are** | **We're** is an ordinary contraction. Its relationship to **where** depends on accent; similarity in sound does not change the words or grammar. |
| **right away** | Linking and, in suitable accents, a possible tap can make the boundary compact. Do not assume /t/ must disappear. |
| **at first sight** | Several /t/ sounds occur here. Their realization depends on the following sound and accent. A final /t/ without a clear burst can still be present; do not deliberately turn **sight** into **sigh**. |
| **inside you** | **You** can be unstressed, but is not generally deleted. /d/ + /j/ can coalesce into [dʒ] in connected speech. Whether that happens in this performance needs audio. |
| **diamonds** | The regular plural ending is /z/ after the voiced /d/ in the base word /ˈdaɪmənd/. It does not directly follow /n/ in the underlying form. Some speakers reduce the /d/ in the cluster, but that does not explain the suffix as /n/ turning /s/ into /z/. |
| The phrase transcribed as **moonshine and my life** | The words may have been misheard. Check a reliable lyric source and the exact audio before analysing the phrase. Reduction of **and** is possible, but the wording remains unverified. |

### Added listening routine

1. Listen to a short segment once without reading; write what you hear.
2. Check the words against a reliable lyric source.
3. Replay it and identify one process: **linking**, **weak form**, **assimilation**, or **elision**.
4. Say the words clearly in ordinary speech, then imitate the rhythm gently.
5. Compare the same expression in spoken audio. A song is useful practice, but not a universal pronunciation model.

## 2. Speaking Task: A Time of Day You Enjoy

### Reported content

The learner chose the period **after lunch**, during a midday break. Activities included walking in a park for around an hour, listening to birds, watching videos, and listening to music. She usually enjoyed the time alone but sometimes spoke to people from different regions. The break helped her feel refreshed before returning to work.

**Time clarification:** **After lunch, during my midday break** is clearer than “after lunch but before the afternoon,” because lunch and early afternoon can overlap.

### Added cue card

Describe a time of day you enjoy. Say:

- when it is;
- what you usually do;
- who you spend it with;
- why you enjoy it.

**Structure:** **Time → Activities → Company → Benefit**.

### Added model answer

> My favourite time of day is the break after lunch. If I have enough time, I go for a walk in a nearby park, sometimes for about an hour. I enjoy listening to the birds singing and taking a break from work. I also listen to music or watch a few videos. I'm usually on my own, and I enjoy the peace and quiet. Occasionally, I chat with people from different parts of the country. What I like most is that the break helps me recharge, so I feel more focused when I return to work.

**Useful phrases:** **during my lunch break**, **go for a walk**, **take a walk**, **walk around the park**, **enjoy some peace and quiet**, **clear my head**, **recharge**, **feel refreshed**, **return to work**.

**Alone versus lonely:** **I'm alone** describes being without other people. **I feel lonely** describes an unwanted feeling of isolation. You can enjoy being alone without feeling lonely.

## 3. Speaking Task: A Song You Like

### Reported content

- The learner discussed **Ceng Jing De Ni**, a Chinese rock song by **Xu Wei**. The supplied summary calls it “Once You”; English translations vary, so the romanized title avoids presenting one translation as official.
- The learner interpreted it as a reflection on youthful dreams, travel, growing older, and adult responsibilities.
- She felt it encouraged listeners to reconnect with their enthusiasm for life.
- She had heard it since childhood but understood its meaning more deeply as an adult.
- Xu Wei recently performed in her city, but she did not attend.
- Claims that the song prompted people to resign and travel were reported impressions, not verified effects.

### Added cue card

Describe a song you like. Explain:

- what it is and who performs it;
- when you first heard it;
- what it means to you;
- why you like it.

**Structure:** **Song → First encounter → Meaning → Personal connection**.

### Added model answer

> I'd like to talk about a Chinese rock song called *Ceng Jing De Ni*, performed by Xu Wei. I've listened to it since I was young, but I understand it differently now that I'm an adult. To me, it is about youthful dreams and how life changes as we take on more responsibilities. It reminds me not to lose my curiosity or enthusiasm for life. I find it moving because it reflects real experiences rather than an ideal world. Xu Wei recently gave a concert in my city, but I didn't get a ticket. Even so, I still enjoy listening to his recordings.

**Useful phrases:** **The song resonates with me**, **It touches my heart**, **It reminds me of my younger self**, **It reflects real-life experiences**, **pursue a dream**, **make a dream come true**.

### Discussing Music Preferences

**Added example:** **I prefer slower music with lyrics about everyday experiences.** Explain a preference without treating it as a fact about every song by an artist.

## 4. The learner's Grammar & Expression Review

The following audit corrects the supplied error analysis where needed. A teacher's alternative wording does not automatically mean the original expression was wrong.

### A. Clear errors or strong corrections, assuming accurate transcription

| Reported wording | Better version | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “listening the bird sing” | **listening to the birds singing** | **Listen to** needs **to** before its object. Singular **listening to the bird sing** is also grammatical. |
| “make our dreams go through” | **make our dreams come true** | Fixed expression for achieving dreams. **Go through** has other meanings, such as experience or examine. |
| “from different area” | **from different areas** | Multiple areas → plural noun. **From a different area** describes one other area. |
| “talk with some peoples” | **talk with some people** | **People** is the normal plural of **person**. **Peoples** refers to distinct populations or ethnic groups. **Talk with** itself is correct. |
| “The white skin are more beauty” | **Some people consider fair skin more beautiful.** | **Skin** takes singular agreement when it is the subject; **beautiful** is an adjective, **beauty** a noun. This revision attributes the preference rather than presenting it as fact. |
| “I haven't get the ticket” | **I haven't got a ticket yet.** / **I didn't get a ticket.** | **Have + past participle**; **gotten** is also used in American English. Choose present perfect for an open situation, past simple for a finished concert. |
| “what your aim and what you want to do has changed” | **Your goals have changed.** | The original structure is incomplete; **what your aim is** would be a complete embedded clause. A plural subject such as **your goals** takes **have**. |

**Perception-verb pattern:** Both **listen to the birds sing** and **listen to the birds singing** are grammatical. The base form can present the event as a whole; **-ing** highlights an ongoing activity. You do not always have to replace **sing** with **singing**.

### B. Correct expressions that should not be labelled errors

| Expression | Accurate assessment |
| --- | --- |
| **I take a walk.** | Correct and natural. **I go for a walk** is another natural choice. A hesitation inside the phrase is a fluency issue, not a grammatical defect. |
| **In the park** versus **in a park** | Both are correct. **The park** refers to an identifiable or familiar park; **a park** introduces an unspecified one. Choose by meaning, not speech speed. |
| **I am usually alone.** | Correct. **I'm usually alone** is a common conversational contraction, not a required correction. |
| **I still feel happy.** | Correct if happiness continues despite something or over time. **I feel happy** may be enough if no contrast or continuation is intended. |
| **The flavour is different.** | Correct when describing actual flavour. For music or personal preferences, use **Our tastes are different** or **We have different preferences**. |

**Important:** Weak pronunciation of **in the** does not turn it grammatically into **in a**. Listening uncertainty and article choice are separate issues.

### C. Unclear fragments: reconstruct cautiously

- **“I even go to the ticket”**: too unclear for a precise diagnosis. If referring to a missed concert, **I didn't get a ticket** is possible. **I couldn't get a ticket** adds inability, which the supplied summary does not confirm.
- **“The song record that promote that way”**: the intended structure is unclear. A possible complete sentence is **The song encourages people to pursue their dreams** or **The song promotes the idea that people should reconnect with their dreams**.
- **“I'm in the family”**: grammatical in other contexts, but unclear here. If the learner meant she admired the singer, **I'm a fan of his** fits. This is a possible reconstruction, not a verified correction.
- **“race racism” / “reason but a joke”**: likely word-finding or transcription difficulties. A careful complete statement is **It may have been intended as a joke, but it could still reinforce a stereotype**. Calling something a joke does not establish that it is free of prejudice.

### Four priority patterns

1. **Listen to + object:** listen to music; listen to birds singing.
2. **Plural nouns:** different areas; some people; several songs.
3. **Have + past participle:** haven't got/gotten; have listened; have changed.
4. **Natural collocations:** dreams come true; a song resonates with me; feel refreshed.

## 5. Social Discussion: Equality, Racism & Skin Tone

### Martin Luther King Jr.

The class discussed the American civil rights leader and his **“I Have a Dream”** speech. His message included judging people by their character rather than skin colour and securing equal rights for Black Americans.

**Added historical clarification:** King delivered the speech in **1963** and was assassinated in **1968**. The summary's claim that he was killed simply because the speech was influential is an unsupported reduction of a complex historical event.

### Making comparisons carefully

The reported discussion suggested that racial equality had improved in the United States and that China had regional stereotypes rather than racism. These require qualification:

- **Legal progress does not mean discrimination has disappeared.** Discuss a specific period, institution, or experience instead of assuming equal outcomes everywhere.
- **Racism cannot be ruled out across an entire country.** Regional stereotypes, racial prejudice, and ethnic discrimination are different categories that can coexist.
- **Colourism** refers to bias or unequal treatment based on skin tone, including within the same racial or ethnic group. A preference for lighter skin can be an aesthetic preference, but can also reflect or contribute to colourism. Its effects depend on context.
- A north–south disagreement about sweet or savoury tofu pudding is an example of **regional food preferences**; it does not establish whether racial discrimination exists.

**Added balanced model answer:**

> I learned about the American civil rights movement in history class. There has been important progress in legal equality, but racial discrimination still exists. In my own experience, I hear more discussion of regional stereotypes and preferences for lighter skin. However, my personal experience is limited, so I would avoid saying that racism does not exist in a whole country. I think we should judge people as individuals and pay attention to whether stereotypes lead to unfair treatment.

**Vocabulary:** **racial equality**, **civil rights**, **prejudice**, **regional stereotypes**, **colourism**, **fair skin**, **discriminate against someone**, **judge someone by their character**.
