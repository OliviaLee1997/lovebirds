## 1. Describing Pictures: A Useful Structure

**Added study framework:** Move from the overall scene to details, then explain possible meanings and connect them to your experience.

1. **Introduce the scene:** “The picture shows…”
2. **Locate details:** “In the foreground…” / “In the background…”
3. **Describe actions:** “They are holding books.”
4. **Make a cautious inference:** “They might be preparing for an exam.”
5. **Give a personal response:** “I prefer working in pairs because…”

### Choosing the Tense

| Purpose | Tense or structure | Added example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Describe what the picture contains | **Present simple** | The picture **shows** six children. |
| Describe an action shown | **Present continuous** | Two men **are practising** martial arts. |
| State a preference or habit | **Present simple** | I **prefer** sitting in the middle. |
| Suggest an earlier event | **Past simple / present perfect** | Maybe she **got** a good mark. / She **may have received** a good mark. |
| Discuss life experience | **Present perfect** | I **have never swum** in a river. |

**Clarification:** A still image does not require every sentence to be in the present simple. Choose the tense according to the meaning. Separate **what is visible** from **what you infer**: a smile is visible; the reason for it is uncertain.

## 2. Picture One: Children on a Wooden Boat

### Reported Scene and Discussion

- Six children were described on a wooden boat near a shore. The initial count of six boys was revised to **five boys and one girl**.
- The children were described as having dark skin, large eyes, and curly hair. Participants suggested that they might be from Central Africa.
- The water was identified in the discussion as a **river** and described as looking cleaner than other rivers.
- The learner prefers **flying to travelling by boat** because she feels dizzy on boats. She said that flying feels safer to her.
- She considers herself a good swimmer but has **never swum in a river**. The discussion compared river currents with swimming-pool conditions.

**Description clarification:** A person's nationality or region of origin cannot be established from appearance alone. Treat the suggested location and gender count as classroom interpretations. Water that looks clear is not necessarily clean or safe, and river swimming conditions vary with the current and other factors. The learner's transport comparison expresses her personal view.

### Useful Language

- **a wooden boat near the riverbank**
- **feel dizzy / feel seasick** — dizziness describes a sensation; seasickness is motion sickness associated with boat travel.
- **a strong current** — moving water that can make swimming difficult.
- **prefer A to B:** “I prefer flying **to** travelling by boat.”
- **be good at + noun / -ing:** “I'm good at swimming.”

**Edited model answer — added study material:**

> The picture shows six children on a wooden boat near the shore. They have curly hair, and the water looks clear. Personally, I prefer flying to travelling by boat because I often feel dizzy on boats. Although I'm a good swimmer, I've never swum in a river. Swimming against a current can take more effort than swimming in a pool.

## 3. Picture Two: A Classroom

### Reported Scene and Discussion

- The picture was described as showing **five students**, interpreted as international students from different countries.
- One female student was smiling. A possible explanation was that she had received a good mark.
- The learner prefers sitting **in the middle of the classroom**, where she feels less directly watched by the teacher than in the front row.
- She described herself as an **active student** who enjoys conversations.
- The learner's high-school classmates were mostly Chinese. She also mentioned that she did not have many friends in high school.
- Compare interests in **science**, **literature** and **languages** using **more interested in**.

**Clarification:** Students' nationalities and the reason for a smile are interpretations unless additional context confirms them. Enjoying class participation and having a small friendship circle can both be true.

### Useful Language

- **sit in the front row / in the middle / at the back**
- **take part in class discussions**
- **get a good mark** — common in British English; **get a good grade** is common in American English.
- **be more interested in science than literature**

**Edited model answer — added study material:**

> The picture shows five students in a classroom. One student is smiling; perhaps she got a good mark. I prefer sitting in the middle of the classroom because I feel more comfortable there. I'm usually active in class, and I enjoy taking part in discussions. At high school, most of my classmates were Chinese, and I was more interested in science than literature.

## 4. Picture Three: A Group Study Session

### Reported Scene and Discussion

- A group of students, described as mostly girls and one boy, were holding books in a meeting room or reading corner.
- Participants suggested they might be preparing for **a presentation or an exam**.
- The learner prefers **working in pairs** to working alone or in a large group because it is easier to communicate and share ideas.
- An instructor agreed that pair work can give each person more opportunities to speak.
- The learner has given presentations at university and finds the **question-and-answer session** particularly challenging.

### Useful Language

| Expression | Added example |
| --- | --- |
| **work individually / in pairs / in groups** | We usually work in pairs. |
| **share ideas / exchange ideas** | Pair work helps us exchange ideas. |
| **prepare for an exam** | They might be preparing for an exam. |
| **prepare a presentation** | We are preparing a presentation about city life. |
| **give a presentation** | I gave several presentations at university. |
| **answer questions on the spot** | It's difficult to answer questions on the spot. |

**Vocabulary clarification:** A **lecture** usually teaches an audience about a subject, often in an academic setting. A **presentation** is a broader term and fits many student speaking tasks. They are not always interchangeable.

**Edited model answer — added study material:**

> The students are holding books and seem to be studying together. They might be preparing for a presentation or an exam. I prefer working in pairs because both people have more time to speak and share ideas. I've given presentations at university, but I find the Q&A session challenging because I have to answer questions on the spot.

## 5. Picture Four: Chinese Martial Arts

### Reported Scene and Discussion

- Two men were described as practising **kung fu or tai chi** in front of a Chinese temple in the mountains.
- The participants interpreted one as a master and the other as a visitor from another country learning from him.
- **Red lanterns** were associated with celebration or special occasions, and a **stone lion** with power.
- The learner said that she had tried kung fu and that it is popular with both men and women.
- The discussion mentioned **Bruce Lee** and the international influence of Chinese martial arts.
- The supplied summary records a film title as **“Nan Shaku”**; its intended title is unclear.

**Added clarification:** **Martial arts** is the broad category. **Kung fu** commonly refers to Chinese martial arts in English; **tai chi**, also called **taijiquan**, is a specific Chinese martial art. They are not exact synonyms. Chinese guardian lion statues are often associated with **protection and power**, while red lanterns can suggest celebration and good fortune; meaning depends on context. A person's role or nationality needs contextual support.

**Edited model answer — added study material:**

> Two men are practising Chinese martial arts in front of a temple in the mountains. One appears to be teaching the other. There are red lanterns and a stone lion near the temple. I've tried kung fu before. Chinese martial arts are well known around the world, partly through films and famous performers such as Bruce Lee.

## 6. Picture Five: A Mental Health Consultation

### Reported Scene and Discussion

- The image was described as showing two women in a room resembling a living room.
- Participants interpreted one as a **psychiatrist** and the other as a **patient**, possibly discussing medication.
- The learner suggested that **pressure from work or school** and a person's **environment** can contribute to psychological difficulties.
- An instructor added **shock or traumatic experiences**, such as serious accidents, as possible contributing factors.

**Interpretation clarification:** The image alone does not establish a person's profession, a diagnosis, or whether medication is being discussed. Mental health conditions can involve interacting biological, psychological, and social factors; stress and trauma are possible contributors, not a complete explanation of every condition.

### Psychiatrist, Psychologist, and Counsellor

| Term | General meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **psychiatrist** | A medical doctor specialising in mental health who can prescribe medication. |
| **psychologist** | A professional trained in psychology; clinical psychologists may assess mental health and provide therapy. Most do not prescribe medication, though rules vary by location and qualifications. |
| **counsellor / therapist** | A professional providing counselling or therapy; titles, training requirements, and permitted activities vary by location. |

**Useful phrases:** **mental health**, **experience stress**, **be under pressure**, **a traumatic experience**, **seek professional support**, **explain how to take medication**.

**Edited model answer — added study material:**

> The picture shows two women talking in a comfortable room. One might be a mental health professional, and the other might be a patient. They could be discussing treatment. Stress at work or school, difficult living conditions, and traumatic experiences can contribute to mental health problems.

## 7. Picture Six: A Busy Urban Street

### Reported Scene and Discussion

- The cartoon-style picture included shops selling **fruit, bread, and meat**, **St. Mary's School**, and cars, buses, bicycles, and a train.
- A plane was visible in the blue sky, and the area behind the railway looked like a forest.
- The learner said that **tourist areas and markets** in her city are often crowded.
- She prefers supermarkets because she can buy everything she needs **in one place**, although she rarely visits traditional markets.
- She identified local rush hours as around **8 a.m. and 6 p.m.**
- She said that most people in her city drive, contributing to congestion. In her account, trains are mainly used for **intercity travel** and become crowded during holidays.

**Context:** These are the learner's observations about her city, not general statements about every city or transport system.

### Useful Language

- **a fruit shop / a bakery / a butcher's shop**
- **different modes of transport**
- **during rush hour / at 8 a.m.**
- **heavy traffic / traffic congestion / a traffic jam**
- **a crowded market** — many people; **a congested road** — too much traffic.
- **intercity trains** — trains connecting cities; **urban rail / the metro** — rail transport within an urban area.

**Edited model answer — added study material:**

> The picture shows a busy street with shops, a school, and several modes of transport. A train is travelling along the railway, and a plane is flying overhead. In my city, tourist areas and markets are often crowded. I prefer supermarkets because I can buy everything I need in one place. Traffic is especially heavy around 8 a.m. and 6 p.m., when many people are travelling to or from work.

## 8. Personal Grammar and Expression Review

The first column adapts **reported wording from the supplied error report**, anonymised examples, not audio-verified quotations. Revisions preserve the likely intended meaning.

| Reported wording | Corrected or more natural version | Learning point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “I have never swam in a river.” | **I have never swum in a river.** | Present perfect: **have/has + past participle**. |
| “I didn't saw a woman...” | **I didn't see a woman.** | **Didn't + base verb**. |
| “Maybe she get a good mark.” | **Maybe she got a good mark.** | Use past simple for a possible completed event. For a habit: **Maybe she gets good marks.** |
| “His skin is more lighter than others.” | **His skin is lighter than that of the others.** | Use **lighter**, not **more lighter**; compare skin with skin. |
| “It's more clean than other rivers.” | **It's cleaner than other rivers.** | **Clean** has one syllable; **cleaner** is the usual comparative. For appearance only: **It looks cleaner…** |
| “When I on the boat.” | **When I am on the boat…** | Add the missing verb **am**. This is still a dependent clause; attach it to a main clause. |
| “Sitting in the middle... At the middle.” | **I prefer sitting in the middle of the classroom.** | Use **in the middle** for this location. |
| “They are holding a books.” | **They are holding books.** | Do not use **a/an** with a plural noun. **They are each holding a book** specifies one per person. |
| “Different modes of transports.” | **Different modes of transport.** | **Transport** is uncountable in this expression. |
| “The five boys has black skin.” | **The five boys have dark skin.** | Plural subject **boys → have**. **Dark skin** is a natural appearance description; the agreement error is **has**. |
| “Because I flight flight dizzy Why I'm on the boat?” | **I prefer flying because I feel dizzy when I'm on a boat.** | Reconstruct the likely meaning with **feel dizzy** and **when**. The transcript does not establish exactly what was pronounced. |

### Irregular Verb Forms

| Base form | Past simple | Past participle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **swim** | swam | swum |
| **see** | saw | seen |
| **give** | gave | given |
| **go** | went | gone |
| **feel** | felt | felt |

**Added examples:**

> I **swam** in a pool yesterday, but I've never **swum** in a river.  
> I **saw** the picture, but I didn't **see** the woman at first.  
> I've **given** presentations before. I **gave** one at university last year.

### Three Patterns to Remember

- **Have/has + past participle:** “She has **seen** it.”
- **Do/does/did + base verb:** “He doesn't **like** boats.” / “Did you **see** it?”
- **Be + -ing** for an action shown: “They are **studying** together.”

For comparisons, use **clean → cleaner**, **light → lighter**, and **safe → safer**, but **comfortable → more comfortable**. Avoid combining **more** with an **-er** comparative.

## 9. Feedback and Speaking Practice

### Reported Feedback

The learner described her level as “junior/intermediate” and wanted more practice. This was a self-assessment, not a formal proficiency rating. The supplied feedback praised her willingness to speak, vocabulary, and ability to communicate opinions, while identifying grammar as a useful area for improvement.

The discussion encouraged **daily speaking practice**, with regular opportunities to use English in conversation.

**Added clarification:** Helpful practice partners can include teachers, fluent speakers, and fellow learners. Native-speaker status alone does not determine the quality of practice. Consistency, useful feedback, and opportunities to try again matter.

### A Short Daily Routine — Added Study Material

1. **Describe a picture for one minute:** Include the setting, two details, and one action.
2. **Make one cautious inference:** Use **might**, **could**, or **seems to**.
3. **Add a personal opinion and reason:** Use **I prefer… because…**.
4. **Listen to your recording:** Check one target, such as **did + base verb** or **have + past participle**.
5. **Repeat the answer:** Apply the correction while keeping a natural pace.

**Q&A phrases to practise:**

> Could you repeat the question, please?  
> Do you mean…?  
> Let me think for a moment.  
> In my experience,…  
> I'm not sure, but I think…

**Study takeaway:** The lesson moved from describing visible details to giving reasons and discussing broader ideas. Pair work suits the learner's stated preference and can provide repeated speaking opportunities; that preference alone does not establish a fixed personality type.
