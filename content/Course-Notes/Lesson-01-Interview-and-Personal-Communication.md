# Lesson 01 — Interview & Personal Communication

**Privacy note:** Personal and third-party details have been removed or generalised. First-person stories and altered quotations are anonymised study examples, not a biographical record or verbatim transcript.

> **Focus:** Learning goals, personal introductions, experiences, interests, and scheduling.  
> **Editorial note:** Repeated summaries have been combined. Model answers are polished study examples based on the notes, not verbatim quotations. Additional explanations and exercises are identified below.

## 1. Learning Goals

### Learning Context

| Area | Study focus |
| --- | --- |
| Work | Describing a role and explaining a project in general terms |

| Learning history | Describing childhood learning and later periods of practice |

| Previous study | Connecting grammar knowledge with spoken communication |
| Topics | Everyday activities, nature, travel and culture |

**Main goal:** Communicate independently in everyday and workplace situations.

### Study Priorities — Editorial Suggestions

1. **Build complete sentences:** Practise subjects, verbs, articles, and noun forms together.
2. **Practise reusable phrases:** Learn expressions such as **communicate with**, **have been to**, and **be available** in context.
3. **Give connected answers:** Start with a direct answer, then add **one reason or example**.
4. **Use relevant topics:** Combine **workplace situations** with personal interests to encourage regular speaking.

## 2. Conversation Review

### Q1. What is your main purpose for learning English?

**Class notes:** the learner needs English to communicate with overseas colleagues and users of the digital project she develops.

**Model answer:**

> I'm a **software engineer**, and I work on an **digital project**.  
> I want to **communicate with colleagues and users from different countries**.

**Key distinction:** An **engineer** is a person; **engineering** is a field of study or work.

### Q2. Which part of English learning do you find most difficult?

**Class notes:** Speaking is the main difficulty because the learner has had limited conversation practice; she said she could understand others much of the time.

**Model answer:**

> **Speaking is the most difficult part** for me.  
> I can usually **understand what people are saying**, but I need more **conversation practice**.

**Key structure:** **Speaking** functions as a noun here; **what people are saying** is a clause describing the information understood.

### Q3. How long have you been learning English?

**Anonymised model answer:**

> I **started learning English when I was a child**. I mainly studied it **for exams**. Later, I **had a long break from regular study**.

**Tense focus:** Use past simple for a completed period; choose the form that matches the intended time.

### Q4. Could you describe where you live?

**Anonymised model answer:**

> I **live in a city**. I **share a home with someone I know**.

**Useful patterns:** **Live with + person**; **live in + place**.

### Q5. Have you ever traveled abroad?

**Class notes:** the learner spent two weeks in the USA, entered a competition, and won an award; she would like to visit Thailand, Indonesia, and Vietnam and enjoys natural scenery.

**Model answer:**

> I **have been to the USA**.  
> I **spent two weeks there** and **won an award in a competition**.  
> It was **an amazing experience**.  
> I'd like to **travel to Thailand, Indonesia, and Vietnam**.  
> I enjoy **seeing beautiful natural scenery** when I travel.

**Tense focus:** **Have been to** introduces a life experience; **spent**, **won**, and **was** give details about that completed trip.

### Q6. What do you usually do in your free time?

**Model answer:**

> I enjoy **hiking in my free time**.  
> I **go swimming twice a week**.  
> Swimming helps me **cool off** during the hot summers in the city.

**Useful patterns:** **Enjoy + -ing**; **go swimming**; **twice a week** for frequency; **cool off** for becoming less hot.

### Q7. Do you prefer being alone or spending time with other people?

**Anonymised model answer:**

> I **prefer quiet places** and enjoy spending time with people I know well. I like **walking in parks** and **going camping with friends**.

**Useful patterns:** **Prefer to do / prefer doing**; **spend time with + person**.

### Q8. Do you like going to cafés or watching movies?

**Class notes:** the learner visits cafés with colleagues, likes cappuccinos or Americanos, rarely goes to cinemas because of ticket prices, and enjoys Indian and horror movies; she described Indian movies as distinctive and popular.

**Model answer:**

> I often **go to cafés with my colleagues**.  
> I usually order **a cappuccino or an Americano**.  
> I rarely **go to the cinema** because the tickets are expensive.  
> I prefer **watching TV at home**.  
> I like **Indian movies and horror movies**.

### Q9. Do you have any favorite foods or drinks?

**Model answer:**

> I **don't have a particular favorite food**.  
> I often **eat fast food** because I don't really like vegetables or meat.  
> I like **coconut juice**.

**Vocabulary clarification:** The notes use **coconut juice**; if the intended meaning is the clear liquid naturally inside a coconut, **coconut water** is the more usual term, and it is different from **coconut milk**.

### Q10. When are you usually available for classes?

**Practice template:**

> I'm **available on [day] after [time]**. We could arrange a lesson then, or choose another time.

### Q11. How do you describe a work arrangement?

**Vocabulary:** **Full-time** and **part-time** describe work arrangements.

**Added examples:** **I work full-time. / I study part-time.**

## 3. Grammar Corrections & Difficult Points

> **How to use this section:** The left column contains expressions recorded in the original notes, including errors; practise the **corrected expressions** in the middle column.

### A. Tenses & Verb Forms

| Recorded expression | Corrected expression | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I have traveling to USA. | I **have traveled to the USA**. / I **have been to the USA**. | Present perfect: **have/has + past participle**; use **the USA**. |
| I haven't em learn english. | I **haven't learned English**. | Use a **past participle** after haven't; the intended time period needs context. |
| what they are say | what they **are saying** | Present continuous: **be + -ing**; this is a clause fragment. |
| I start to learning English. | I **started learning English**. / I **started to learn English**. | Both **start doing** and **start to do** are possible; use past tense for the recorded past event. |
| Indian movies is very different. | Indian movies **are** very different. | A **plural subject** takes are. |

### B. Word Forms, Articles & Complements

| Recorded expression | Corrected expression | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| most difficult part is a speak | The most difficult part is **speaking**. | Use **speaking** to name the activity; a superlative normally takes **the**. |
| I prefer to alone. | I **prefer to be alone**. | Infinitive **to** takes a base-form verb; **be** is needed before alone. |
| I am a software engineering. | I'm **a software engineer**. | **Engineer = person**; **engineering = field**. |
| I don't have best friend. | I don't have **a best friend**. | A singular countable noun normally needs a **determiner**. |
| It's a amazing experience. | It's **an amazing experience**. | Choose a/an according to the following **sound**, not just the letter. |

### C. Questions, Prepositions & Plurals

| Recorded expression | Recommended expression | Explanation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How to speak in your name? | **How do you pronounce** your name? | Ask about **pronunciation**; **What is your name?** asks for different information. |
| How how to how to speak? How to speak it? | **How do you say** this word? | A complete question based on the likely intention; the original wording is unclear. |
| Where do you live in? | **Where do you live?** | Compare with **Which city do you live in?**, where in is appropriate. |
| …like Thailand In Indonesia | …such as **Thailand and Indonesia** | Use **and** or commas to join country names. |
| There are many users … from another country. | There are many users from **other countries**. | Use **other countries** if several countries are intended; **another country** is valid when referring to one other country. |

**Already correct:** I **live in the city**.

**Fluency, not necessarily grammar:** Fillers and repetitions such as **“em,” “for for for,” and “yes yes yes”** may interrupt fluency; practise using a short pause to plan the next phrase instead of treating every repetition as a grammatical error.

## 4. Useful Classroom Quotations

### Language Learning Requires Practice

> “Language is **practice**, so if you don't **practice the language**, you will not be able to **speak it fluently**.”

**Study takeaway:** Put vocabulary and grammar into **actual conversations**, rather than only recognizing them in exercises.

### Solitude Creates Space for Thought

> “It's good to **be alone sometimes** so that we can have enough time to **contemplate**, to **think freely**, to **make decisions**.”

**Language focus:** **So that** introduces a purpose; **contemplate** means to think carefully or deeply.

### Learning Approach — Editorial Suggestions

- **Use work situations:** Practise platform issues, user feedback, and international collaboration.
- **Include personal interests:** Use nature, travel, and pets to support spontaneous speaking.
- **Connect rules to speech:** After reviewing a correction, immediately use it in a **new sentence**.
- **Try a comfortable setting:** A quiet one-to-one lesson may suit the learner's stated preferences; interests alone do not establish which teaching method will work best.

## 5. Review Practice

> **Additional practice:** These exercises were added during editing and are not recorded teacher assignments.

### A. Complete the Sentences

1. I'm ______ software engineer. *(a / an)*
2. It was ______ amazing experience. *(a / an)*
3. I prefer to ______ alone. *(be / being)*
4. I started ______ English at the age of eight. *(learn)*
5. I have ______ to the USA. *(be)*
6. Indian movies ______ very different. *(is / are)*

**Answers:** 1. **a**; 2. **an**; 3. **be**; 4. **learning / to learn**; 5. **been**; 6. **are**.

### B. Speaking Tasks

- **Introduce yourself:** Give four to six sentences about your work, home, learning goals, and main difficulty.
- **Describe a trip:** Introduce the experience with **have been to**, then give details in the **simple past**.
- **Explain a preference:** Use **enjoy doing** or **prefer to do**, followed by a reason.
- **Arrange a class:** Use **be available** to explain your schedule.

### C. Review Checklist

- [ ] I can distinguish **engineer** from **engineering**.
- [ ] I can use **have/has + past participle** to describe an experience.
- [ ] I check **a/an, plurals, and verb forms**.
- [ ] I can ask complete questions about **pronunciation and location**.
- [ ] I can give an answer with **one supporting detail**.
- [ ] I use **short pauses** to reduce repeated starts.
