**A comprehensive review of the identifiable grammar patterns in your 19 sessions, grouped by structure rather than by lesson or speaking topic.** Repeated instances are combined where they teach the same rule. This review covers everyday conversation, workplace English and general discussion as well as cue-card answers.

**How to read the examples:** Quoted expressions are recorded wording or short excerpts from your notes; they have not been checked against the original audio. ## Your Main Learning Priorities

The recurring difficulty is keeping the **whole sentence structure accurate while developing an answer**: choosing the time frame, building the verb phrase, and maintaining agreement and noun forms. Work on these patterns before trying to add more complicated language. The priorities below reflect repeated patterns and their usefulness; they are not measured error rates or an IELTS score.

| Priority | Habit to build | Correct reference sentence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Choose the time before choosing the verb.** | Separate finished events, current habits and experiences continuing to now. | I **first visited** it in [year]. I **usually go** on Sundays. |
| **2. Complete the verb phrase.** | Check for missing **be**, the right auxiliary, and the required verb form. | She **has been living** abroad. It **doesn't get** crowded. |
| **3. Learn verbs with their following structure.** | Practise **teach someone to do**, **let someone do**, **enjoy doing**, and similar patterns as units. | She **suggested that I contact** him. |
| **4. Match the subject and verb.** | Find the main subject, even when other nouns come between it and the verb. | There **were three tourists**. He **still teaches**. |
| **5. Check the noun phrase.** | Decide singular/plural, countable/uncountable, and whether a determiner is needed. | **A pet**, **two pets**, **some good news**. |
| **6. Keep prepositions with their expressions.** | Learn **listen to**, **similar to**, **interested in**, and **in class** together. | I enjoy **listening to music**. |
| **7. Connect complete ideas.** | Distinguish a clause from a noun phrase and make each pronoun's reference clear. | I was tired **because we hadn't slept**. |

## 1. Tense Choice and Time References

### Finished events: keep the narrative in its past setting

| Recorded wording or excerpt | Correction for the recorded meaning | What to remember |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “I just go outside for eat.” | I just **went outside to eat**. | **Went** for the completed outing; **to eat** expresses purpose. **Just** alone does not force a particular tense. |
| “I fly to London.” — the exercise referred to last month | I **flew to London last month**. | A finished time such as **last month** normally takes past simple. This exercise does not establish that you made the trip. |
| “I drink a coconut water.” — the exercise referred to yesterday | I **drank some coconut water yesterday**. | Use **drank**; a container or serving meaning can change article choice. |
| “When I'm in high school? We learned twelve hours one day.” | **When I was in high school, we studied twelve hours a day.** | Past school period → **was/studied**; **a day** expresses a rate or routine. |
| “When I was a child... I want to be a scientist.” | When I was a child, I **wanted** to be a scientist. | Keep the wish in the childhood time frame. |
| “My math teacher doesn't like me.” | My maths teacher **didn't like me**. | **Context-dependent:** use the past for your account of that earlier relationship. This expresses your perception. |
| “…she choose to get a PhD degree…” | She **chose to do a PhD**. | **Choose → chose** for a completed decision. |
| “She translate late. Chinese to English for me” | She **translated from Chinese into English for me**. | Past tense; **interpreted for me** is precise for live spoken help. The fragment **late** is unclear. |
| “Maybe she get a good mark.” | Maybe she **got a good mark**. | A possible completed event → **got**. A habit would be **gets good marks**. |
| “I have a environment…” | I **had a supportive environment** for learning Russian. | For the past university situation, use **had**. Without the adjective: **an environment**. |
| “temperature is minus forty degree” | The temperature **was minus forty degrees Celsius**. | Past trip → **was**; a numerical temperature needs plural **degrees** here. |
| “I slept for all day and I just wake up.” | I **slept all day and just woke up**. | **Wake → woke**; **all day** is the usual wording. **I've just woken up** is another natural recent-event form. |
| “I first visit…” — referring to [year] | I first **visited** it in [year]. | A first visit at a stated finished time is a past event. |
| “we don't have a seat” — describing the train journey | We **didn't have any seats**. / None of us **had a seat**. | Do not drift into a present-tense report of a finished journey. |

**Your irregular-form slip:** “I catched…” → **I caught…**.

**Added reference:** **go–went–gone; catch–caught–caught; fly–flew–flown; drink–drank–drunk; choose–chose–chosen; see–saw–seen; swim–swam–swum; wake–woke–woken; feel–felt–felt; win–won–won**. The past participle is not always the same as the past simple.

### Current habits versus temporary or past situations

| Recorded wording | Revision when describing the intended situation | Important qualification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “I didn't have enough time to rest.” | I **don't have enough time to relax**. | Use this for a current general situation. **Didn't have** is correct for a past period. |
| “The website always provided newest things.” | The website **provides the latest news**. | Present simple for its current regular function. **Provided** works if you mean its past function. |
| “On friday night we will go to a bar and drink.” | **On Friday nights, we go to a bar.** | This expresses the reported routine. **Will go** can correctly refer to a particular future Friday or, in suitable contexts, habitual behaviour. |
| “We are best friends in high school.” | We **were** best friends in high school. / We **have been** close friends **since high school**. | Choose a past description or a relationship continuing to now. |
| “The grass turns yellow.” | The grass **is yellow**. / The grass **has turned yellow**. | **Context-dependent:** **turns yellow** is correct for a regular change; **is** describes the visible state. |

### Present perfect versus a past reference point

Recorded: **“I have been to the high school before I went to university.”**

For the intended sequence, use **I had finished high school before I started university**. If you only mean attendance, use **I had attended high school before I went to university**. **I finished high school before I started university** is also grammatical: **before** already makes the order clear. Do not change “attended” into “finished” unless completion is intended.

**Added contrast:**

- **I have lived here for five years.** The reference point is now.
- **I had lived there for five years when I changed jobs.** The reference point is a past job change.
- **He has been teaching for many years.** Teaching continues to, or very close to, now.
- **At that time, he had been teaching for twenty years.** The duration is measured up to a past point; this does not tell us that he later stopped.

**Personal speaking check:** Before a story, choose **past event / current habit / continuing experience**. Change tense when the meaning changes, rather than trying to keep every verb in one tense.

## 2. Verb Forms After Auxiliaries and Modals

### Have/has + past participle; have/has been + -ing

| Recorded wording | Correction | Structure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “I have traveling to USA.” | I **have travelled to the USA**. / I **have been to the USA**. | **Have + past participle**. |
| “I haven't em learn english.” | I **haven't learned English**. | Use **learned/learnt** after **haven't**; decide the intended time period from context. |
| “I have visit Harbin.” | I **have visited Harbin**. | **Visited**, not **visit**. |
| “I have never swam in a river.” | I have never **swum** in a river. | **Swam** is past simple; **swum** is the participle. |
| “I haven't get the ticket” | I **haven't got a ticket yet**. / I **didn't get a ticket**. | Present perfect for an open situation; past simple for a finished concert. **Gotten** is a common American participle. |
| “I have been lived there for five years.” | I **have lived** there for five years. / I **have been living** there for five years. | Do not combine the perfect-simple and perfect-continuous forms. |
| “She has been live with her friend…” | She **has been living** with her friend. | **Has been + -ing**. |
| “She has have much power on the research work.” | She **has strong research skills**. / She **is very capable in research**. | For a current ability, use a complete present-tense predicate. **Has had** would be a possible perfect form with a different sentence and meaning. The recorded wording may include a restart. |
| “we have have a test” | We **had a test**. | For the completed test, use past simple. A repeated **have** may also reflect a restart, not a stable rule error. |

### Be + -ing for a continuous verb phrase

| Recorded wording | Correction | Structure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “what they are say” | what they **are saying** | **Are + -ing**. This is a clause that can be placed inside a full sentence. |
| “AI making life better.” | AI **is making life better**. | Include the auxiliary **is**. |
| “It growing rapidly.” | It **is growing rapidly**. | A standalone statement needs **is growing**, not only **growing**. |

### Modal or do/does/did + base verb

| Recorded wording | Correction | Structure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Everything will goes.” | Everything **will go well**. | **Will + base verb**. |
| “I didn't saw a woman...” | I **didn't see** a woman. | **Didn't + base verb**, not a second past-tense marker. |
| “I needn't to check every website.” | I **needn't check** every website. / I **don't need to check** every website. | Two correct patterns; do not mix them. |
| “I will … spending time go there” | I'll **spend some time looking around the shop**. | **Will spend** and **spend time doing**. If travel time is meant: **spend some time getting there**. |

**Added contrast:** **I will be spending…** is grammatical because **will** is followed by the base verb **be**, which is then followed by **spending**. **The work will be completed** similarly contains **will + be**, then a passive participle. The base-verb rule concerns the verb immediately after the modal.

**Personal speaking check:** After **didn't / doesn't / will / can / should / needn't**, check the next verb. After **have/has**, decide whether you need a participle alone or **been + -ing**.

## 3. Complete Predicates, Missing Be, and Active/Passive Confusion

### Include the verb that gives the sentence its structure

| Recorded wording or excerpt | Correction | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “I prefer to alone.” | I **prefer to be alone**. | **Alone** cannot replace the verb **be** here. |
| “When I on the boat.” | **When I am on a boat, I feel dizzy.** | Add **am**, then a main clause; the original **when** clause alone is incomplete as a planned full sentence. |
| “Beijing located in the north of China.” | Beijing **is located in the north of China**. | **Is located**, or simply **is in**. |
| “Most of the time I will angry.” | I often **get angry**. / In that period, I often **got angry**. | Include **be/get** before **angry**; choose time separately. **I will get angry** is a possible future meaning. |
| “The next event will in the [month]” | My next event **will be in [month]**. | Include **be**; normally no **the** before a month name. |
| “Don't stuck in one tough situation.” | Don't **get stuck** in a tough situation. | **Stuck** needs **get/be** in this structure. |
| “She never back the back to China.” | She **never came back to China**. / She **hasn't come back to China**. | **Back** does not itself express the return action here. Choose a closed past period or a situation continuing to now. |
| “I back to the North China.” | I **went back to northern China**. / I **returned to North China**. | Add a movement verb; normally no **the** in these place expressions. |
| “back to the university” — intended as a full past clause | We **went back to the university**. | Add the subject and verb. A short answer can be a fragment, but a full narrative clause needs more. |
| “the files not work” | The files **don't work**. / The file **doesn't work**. | For ordinary simple-present negation, include **do/does**. **The files aren't opening** uses a different, continuous structure. The recording is fragmentary. |

**Do not overgeneralise:** **Back** can be a verb meaning support, as in **I back your proposal**. That does not make **I back to China** a normal way to say **I return to China**.

### Use an ordinary verb without adding an unnecessary be

| Recorded wording | Correction | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “a vowel… that is not exist” | a sound that **does not exist** in my first language | **Exist** is the main verb; use **does not exist**. |
| “this voice is exist in Arabic” | This **sound exists** in Arabic. | No **is** before **exists**; **sound** is the intended speech term. |
| “keyboard is consist of…” | It **consists of**… / It **is composed of**… | These are different verb patterns. For the actual object, **A keyboard has keys for letters, numbers and symbols** is clearer. |

### Distinguish doing an action from receiving an action

| Recorded wording | Correction | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “The situation was occurred.” | The incident **occurred**. | **Occur** does not take a passive in this use. |
| “it was happened” | It **happened**. | **Happen** does not take a passive in this use. |
| “The event was sudden happen” | The event **happened suddenly**. / It **happened unexpectedly**. | Choose a main verb and an adverb; suddenness and lack of expectation are different meanings. |
| “The person who I am respected” | The person **I respect** | **I respect him** is active. **I am respected** means others respect me. |
| “The picture… stored there for several years.” | The picture **has been stored on my phone for several years**. / **I've kept it on my phone** for several years. | A passive needs the appropriate auxiliary chain. The recorded fragment may omit words. |
| “my country is divide was divided…” | My country **was divided into several smaller states**. | If describing that historical event, use **was + past participle**. The record may contain a successful restart. The historical claim itself needs context. |
| “Its built for army purpose.” | It **was built for military purposes**. | Past passive for completed historical construction. **Is built** can be grammatical in other contexts; the written **its/it's** spelling is not an audible grammar distinction. |

**Added reference:** **The teacher called me** → **I was called by the teacher**. This has a direct object that can become the passive subject. **The incident happened** has no such object in this meaning.

**Personal speaking check:** Ask: **Who is doing what?** If the sentence describes a state or location, check for **be**. If it already contains **exists, consists, happened, occurred**, do not add **be** automatically.

## 4. Subject–Verb Agreement

| Recorded wording or excerpt | Correction | Main subject |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Indian movies is very different.” | Indian movies **are** very different. | **Movies**, plural. |
| “it translate” | It **translates**. | **It**, third-person singular. |
| “…only big city have… job opportunity.” | **Big cities have job opportunities.** / **A big city has job opportunities.** | Match singular/plural forms. The original **only** claim can be softened to **Big cities offer more opportunities in my field**. |
| “It have been raining for a week.” | It **has been raining** for a week. | **It → has**. |
| “Some job is replaced by AI.” | Some **jobs are being replaced** by AI. / Some **jobs may be replaced** by AI. | Plural jobs for the intended general statement. A current process and a possibility are different meanings. |
| “The five boys has black skin.” | The five boys **have dark skin**. | **Boys → have**. The choice of **dark** improves the description but is not the agreement repair. |
| “the textbooks is… it's…” | The textbooks **were**… **They**… | Plural subject and plural pronoun; **are** if describing them now. |
| “he was my teacher… he still teach” | He was my teacher, and he **still teaches**. | **He → teaches**; the two time frames are appropriate. |
| “There was only three tourists.” | There **were** only three tourists. | **Three tourists** determines the formal agreement. |
| “their interest and their passion is…” | Their interest and their passion **are**… | Two distinct subjects joined by **and** normally take a plural verb. |
| “it don't have so many people” | It **doesn't get too crowded**. / There **aren't many people there**. | **It → doesn't**; the second version uses a different subject and structure. |
| “they goes goes old” | They **get older**. / They **grow old**. | **They → get/grow**, without third-person **-s**. |
| “business about the pets are very popular” | The **pet industry is** popular. / **Pet-related businesses are** popular. | Choose singular **industry** or plural **businesses**, then match the verb. |

**A longer-subject pattern from your record:** in **“the status of… white people… are more higher…”**, the head noun is **status**, not the nearby plural **people**. For the structure, use **The status of X is higher than that of Y**. The historical or social claim would need a specific context; this is a grammar formula.

**Another combined issue:** “The white skin are more beauty” involves singular **skin**, adjectival **beautiful**, and the meaning of the claim. A contextualised version is **Some people consider fair skin more beautiful**. This expresses an attributed preference rather than a universal fact.

**Added contrast:** **A number of shops are closing** versus **The number of shops is falling**. Find the main subject rather than automatically matching the closest noun.

**Personal speaking check:** Circle the subject mentally: **it / he / they / the number / the people**. Then choose the verb ending or auxiliary.

## 5. Verb Complements and Parallel Structure

**The word after a verb depends on that verb's pattern.** Adding **to** everywhere, or using **-ing** everywhere, will not work.

| Recorded wording | Correction | Pattern to practise |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “I start to learning English.” | I **started learning English**. / I **started to learn English**. | **Start doing / start to do**; do not mix the two. |
| “I started a learning Russian.” | I **started learning Russian**. | No article before this verb complement. |
| “We teach blind people swim.” | We **teach blind people to swim**. | **Teach + person + to-infinitive**. |
| “I encourage the people, uh, use bicycles more.” | I **encourage people to use bicycles more**. | **Encourage + person + to-infinitive**. For people generally, omit **the**. |
| “She suggested me to go to another high school.” | She **suggested that I go** to another school. / She **advised me to go**. | **Suggest that + clause** or **suggest doing**; **advise someone to do**. |
| “I want the driving skill is about my motorcycle driving skill” | I **want to improve my motorcycle riding skills**. | **Want to + base verb**. For another person's action: **want someone to do something**. |
| “It's hard to decided it.” | It's hard **to decide**. / It's hard **to tell**. | Infinitival **to + base verb**. The choice of **decide/tell** depends on meaning. |
| “so glad to hearing that” | I was so glad **to hear that**. | **Glad + to-infinitive**. |
| “Let me started to answer…” | Let me **start answering**. / Let me **answer**. | **Let + object + base verb**. **Let me start to answer** also works. |
| “It doesn't let me concentrated.” | It doesn't let me **concentrate**. | **Let + object + base verb**. For swimming's positive effect, say **It helps me concentrate**. |
| “I enjoy talking… and spend time…” | I enjoy **talking… and spending time…** | Both activities share **enjoy**, so both use **-ing**. |
| “I will … spending time go there” | I'll **spend time looking around**. / I'll **spend time getting there**. | **Spend time doing**. Distinguish browsing time from travelling time. |
| “I wish you have a good time” | I **hope you have a good time**. / I **wish you a pleasant trip**. | **Hope + clause** for an ordinary positive future hope; **wish + person + noun phrase** for a good wish. |

**Natural activity wording:** recorded **“I love travels too.”** → **I love travelling too / I love to travel too** when naming the activity. **Travels** is a real noun meaning journeys, so the original is better treated as an awkward choice for this meaning than as proof that the plural noun never exists.

### Make, let and help are different patterns

| Recorded wording or excerpt | Clear revision | Explanation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “let the life alive” | Pets **make my life more interesting**. | A reconstruction for the intended pet-related meaning: **make + object + adjective**. The fragment does not establish one exact original sentence. |
| “It make me makes me work my work easily.” | It **makes my work easier**. / It **helps me work more easily**. | Choose one structure: a result affecting **my work**, or help given to **me**. The second wording changes the verb to express assistance. |
| “made me embarrassment” | It **made me feel embarrassed**. / It **caused me embarrassment**. | **Make someone feel + adjective** versus **cause someone + noun**. |

**Added comparison:**

- **It makes me practise.** A cause or requirement; **make + person + base verb** in the active form.
- **It lets me practise.** Permission or opportunity; **let + person + base verb**.
- **It helps me practise / helps me to practise.** Assistance; both forms are possible.
- **It encourages me to practise.** **To** is required in this pattern.

### Parallel structure depends on what each action belongs to

**Added practice:**

> I enjoy **swimming, walking and spending time outdoors**.

All three actions are complements of **enjoy**.

> I enjoy **swimming**, and **I spend** time outdoors every weekend.

There are two separate clauses here. **Spend** does not have to become **spending** simply because **swimming** appeared earlier.

### Related structures to retain without inventing extra personal errors

- **Worth doing:** **It's worth visiting**; **It's well worth visiting**; **It was time well spent**. The pattern **“It's well spending…”** was discussed, but the notes do not clearly establish it as a separate personal utterance.
- **Prepositional to + -ing:** **I'm addicted to checking my phone**. **Addicted to social media** was already a correct phrase in your notes.
- **Suggest to someone that…:** **She suggested to me that I contact him** is possible. The problem is specifically **suggest me to do**, not every combination of **suggest**, a person and **to**.
- **Hope and wish:** **I wish I had more time** expresses an unreal present wish; **I wish I had contacted him** expresses past regret. These are different from **I hope you have a good time**.

**Personal speaking check:** Learn a short complete phrase for each verb: **teach me to swim; let me try; enjoy swimming; spend time reading; suggest that I go**.

## 6. Countable Nouns, Plurals and Quantity

### Plural nouns after numbers and quantity expressions

| Recorded wording or excerpt | Correction | Pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “two parrot” | two **parrots** | Number greater than one + plural countable noun. |
| “There are many swimming pool.” | There are many swimming **pools**. | **Many + plural countable noun**. |
| “We have many chance.” | We have many **chances/opportunities**. | The intended meaning is countable occasions or opportunities. |
| “a lot of poem” | a lot of **poems** | Individual poems are countable; **poetry** is uncountable. |
| “from different area” | from different **areas** | Several areas; compare **from a different area** for one. |
| “one of my favourite exercise” | one of my favourite **exercises/sports** | You select one member from a plural group. |
| “There are three girls and one boys.” | There are three girls and one **boy**. | **One + singular countable noun**. |
| “Swimming is a very popular sports here.” | Swimming is a very popular **sport** here. | **A + singular countable noun**. |
| “Below forty degree.” | Below minus forty **degrees Celsius**, if −40 is intended. | Plural unit; **minus** changes the numerical meaning and must not be lost. |
| “three kilometer” — giving a distance | three **kilometres/kilometers** | Plural in a distance expression. |
| “for almost a third years” | for almost **thirty years**, if 30 is intended | **Thirty** is a cardinal number; **third** is an ordinal. No **a** before **thirty years**. The number may have been misheard. |
| “a pets” / “a children” | **a pet / pets; a child / children** | Do not combine **a** with these plural forms. |
| “They are holding a books.” | They are holding **books**. / They are each holding **a book**. | Choose a plural noun or a singular noun with **a**. |

**Added contrast:** **The park is three kilometres away** versus **It is a three-kilometre walk**. The unit stays singular inside this compound modifier.

### Person, people and peoples

Your records include **“Sixty person…”**, **“many peoples in my university”**, **“talk with some peoples”**, and **“peoples”** when referring to individual visitors.

Use **one person → two people → many people**. For the class example, **There were sixty students in my class** is more specific. Use **many people at my university** and **talk with some people**.

**People** can also refer to a population, and **peoples** to several populations, as in **Indigenous peoples**. Those meanings do not fit the ordinary counting of classmates or visitors. Similarly, the recorded excerpt **“a white people”** needs **a white person** if it means one individual; the noun **a people** has a different collective meaning.

### Nouns normally uncountable in your intended meanings

| Recorded wording or excerpt | Correction | Meaning distinction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “received a good news” / “it's a good news” | received **some good news** / **it's good news** | **News** is uncountable and takes singular agreement: **The news is good**. Also **a piece of news**. |
| “I had a work the whole day” | I **had work all day**. / I **worked the whole day**. | **Work** as employment or activity is uncountable. **A job / a piece of work** supplies a countable expression. |
| “Different modes of transports.” | different modes of **transport** | Uncountable for the general transport category. |
| “I drank a coconut water.” | I drank **some coconut water / a glass of coconut water**. | **Context-dependent:** **a coconut water** can mean one serving in an ordering context. |
| “Chinese cultures.” | **Chinese culture**, when referring to it as a whole | **Cultures** can be correct when distinguishing several cultures or cultural traditions. |
| “I have heard a lot of histories about American…” | I have learned a lot about **American history**. | For the school subject, use **history**. **Histories** can mean separate historical accounts; **American** modifies **history**. |
| “A sweet food.” | I like **sweet food / sweet foods**. | For a general preference, no **a**. **A sweet food** is grammatical when identifying one kind. |

**Added countability reference, not more recorded errors:** **advice, information, research, traffic, equipment, furniture, privacy** are normally uncountable in their ordinary general senses. Practise **some advice, a piece of information, research projects, heavy traffic, items of equipment, enough privacy**.

### Little, less, few and fewer

Recorded: **“There are less chance for me to meet her.”**

- **There is little chance of seeing her soon.** Low probability.
- **There is less chance of seeing her now.** Lower probability than before; a comparison is intended.
- **There are few opportunities to meet her.** Not many occasions.
- **There are fewer opportunities to meet her now.** Fewer occasions than before.

**Chance** can be uncountable probability or a countable opportunity. **Less chance** is not inherently wrong; the recorded sentence mixes the noun meaning, quantifier and agreement.

**Personal speaking check:** Before a noun, ask **one thing, several things, or an uncountable amount?** Then check the determiner, quantity word and ending together.

## 7. Articles, Determiners and Naming Conventions

### A singular countable noun usually needs a determiner

| Recorded wording | Correction | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “I don't have best friend.” | I don't have **a best friend**. | **Friend** is a singular countable noun. |
| “We use translator to communicate.” / “We have translator.” | We use **a translator / a translation app**. / We have **a translation app**. | Include a determiner; **translation software** is an uncountable alternative when a tool is intended. |
| “Today is today is holiday.” | Today is **a holiday**. | The article is the grammar point; the repetition is a separate fluency issue. **I have the day off** may better express a personal rest day. |
| “We just bought some snacks from supermarket.” | We just bought some snacks from **the supermarket**. | Use **the** for an identifiable shop; **a supermarket** also works if it is unspecified. |
| “…only big city have… job opportunity.” | **A big city has job opportunities**. / **Big cities have job opportunities**. | Singular and plural noun phrases require different forms. |

### A or an depends on the next sound

| Recorded wording or excerpt | Correction | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “It's a amazing experience.” | It's **an amazing experience**. | Vowel sound at the start of **amazing**. |
| “A insect can eat plastic.” | **An insect** can eat plastic. | Vowel sound. |
| “We have a environment problem.” | We have **an environmental problem**. | **An** plus the adjective **environmental**. |
| “She is on outgoing girl.” | She is **an outgoing person**. | **An** before **outgoing**. **On/an** may be a transcription problem. |
| “a honor” | **an honour / an honor** | The initial **h** is silent. |

**Added contrasts:** **a university** begins with /j/; **an hour** begins with a vowel sound. Use the sound of the next word: **an environment**, but **a supportive environment**.

### The, zero article and fixed place/time expressions

| Recorded wording or excerpt | Correction | Qualification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Internet is very slow today.” | **The internet** is very slow today. | Standard wording for this sentence. |
| “to USA” | to **the USA** | Learn the country name as a phrase. |

## 13. Linking Words, Clauses and Logical Relationships

### Because versus because of

Recorded: **“I didn't wake up earlier because of I went to bed so late yesterday.”**

Use **I didn't wake up early because I went to bed so late last night**. The cause after **because** is a complete clause: **I went to bed late**. **Earlier** can be correct if you are comparing wake-up times; it is not itself universally wrong.

**Added contrast:**

| Structure | Correct example |
| --- | --- |
| **Because + subject + verb** | I was exhausted **because we hadn't slept**. |
| **Because of + noun phrase** | I was exhausted **because of the overnight journey**. |
| **Because of + -ing phrase** | I was exhausted **because of travelling all night**. |
| **Although + subject + verb** | **Although we were tired**, we continued. |
| **Despite + noun/-ing phrase** | **Despite the tiredness / Despite feeling tired**, we continued. |

The **although/despite** examples extend the pattern for practice; they are not claims that you made those exact mistakes.

### Learn fixed connectors as complete phrases

Recorded **“As result, as the result”** → **As a result, …**.

**Added meaning contrasts:**

- **As a result** gives a consequence.
- **In the end** gives a final outcome.
- **Eventually** means after some time or difficulty.
- **Overall / to sum up** give an assessment or summary.
- **Because** gives a reason; **although** introduces a concession or contrast.

Choosing a connector is a meaning decision. An expression is not automatically suitable just because it sounds formal.

### Time clauses and lists

The recorded fragments **“why was young…”** and **“Why was young? We have a environment problem…”** may reflect transcription problems. If the intended meaning is a past period, use **When I was young, …**. **Why** asks for or introduces a reason; **when** introduces time. These are useful reconstructions, not verified exact quotations.

For the recorded country-list fragment **“…like Thailand In Indonesia”**, **such as Thailand and Indonesia** expresses a list. Do not infer a separate rule error from every unclear word in the fragment.

**Added practice caution:** Use **Although X, Y** or **X, but Y** in ordinary standard English, rather than combining both into **Although X, but Y**. Likewise, distinguish **Because X, Y** from **X, so Y**. These are new practice examples, not additional recorded mistakes.

**Personal speaking check:** Decide the relationship first: **time, cause, result, contrast, addition, example or summary**. Then choose the expression and its following structure.

### B. Change the Meaning Without Breaking the Structure

Use these new prompts for spoken practice. They test whether you can transfer the patterns rather than only recognise a correction.

| Task | Example answer |
| --- | --- |
| Describe a first visit, then a current routine. | I **first visited** the park in [year]. I **usually go** there on Sundays. |
| Describe a friendship that continues. | We **have been close friends since high school**. |
| Put an earlier event before a past phone call. | I **had already bought a ticket when the teacher called**. |
| Turn a cause clause into a noun phrase. | I was tired **because we hadn't slept** → I was tired **because of the lack of sleep**. |
| Change permission into encouragement. | She **let me try** → She **encouraged me to try**. |
| Change a countable opportunity into probability. | We have **few opportunities to meet** → There is **little chance of meeting soon**. |
| Turn an active photo sentence into a passive one. | Someone **took the photo at the school gate** → The photo **was taken at the school gate**. |
| Express both the person’s feeling and the cause. | I felt **embarrassed** because it was an **embarrassing situation**. |

## A Short Review Routine

1. **Choose three patterns**, such as past tense, plural nouns and **let + base verb**.
2. **Read the corrected examples aloud**, then change one detail: the person, place, activity or time.
3. **Record a one-minute answer from keywords.** Do not read a polished script for this check.
4. **Listen for those three patterns first.** Record actual errors from the new attempt, including the full intended sentence.
5. **Retell the answer once**, using the corrected structures. In a later session, try the same patterns on a different topic.

**Final speaking check:** **Time → subject → complete verb phrase → verb complement → noun phrase → preposition → connection.** You do not need to run a long checklist after every word; use it to locate a problem during review and make one pattern more automatic at a time.
