# IELTS Speaking Part 1 — Questions and Flexible Templates for Band 7–9 Practice

**Privacy note:** Personal and third-party details have been removed or generalised. First-person stories and altered quotations are anonymised study examples, not a biographical record or verbatim transcript.

**Part 1 is a short interview about you and familiar, everyday topics.** It lasts about **4–5 minutes**. You may be asked about your work or studies, home, interests, habits, and experiences. There is no cue card or one-minute preparation period in this part.

**Added study material:** The questions, frameworks, and model answers below are original practice material, not official test questions, a forecast of upcoming topics, or quotations from your teacher. Details in the model answers are illustrative; adapt them to your life.

These materials support preparation for **Bands 7–9**, but a written answer cannot establish a speaking score. IELTS assesses **fluency and coherence, lexical resource, grammatical range and accuracy, and pronunciation**, with equal weighting, across the Speaking test.

## How to Use These Templates

- Replace **`[brackets]`** with your own details.
- With **`A / B / C`**, choose **one** option. Do not say every alternative.
- Alternatives can express different attitudes or levels of certainty. Choose the meaning that fits you and complete the sentence with the right grammar.
- Start with a direct answer, then add a useful reason, detail, or small example.
- **Two to four sentences** can be a useful starting point for practice, but this is **not an IELTS rule**. A simple factual question may need less; an explanatory question may need more. There is no prescribed word count or fixed time per answer.

**Basic pattern: Direct answer → Reason or detail → Optional example or qualification.**

Question: “Do you enjoy walking?”

> Yes, I do, especially in the evening. It helps me clear my head after work, and I like noticing things I'd miss if I were travelling by car.

This is enough to develop the answer naturally. You do not need a formal introduction or a concluding summary.

## 1. Describing Your Situation: Work, Studies, Home, or Hometown

**Practice questions:** Do you work or are you a student? What is your hometown like? What kind of home do you live in?

**Pattern:** Give the relevant fact → Add one distinctive detail.

### Flexible Answers

For work:

> **I work as / I'm currently working as** [a/an + job title, such as a designer]. My role mainly involves [tasks in the -ing form], and **what I enjoy most is / the most interesting part is** [aspect of the work].

For studies:

> **I'm studying / I'm currently taking a course in** [subject]. I chose it because [reason], and I'm particularly interested in [specific area].

For a place:

> **I live in / I'm originally from** [place]. It's [brief description], and one thing I particularly like about it is [feature].

**Model — “What is your hometown like?”**

> It's a fairly small city with a busy centre and some quieter residential areas. What I like most is that everyday places, like shops and parks, are quite easy to reach. It feels familiar without being completely quiet.

**Language note:** **I live in** identifies your current home; **I'm originally from** identifies your place of origin. Answer the location the examiner actually asks about. Use **work as + role** and **work in + field**: “I work as a designer” / “I work in design.”

## 2. Likes and Dislikes: Do You Enjoy It?

**Practice questions:** Do you like cooking? Do you enjoy shopping? Do you like rainy weather?

**Pattern:** Attitude → Reason → Optional qualification.

### Flexible Answers

Positive:

> **Yes, definitely / Yes, quite a lot / Yes, especially when I have time**. I find [activity in the -ing form] **relaxing / enjoyable / satisfying**, mainly because [reason]. I particularly like [specific aspect].

Negative or mixed:

> **Not particularly / Not really / Only occasionally**. [Explain your actual feeling or habit.] That said, I do enjoy [limited exception].

**Model — “Do you like cooking?”**

> Yes, especially when I have time. I find it satisfying to make something from scratch, even if it's only a simple meal. During a busy week, though, I usually stick to dishes I can make quickly.

**Useful alternatives:** **I'm quite keen on cooking / I enjoy trying new recipes / Cooking is something I find relaxing.** Use **keen on + noun or -ing form**. “Only occasionally” describes how often your enjoyment occurs, so explain the situation.

## 3. Preferences: Which Would You Choose?

**Practice questions:** Do you prefer mornings or evenings? Would you rather travel alone or with friends? Do you prefer reading on paper or on a screen?

**Pattern:** Choice → Main reason → Optional exception.

### Flexible Answers

> **I generally prefer / I tend to prefer** [A] because [reason]. [Add one practical or personal detail.] Having said that, [B] can be better when [specific situation].

Or:

> **I'd rather / Given the choice, I'd rather** [base verb phrase] than [base verb phrase]. The main reason is that [reason].

**Model — “Do you prefer mornings or evenings?”**

> I generally prefer evenings because I feel less rushed then. I can go for a walk or read without immediately having to get ready for work. Mornings are pleasant too, but only when I don't have to get up early.

**Grammar:** **Prefer A to B**: “I prefer paper books to e-books.” **Would rather do A than do B**: “I'd rather read on paper than read on a screen.” Avoid mixing the two patterns.

## 4. Frequency and Routine: How Often Do You Do It?

**Practice questions:** How often do you exercise? When do you usually listen to music? Do you often go to parks?

**Pattern:** Frequency or time → Typical circumstances → Brief variation or effect.

### Flexible Answer

> **Usually, / Most weeks, / As a rule,** I [activity and frequency]. I tend to do it [time, place, or company], because [reason]. If [change in circumstances], I [how the routine changes].

**Model — “How often do you go to parks?”**

> Usually once a week, often on a Sunday evening. I like going when I have enough time for a proper walk. If the weather is very hot, I tend to wait until later in the day or choose somewhere with plenty of shade.

**Frequency choices:** **almost every day / a couple of times a week / every few weeks / once in a while / hardly ever**. Choose a reasonably accurate description; exact numbers are not necessary.

## 5. Childhood or a Past Habit: What Did You Do Before?

**Practice questions:** Did you enjoy drawing as a child? What games did you play when you were younger? Did you often visit your grandparents?

**Pattern:** Past answer → One remembered detail → Optional present contrast.

### Flexible Answer

> **Yes, I used to / Yes, when I was younger, I used to** [base verb phrase]. I remember [an -ing phrase or a complete clause about a small detail]. These days, [current situation].

If the answer is no:

> **Not very often / Not really**. When I was a child, I was more interested in [alternative]. [Add a brief memory or explanation.]

**Model — “Did you enjoy drawing as a child?”**

> Yes, I used to draw quite a lot after school. I remember filling notebooks with pictures of animals, although they weren't particularly realistic. I don't draw much now, but I still enjoy looking at illustrations.

**Grammar:** **Used to + base verb** describes a past habit or state. For one specific event, use past simple: “I drew a picture for my teacher last Friday.”

## 6. Changes: Is It Different Now?

**Practice questions:** Have your reading habits changed? Has your hometown changed since you were a child? Do you spend your weekends differently now?

**Pattern:** State the change → Contrast past and present → Explain why.

### Flexible Answer

> **Yes, quite a bit / Yes, especially in the last few years / A little**. I used to [past habit], whereas now I [current habit]. That's mainly because [reason for the change].

If there has been little change:

> **Not very much / My habits have stayed fairly similar**. I've always [a continuing preference or habit], although [one small change].

**Model — “Have your reading habits changed?”**

> Yes, quite a bit. I used to read mainly novels, whereas now I also read articles related to work or topics I'm curious about. I still enjoy fiction, but I tend to read it at weekends when I can concentrate for longer.

**Useful contrast:** **I used to…, but now… / These days, I…, whereas in the past I… / I've gradually started to…**

## 7. Experience: Have You Ever Tried It?

**Practice questions:** Have you ever grown a plant? Have you ever learned a musical instrument? Have you ever visited a museum on your own?

**Pattern:** Yes or no → Brief experience or reason → Reaction or interest.

### Flexible Answers

If yes:

> **Yes, I have / Yes, a few times**. The first time was [finished past time], when I [specific event]. I found it [reaction], because [reason].

If no:

> **No, I haven't / Not yet**. [Give the reason or relevant context.] **I'd be interested in trying it / I don't think it would be my first choice**, because [honest reason].

**Model — “Have you ever grown a plant?”**

> Yes, I have. I grew some herbs on a windowsill last year because I wanted something I could use in cooking. I enjoyed seeing new leaves appear, although remembering to water them regularly took a bit of effort.

**Grammar:** Use present perfect for the experience, then past simple for a finished occasion: **“Yes, I have. I tried it last year.”** Avoid “I've tried it last year.”

## 8. Future Wishes and Plans: Would You Like to Do It?

**Practice questions:** Would you like to learn another language? Is there a hobby you would like to try? Do you plan to move home in the future?

**Pattern:** Intention or wish → Reason → Realistic detail or condition.

### Flexible Answer

> **Yes, I'd like to / Yes, I'm hoping to / Yes, I'm planning to** [base verb phrase]. It appeals to me because [reason]. [Add a possible first step or an arrangement, depending on how definite the plan is.]

If uncertain:

> **Possibly / I haven't decided yet**. I'd consider it if [condition], but at the moment [current priority or obstacle].

**Model — “Would you like to learn another language?”**

> Yes, I'd like to learn some basic Spanish. I'm interested in travelling to a Spanish-speaking country, and it would be useful to manage simple conversations myself. I'd probably start with a short course to see how I get on.

**Meaning:** **I'd like to** expresses a wish; **I'm hoping to** expresses a hope; **I'm planning to** suggests an actual intention. Do not describe a wish as a booked arrangement.

## 9. Reasons: Why Do You Like or Do Something?

**Practice questions:** Why do you enjoy your work? Why do you like your neighbourhood? Why is that hobby important to you?

**Pattern:** Main reason → Explain the personal benefit → Optional example.

### Flexible Answer

> **The main reason is that / I think it's mainly because** [complete clause]. It helps me [base verb phrase], especially when [situation]. For example, [one brief illustration, if useful].

**Model — “Why do you like your neighbourhood?”**

> The main reason is that most of the places I need are within walking distance. I can buy groceries or go for a walk without planning a journey. That makes ordinary days feel a bit less complicated.

**Keep the explanation specific:** Replace “because it's convenient” with what is convenient and how that affects you. A second reason is optional; one developed reason is often enough.

## 10. Ability and Difficulty: Are You Good at It?

**Practice questions:** Are you good at remembering names? Do you find it easy to get up early? Are you good at organising your time?

**Pattern:** Honest assessment → Example or explanation → Optional coping strategy.

### Flexible Answers

Positive:

> **I'm fairly good at / I've become quite good at** [activity in the -ing form]. [Explain what you do well.] I think [habit or experience] has helped.

Mixed or negative:

> **Not particularly / It depends on the situation**. I sometimes find it difficult to [base verb phrase], especially when [circumstance]. What helps is [strategy in the -ing form].

**Model — “Are you good at remembering names?”**

> Not particularly, especially if I'm introduced to several people at once. I usually remember their faces more easily. What helps is repeating someone's name during the conversation so I make a stronger connection between the name and the person.

**Useful alternatives:** **I'm still working on it / I find it fairly straightforward / I can manage it reasonably well / It takes me a little longer than I'd like.**

## Twelve Everyday Topics to Practise

Use the question patterns above with these topics. Each example illustrates one possible answer to the **first** question; it does not describe your personal circumstances unless the details happen to match.

### A. Work or Studies

**Questions:** What do you enjoy about your work or studies? Is there anything you find difficult?

> I enjoy learning how to solve practical problems. It's satisfying when something that seemed confusing at first starts to make sense. Working to a deadline can be stressful, but it also helps me focus.

**Useful language:** **solve practical problems / learn from colleagues / work on different projects / put ideas into practice**.

### B. Home and Accommodation

**Questions:** What is your favourite room at home? Is there anything you would like to change about your home?

> Probably the living room, because that's where I relax in the evening. It gets plenty of natural light during the day, and I have a comfortable chair by the window where I like to read.

**Useful language:** **plenty of natural light / a quiet corner / enough storage space / feel at home**.

### C. Hometown and Neighbourhood

**Questions:** What do you like about the area where you live? Has the area changed recently?

> I like how easy it is to get around on foot. There are shops nearby and a few quiet streets where I can go for a walk. That makes the area quite convenient for everyday life.

**Useful language:** **within walking distance / a residential area / good transport links / a strong sense of community**.

### D. Free Time and Weekends

**Questions:** What do you usually do at weekends? Would you like to have more free time?

> I usually keep part of the weekend free for a walk or a meal with someone close to me. I also need some time at home to recharge. I prefer having a loose plan to filling every hour.

**Useful language:** **catch up with friends / take things slowly / recharge / leave some time free**.

### E. Weather and Seasons

**Questions:** What kind of weather do you like? Does the weather affect your plans?

> I prefer cool, dry weather because it's comfortable for walking. When it's very hot, I find it harder to stay outdoors for long. A mild autumn day is probably ideal for me.

**Useful language:** **mild weather / a cool breeze / heavy rain / feel too humid / spend time outdoors**.

### F. Food and Cooking

**Questions:** Do you prefer eating at home or eating out? Did you learn to cook when you were younger?

> Most of the time, I prefer eating at home because I can decide exactly what goes into the meal. Eating out is a treat, though, especially when I want to try something I wouldn't know how to cook myself.

**Useful language:** **cook from scratch / try a new recipe / a home-cooked meal / grab something quick**.

### G. Music

**Questions:** When do you usually listen to music? Has your taste in music changed?

> Mostly when I'm travelling or doing simple jobs around the house. It makes routine tasks more enjoyable. If I need to concentrate on something difficult, though, I usually prefer silence or music without lyrics.

**Useful language:** **put on some music / music without lyrics / suit my mood / listen to an album all the way through**.

### H. Reading

**Questions:** What do you like reading? Do you prefer printed books or e-books?

> I read a mixture of fiction and short articles about things I'm interested in. Articles are easier to fit into a busy day, but I enjoy a novel when I have enough time to get absorbed in the story.

**Useful language:** **get absorbed in a story / browse articles / read a few pages / pick up a book**.

### I. Technology and Apps

**Questions:** What do you use your phone for most? Is there an app you would like to use less?

> Mainly for messages, maps and looking things up. I also use it to keep short notes, which is useful when an idea occurs to me while I'm out. I try to avoid checking it constantly when I'm with other people.

**Useful language:** **look something up / keep track of appointments / check notifications / limit screen time**.

### J. Transport

**Questions:** How do you usually travel around your city? Would you like to cycle more often?

> For short journeys, I usually walk. If I'm going further, I tend to use public transport because I don't have to think about parking. The main inconvenience is that it can get crowded at busy times.

**Useful language:** **use public transport / avoid rush hour / get stuck in traffic / a short bus ride**.

### K. Friends and Socialising

**Questions:** How do you keep in touch with your friends? Do you prefer meeting one friend or a large group?

> We mostly send messages during the week and try to meet when our schedules allow. I like messages for small updates, but a proper conversation over a meal usually feels more satisfying.

**Useful language:** **keep in touch / catch up over a meal / make time for each other / arrange a get-together**.

### L. Photographs

**Questions:** Do you like taking photographs? Do you prefer keeping photos on your phone or printing them?

> Yes, particularly when I'm spending time with friends or visiting somewhere interesting. I like having a record of small moments, although I try to put my phone away once I've taken a few pictures so I can enjoy what's happening.

**Useful language:** **capture a moment / keep a record of something / look back at old photos / take a candid picture**.

## Expressions for Natural, Flexible Answers

| Purpose | Alternatives |
| --- | --- |
| A positive answer | **Yes, definitely / Yes, quite often / Yes, especially when…** — choose wording that matches the question. |
| A qualified answer | **To some extent / Sometimes, depending on… / Only when…** |
| A negative answer | **Not really / Not particularly / Hardly ever** — **hardly ever** refers to frequency. |
| A preference | **I tend to prefer… / Given the choice, I'd choose… / I'm more interested in…** |
| A reason | **It's mainly because… / The main reason is that… / What I like about it is…** |
| A small example | **For example, last weekend… / I often… / One thing I usually do is…** |
| An exception | **Although sometimes… / That said,… / Unless I'm particularly busy,…** |
| An approximate answer | **Roughly once a week / Usually, but not always / It varies a little** |
| A brief thinking pause | **Let me think for a moment / I haven't thought about that much, but…** |

Use thinking phrases only when you actually need a moment. Starting every answer with the same phrase can make your speech sound rehearsed.

**Pronunciation practice:** Choose a few words that carry the main meaning and stress them naturally. For example: “I usually prefer **evenings**, because I feel **less rushed**.” Use pauses at meaningful boundaries; you do not need to imitate a particular national accent.

## Improving an Answer Without Making It Much Longer

| Starting answer | More informative answer |
| --- | --- |
| “Yes, I like music.” | “Yes, especially when I'm travelling. It makes the journey feel shorter, and I enjoy choosing something that matches my mood.” |
| “My hometown is beautiful.” | “It's a small city with a river running through the centre. I particularly like the paths beside the water because they're pleasant places to walk.” |
| “I don't know. I don't cook.” | “I don't cook very often, mainly because I get home late. When I do, I usually make something simple like noodles or an omelette.” |

The improvement comes from **specific, relevant information**, not simply from adding more words. You do not need an idiom, a complex sentence, or several linking phrases in every answer.

## Common Grammar and Usage Checks

| Avoid | Use |
| --- | --- |
| “I very like reading.” | **I really like reading. / I enjoy reading a lot.** |
| “I prefer tea than coffee.” | **I prefer tea to coffee. / I'd rather have tea than coffee.** |
| “I am living here since [year].” | **I've lived here since [year]. / I've been living here since [year].** |
| “I am used to go there as a child.” | **I used to go there as a child.** For present familiarity: **I'm used to going there.** |
| “I have visited it last year.” | **I visited it last year.** |
| “I go there two times in a week.” | **I go there twice a week.** |
| “It depends of the weather.” | **It depends on the weather.** |
| “I enjoy to cook.” | **I enjoy cooking.** |

## Review Practice

### Round 1: Answer from Your Own Life

Choose six questions from different topics. Write only **three or four keywords** for each, then answer aloud. Develop each answer enough to explain yourself without turning it into a long speech.

### Round 2: Change the Wording

Answer the same questions again. Choose different `/` options, change one detail, and keep the meaning accurate. The goal is to express the idea comfortably in more than one way.

### Round 3: Handle a Follow-up

After each answer, ask yourself one relevant follow-up:

- **Why is that?**
- **Has that always been the case?**
- **Would you like that to change?**

Respond to the new question directly. Do not repeat the entire first answer.

### Self-Check

1. Did I answer the exact question immediately?
2. Did I give a reason or useful detail instead of stopping at yes or no?
3. Did I use the right time frame: now, childhood, a finished event, or a future possibility?
4. Did my vocabulary sound natural and fit what I meant?
5. Was my speech clear, with manageable pauses and complete sentences?

### Quick Grammar Exercise

Correct these sentences:

1. I prefer walking than taking a bus.
2. I've started learning English last year.
3. I enjoy to spend time outdoors.
4. It depends of how busy I am.

**Answers:**

1. **I prefer walking to taking a bus.**
2. **I started learning English last year.**
3. **I enjoy spending time outdoors.**
4. **It depends on how busy I am.**
