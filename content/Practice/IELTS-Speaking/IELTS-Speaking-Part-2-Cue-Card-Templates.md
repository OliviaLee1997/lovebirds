# IELTS Speaking Part 2 — Flexible Cue Card Templates for Band 7–9 Practice

**Privacy note:** Personal and third-party details have been removed or generalised. First-person stories and altered quotations are anonymised study examples, not a biographical record or verbatim transcript.

**Use these frameworks to organise your own answers and practise flexible, natural English.** They are designed to support preparation for Bands 7–9; a template alone cannot demonstrate or guarantee a score. Your Speaking score reflects your performance across the test, including your pronunciation and delivery.

**Added study material:** All practice cue cards, templates, examples, and exercises below were created for study. They are not official test questions, examiner-scored answers, or quotations from your teacher. The topic groups are useful recurring preparation categories, not a prediction of upcoming questions or a frequency ranking.

## How to Use the Templates

**Why “Part 2”?** The IELTS Speaking test has three parts: **Part 1** is an interview about familiar topics; **Part 2** is an individual long turn based on a cue card; **Part 3** is a discussion of broader issues connected to the Part 2 topic. These templates focus on the cue-card talk in the second part.

- **`[Brackets]`** mark details you supply. Replace them with specific information, usually a phrase or one or two sentences.
- **`A / B / C`** offers alternatives. Choose **one** option each time; do not read every option aloud. Alternatives may express different meanings, so choose the one that fits your experience.
- **Bold expressions** are useful chunks to practise. You do not need to use every one.
- Keep the cue card's exact focus. A story about a helpful colleague needs a different emphasis from a description of a famous person.
- These are **core frameworks**, not fixed two-minute scripts. Develop the main example with what happened, what you noticed, and how you reacted. Time your completed version rather than counting the words in a template.

**Example of choosing alternatives**

Template: **What I appreciate most is / The main reason I like it is** [reason].

Spoken version: “What I appreciate most is how quiet it becomes in the early evening.”

### What Higher-Band Speaking Involves

IELTS assesses **fluency and coherence, lexical resource, grammatical range and accuracy, and pronunciation**, with equal weighting. The following are practical paraphrases of the progression in the official descriptors, not separate scoring rules for these templates.

| Target | What to work towards |
| --- | --- |
| **Band 7** | Sustain and organise an answer; paraphrase when needed; use vocabulary flexibly and produce frequent error-free sentences, including some complex structures; remain easy to understand. |
| **Band 8** | Develop ideas coherently; choose words precisely and flexibly; use a wide range of structures with most sentences error-free; speak with fluent, easily understood delivery. |
| **Band 9** | Develop the topic fully and appropriately with effortless fluency, precise and flexible language, consistently accurate grammar apart from natural slips, and pronunciation that is effortless to understand. |

**There is no “Band 9 phrase” that automatically raises an answer to Band 9.** Precise everyday English, relevant detail, and flexible delivery matter more than inserting rare words or memorised idioms.

## A Flexible Structure for Almost Any Cue Card

**Direct answer → Background → Specific example or scene → Meaning or reflection**

| Stage | What to include | Useful language |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Direct answer** | Identify the person, place, thing, or experience immediately. | **I'd like to talk about… / The first thing that comes to mind is… / One example that stands out is…** |
| **Background** | Explain the relevant who, where, when, or how. | **I first came across it… / At the time, I was… / I've known them since…** |
| **Development** | Give an observable detail or tell one small story. | **What stood out was… / I remember one occasion when… / To give you an example,…** |
| **Reflection** | Explain why it matters, what changed, or what you would do next. | **Looking back,… / Since then,… / That's why it has stayed with me.** |

For a description, develop a **scene or characteristic**. For an event, develop a **sequence of actions**. For a future plan, develop **what you would do and why**. Not every answer needs a problem or a life lesson.

### Your One-Minute Preparation

You have **one minute to prepare**, then speak for **one to two minutes**. The examiner stops you when the speaking time is up; you do not need to rush to finish a prepared ending.

Suggested preparation routine:

1. **First 10 seconds:** Choose an example that fits the exact question and gives you enough to describe.
2. **Next 20 seconds:** Note a few keywords for the cue card's prompts.
3. **Next 20 seconds:** Add one concrete scene, incident, or example.
4. **Final 10 seconds:** Note why it matters and check the main tense.

Write keywords rather than a script. For example:

> the park — the city — [year] — some evenings — waterways, birds — boat ride — quieter surroundings — relax

## 1. A Person: Someone You Admire or Someone Who Helped You

**Related practice topics:** A patient person, a helpful colleague, an inspiring teacher, a creative friend, or someone you enjoy talking to.

**Practice cue card:** Describe a person you admire. Say who they are, how you know them, what they are like, and explain why you admire them.

**Structure:** Identity → Relationship → Characteristic → Evidence → Influence.

### Flexible Answer

> **I'd like to talk about / The first person who comes to mind is** [name], who is [relationship or role]. I've known them for [length of time], and we first met [where and how].
>
> What I particularly **admire / appreciate / respect** about them is [quality]. They tend to [observable behaviour], even when [a difficult situation]. **To give you an example, / I remember one occasion when** [brief situation]. At the time, I was [your circumstances], and they [specific action].
>
> **What impressed me most was / What really stayed with me was** the way they [detail]. That made me feel [emotion], because [reason].
>
> Since then, I've tried to [something you learned from them]. We [how you interact now], and I still value [part of the relationship]. **That's probably why I look up to them / That's what makes them such an important person in my life**.

**Choose qualities you can demonstrate:**

- **patient / considerate / dependable** — illustrate how the person treats others.
- **resourceful / level-headed / good at finding practical solutions** — illustrate how they handle a problem.
- **They put people at ease / They explain things clearly / They keep their promises.**

**Adaptation:** For a famous person you have never met, replace the meeting history with **“I first heard about them through…”** and describe something you actually know about their work. For “someone who helped you,” make the help and its result the centre of the answer.

## 2. A Place: Somewhere Peaceful or Somewhere You Enjoy Visiting

**Related practice topics:** A park, a quiet café, a beautiful outdoor place, a part of your hometown, or somewhere you take visitors.

**Practice cue card:** Describe a place where you like to relax. Say where it is, when you go there, what you do there, and explain why it helps you relax.

**Structure:** Location → Discovery → Atmosphere → Typical visit → Personal appeal.

### Flexible Answer

> **One place I really enjoy visiting is / A place that immediately comes to mind is** [place], which is [location]. I first went there [when and why], and I've been back [how often] since then.
>
> **The first thing you notice is / What stands out when you arrive is** [specific feature]. There are [visible details], and you can usually hear [sound]. The atmosphere is **peaceful / lively / welcoming**, especially [time or condition].
>
> I normally go there [when and with whom]. We tend to [activity], although sometimes we simply [another activity]. One visit I remember particularly well was [occasion]. [Add what you saw or did and how you felt.]
>
> **What I appreciate most is / The main reason I keep going back is** [specific reason]. Whenever I've had [type of day or week], spending time there helps me [effect]. For me, it's somewhere I can **switch off / slow down / clear my head**.

**Useful detail choices:** **tree-lined paths / small independent cafés / open views across the water / the sound of birds / warm lighting / a sheltered courtyard**.

**Adaptation:** A café answer may need food, service, or layout; a historic building needs something about its appearance and history. For a place you have **not visited**, use Template 12 and avoid inventing past visits.

## 3. An Object: A Useful Possession or a Meaningful Gift

**Related practice topics:** Something you use often, a gift you received, an item you have kept for years, or a purchase you are pleased with.

**Practice cue card:** Describe an object that is important to you. Say what it is, how you got it, how you use it, and explain why it is important.

**Structure:** Identify → Acquisition → Features and use → Specific example → Value.

### Flexible Answer

> **I'd like to describe / One possession that means a lot to me is** [object]. I've had it for [length of time]. [I bought it when… / It was given to me by… / I've kept it since…]
>
> In terms of appearance, it's [size, colour, or material], with [distinctive feature]. **What makes it useful is / One feature I particularly like is** [feature]. I mainly use it for [purpose], usually [when or where].
>
> A good example of its value was [occasion]. I needed to [task], and it helped me [specific result]. [Add a practical detail about how you used it.]
>
> It does have one drawback: [small limitation]. Even so, I **use it regularly / take good care of it / wouldn't want to replace it**. **Its main value to me is / The reason I've kept it is** [practical or emotional reason]. Whenever I [use or see it], it [effect or association].

**Choose the right kind of value:**

- Practical: **It saves me time / It makes everyday tasks easier / It's reliable and easy to use.**
- Emotional: **It has sentimental value / It reminds me of someone important / It brings back memories of…**

**Adaptation:** For an object you mainly display or keep, replace “how I use it” with where you keep it and when you look at it. For a gift you **gave**, explain why you chose it and how the recipient reacted.

## 4. A Photograph: A Picture That Matters to You

**Related practice topics:** A favourite photograph, a picture of friends or family, a photograph you took, or a picture from a memorable day.

**Practice cue card:** Describe a photograph you like. Say what it shows, when and where it was taken, who took it, and explain why you like it.

**Structure:** Identify → Describe the image → Reconstruct the moment → Explain its meaning now.

### Flexible Answer

> **One of my favourite photographs is / A photograph I'm particularly fond of is** a picture of [subject]. It was taken [when and where] by [person], and I keep it [where].
>
> In the picture, [main visual detail]. In the background, you can see [setting]. **What I like about it is / What gives it its character is** [expression, composition, or small detail].
>
> I remember that day quite clearly. We had [earlier event], and we were [activity] when the photo was taken. Just before that, [small incident]. **That explains why / That's probably why** [something visible in the image].
>
> Whenever I look at it, it **brings back memories of / reminds me of** [time, relationship, or feeling]. Since then, [relevant change], so the photo has become [personal significance]. **The reason I treasure it is / What makes it special to me is** the way it captures [specific aspect of that moment].

**Useful expressions:** **a candid photo / a slightly blurry picture / a natural smile / everyone crowded together / a moment I'd almost forgotten**.

**Tense pattern:** **The photo shows…** describes the image now. **We were standing…** reconstructs the past scene. **I've kept it…** connects the past to the present.

## 5. An Enjoyable Experience: A Trip, Celebration, or Memorable Day

**Related practice topics:** A short journey, a celebration, a special meal, an enjoyable day out, or a time you received good news.

**Practice cue card:** Describe an enjoyable experience you had. Say when and where it happened, who was with you, what you did, and explain why you enjoyed it.

**Structure:** Set the scene → Expectations → Main events → Highlight → Feeling afterwards.

### Flexible Answer

> **An experience I remember fondly is / One occasion that really stands out is** [experience]. It happened [when and where], and I was with [people]. We'd decided to [plan] because [reason].
>
> Beforehand, I felt **excited / curious / a little unsure what to expect**. When we arrived, [first impression]. We started by [first activity], and then [next event].
>
> **The highlight was / The part I enjoyed most was** [specific moment]. I remember [sensory detail or someone's reaction], which made the whole experience feel [description]. [Add what you did next and how the other people responded.]
>
> By the end, I felt **refreshed / delighted / pleasantly tired**. What made it especially enjoyable was [personal reason], particularly because [background context]. **I'd happily do it again / It's something I'd love to experience again**, although next time I'd [small change]. Even now, [detail] still makes me smile.

**Adaptation:** For good news, focus on **how you heard it → what it meant → your reaction → what happened next**. For a meal, include the food, the occasion, and the company. Keep the requested experience central.

## 6. A Difficulty: A Problem You Solved or a Challenge You Faced

**Related practice topics:** A difficult task, a mistake, a time you were late, a stressful situation, or a problem with a product or service.

**Practice cue card:** Describe a difficult situation you dealt with. Say what happened, why it was difficult, what you did, and explain how you felt afterwards.

**Structure:** Problem → Stakes → Response → Result → Reflection.

### Flexible Answer

> **I'd like to talk about a time when / One challenging situation I remember was when** [problem]. This happened [when], while I was [background activity].
>
> The main difficulty was [obstacle]. I had expected [expectation], but [what actually happened]. At first, I felt **overwhelmed / frustrated / unsure how to handle it**, mainly because [reason].
>
> My first step was to [action]. When that [worked or failed], I decided to [next action]. **What helped most was / What finally made a difference was** [specific help, a useful discovery, or an action]. [Explain exactly how this changed the situation.]
>
> Eventually, [result]. It wasn't [an unrealistic perfect outcome], but [what you achieved]. Afterwards, I felt **relieved / more confident / proud that I'd kept going**.
>
> **Looking back, / With hindsight,** I would [what you would do differently]. The experience taught me [specific lesson], and since then I've [a real change in behaviour].

**Useful expressions:** **break the task down into smaller steps / ask for clarification / work out what had gone wrong / stay calm under pressure / find a practical solution**.

**Adaptation:** If the cue card is about bad service, explain what went wrong and how you responded. Do not force a successful solution if the problem remained unresolved; explain the actual outcome and your reaction.

## 7. A Decision: A Choice You Made or Advice That Helped You

**Related practice topics:** An important decision, useful advice, a change of plan, or a conversation that influenced you.

**Practice cue card:** Describe an important decision you made. Say what it was, when you made it, how you made it, and explain why it was important.

**Structure:** Choice → Options → Considerations and advice → Decision → Consequences.

### Flexible Answer

> **An important decision I made was to / A decision that had a real impact on me was to** [action]. I made it [when], at a point when [background situation].
>
> I was choosing between [option A] and [option B]. The first option appealed to me because [advantage], while the second offered [different advantage]. **What made the choice difficult was / My main concern was** [uncertainty or trade-off].
>
> To help me decide, I [research, reflection, or conversation]. [Person] suggested that I [advice], which helped me **see things more clearly / focus on what mattered most / look at the situation differently**.
>
> In the end, I chose to [decision], mainly because [decisive reason]. At first, [initial consequence], but over time [later outcome].
>
> **Looking back, I'm glad I made that choice / I still have mixed feelings about that decision** because [honest evaluation]. It changed [part of your life], and it has influenced how I [make similar choices now].

**Useful expressions:** **weigh up the options / think about the long-term consequences / take someone's advice / make up my mind / reconsider my priorities**.

**Adaptation:** For an advice cue card, start with **“A piece of advice that helped me was…”**, identify who gave it and in what situation, and explain how you acted on it. Advice is uncountable: **some advice / a piece of advice**, not “an advice.”

## 8. A Skill: Something You Learned

**Related practice topics:** A practical skill, something difficult you learned, something a person taught you, or an achievement you are proud of.

**Practice cue card:** Describe a useful skill you learned. Say what it is, when you learned it, how you learned it, and explain why it is useful.

**Structure:** Skill → Motivation → Learning process → Breakthrough → Present value.

### Flexible Answer

> **A useful skill I've learned is / One skill I've spent time developing is** [skill]. I started learning it [when], because I wanted to [motivation]. Before that, I could [starting level], but I struggled with [specific limitation].
>
> I began by [first learning method], and later I [next step]. The hardest part was [specific difficulty]. At first, I kept [repeated mistake], which was **frustrating / discouraging / harder to fix than I'd expected**.
>
> **I started to improve when / I began to make progress when** [explain what changed in your practice or what feedback you received]. I remember the first time I managed to [milestone]. [Explain the situation and why this was progress.]
>
> These days, I use the skill to [real application]. I'm still working on [remaining weakness], but I can now [specific ability]. **It has made me more independent / It has given me more confidence / It has made everyday tasks easier**, especially when [situation].

**Useful expressions:** **get the hang of it / learn through trial and error / put what I'd learned into practice / make steady progress / build up my confidence**.

**Adaptation:** For something you **want to learn**, describe your current starting point and a realistic learning plan using Template 12. Do not present hoped-for achievements as completed events.

## 9. An Activity: A Hobby or a Helpful Routine

**Related practice topics:** Something you do to stay healthy, a hobby, an outdoor activity, an activity with friends, or a habit that helps you relax.

**Practice cue card:** Describe an activity you do in your free time. Say what it is, when and where you do it, who you do it with, and explain why you enjoy it.

**Structure:** Activity → How it began → Typical session → Specific example → Benefit.

### Flexible Answer

> **One activity I really enjoy is / Something I regularly make time for is** [activity]. I usually do it [frequency and place], either [alone] or with [people].
>
> I first got into it [when and how]. Initially, I wanted to [original reason], but over time I began to appreciate [additional benefit]. A typical session involves [what you actually do], and it normally lasts [duration].
>
> **What I enjoy most is / The most satisfying part is** [specific aspect]. For example, last [day or period], I [particular instance]. [Describe one detail that made that session enjoyable or useful.]
>
> I sometimes find it difficult to [obstacle], especially when [circumstance]. To make it easier, I [practical adjustment]. It's become **a regular part of my week / something I look forward to / a useful way to unwind**. Afterwards, I usually feel [effect], which is why I've kept it up.

**Useful expressions:** **fit it into my schedule / stick to a routine / take my mind off work / spend quality time with friends / feel a sense of progress**.

**Adaptation:** For an activity you tried only once, use past tenses and describe that occasion. Avoid claiming an ongoing routine just because the template contains one.

## 10. A Book, Film, Series, or Song: Something That Made an Impression

**Related practice topics:** A film you enjoyed, a book you recommend, a memorable story, a TV programme, or a song you like.

**Practice cue card:** Describe a film you enjoyed. Say what it is, when you watched it, what it is about, and explain why you enjoyed it.

**Structure:** Identify → Discovery → Brief content → Memorable detail → Personal response.

### Flexible Answer — Film Version

> **A film I really enjoyed was / One film that has stayed with me is** [title]. I first watched it [when and where], after [how you heard about it]. It's a [genre] about [brief premise].
>
> Without going into the whole plot, the story follows [main character], who [central situation]. **What drew me in was / What I found particularly engaging was** [character, idea, or storytelling feature].
>
> There's one scene in which [specific moment]. I remember feeling **moved / amused / uneasy / completely absorbed**, because [personal reason]. [Explain the detail that produced this reaction.]
>
> I also liked [second feature], although I found [minor reservation]. The film made me think about [theme], especially in relation to [your own experience or viewpoint]. **I'd recommend it to anyone who enjoys / I think it would appeal to people who like** [type of story], and I'd happily watch it again.

**Change the content language to match the medium:**

| Medium | Natural choices |
| --- | --- |
| Book | **read it / the author / a chapter / the writing style / the narrator** |
| Film | **watched it / the director / a scene / the performances / the cinematography** |
| Series | **started watching it / an episode / a character / how the story develops** |
| Song | **first heard it / the singer / a line in the lyrics / the melody / the chorus** |

**Adaptation:** For a song, replace the plot paragraph with what the lyrics express and how the music sounds. For a documentary or non-fiction book, explain its subject and an interesting idea rather than inventing a fictional plot. Keep the summary brief enough to develop your own response.

## 11. Technology: An App, Website, or Device You Find Useful

**Related practice topics:** A useful website, an app you use often, technology that saves time, or a device that helps with work or study.

**Practice cue card:** Describe an app you find useful. Say what it is, how you found out about it, how you use it, and explain why it is useful.

**Structure:** Identify → Need → Normal use → Concrete result → Balanced evaluation.

### Flexible Answer

> **An app I find particularly useful is / One app I use regularly is** [name]. I first heard about it [how and when], while I was looking for a way to [need].
>
> It allows users to [main function]. I mainly use it for [personal use], usually [frequency or context]. **The feature I rely on most is / What I particularly like is** [feature], because [practical benefit].
>
> For example, recently I needed to [specific task]. I used the app to [action], which helped me [result]. Previously, I would have had to [older method], so this was **much more convenient / easier to manage / less time-consuming**.
>
> One limitation is [drawback], so I [how you deal with it]. Even with that limitation, it has become [role in your routine]. **The main reason I value it is / What keeps me using it is** [specific benefit], especially when [situation where that benefit matters].

**Useful expressions:** **keep track of my progress / organise my notes / set reminders / look up unfamiliar words / access information quickly / avoid repetitive work**.

**Adaptation:** Change **app** to **website** or the name of the device as needed. For a device, add a physical feature if relevant. A claim such as “It saves me time” becomes stronger when you explain which task takes less time and how.

## 12. A Future Plan: Somewhere You Want to Go or Something You Want to Try

**Related practice topics:** A place you want to visit, a skill you want to learn, an activity you want to try, a future trip, or a personal ambition.

**Practice cue card:** Describe something you would like to do in the future. Say what it is, when and where you would like to do it, how you would prepare, and explain why you want to do it.

**Structure:** Goal → Origin of interest → Concrete plan → Obstacles and preparation → Expected value.

### Flexible Answer

> **Something I'd really like to do is / One thing I hope to do in the future is** [base verb phrase, such as learn to swim]. I've been interested in it since [when or what sparked your interest], although I haven't had the chance to [action] yet.
>
> Ideally, I'd do it [when and where], possibly with [person]. The main reason it appeals to me is [motivation]. **I'm especially interested in / What I'm most curious about is** [specific aspect].
>
> If I had the opportunity, I'd start by [first step]. After that, I'd [next step]. [Add a concrete detail about what you would do, see, or practise.]
>
> The biggest obstacle at the moment is [time, cost, access, or confidence]. To make it more realistic, I **could / plan to** [preparation that matches your actual level of commitment].
>
> I imagine it would be **rewarding / challenging / refreshing**, because [reason]. Even if [possible difficulty], I think I'd still value the chance to [specific benefit].

**Keep your level of certainty accurate:**

- A possibility: **I'd like to… / I might… / If I had the chance, I'd…**
- An intention: **I'm planning to… / I intend to… / I'm hoping to…**
- A confirmed arrangement: **I've booked… / I'm meeting… / I'm travelling…** — use these only when something is actually arranged.

**Adaptation:** For a place, identify where it is and what you would do there. For a skill, explain how you would learn it and how you would use it afterwards.

## Expressions You Can Reuse Across Topics

These alternatives perform similar functions, but their meanings and grammatical patterns are not always identical. Learn each one with a complete example and choose what fits your message.

| Function | Alternatives with `/` |
| --- | --- |
| Introduce your choice | **I'd like to talk about… / One example that comes to mind is… / Something I remember particularly well is…** |
| Give background | **At the time, I was… / This happened when I was… / To give you a bit of background, I was…** |
| Explain how you discovered something | **I first heard about it through… / I came across it while… / A friend introduced me to it when…** |
| Highlight a feature | **What stands out is… / What I particularly like is… / The thing I notice most is…** |
| Give evidence | **For example,… / One occasion I remember was when… / A good example of this was…** |
| Explain a reason | **The main reason is that… / That's largely because… / What appeals to me is the fact that…** |
| Continue a story | **After that,… / A little later,… / By the time we arrived,…** |
| Introduce a surprise | **To my surprise,… / What I hadn't expected was… / As it turned out,…** |
| Add a qualification | **That said,… / Although I enjoyed it, … / The only drawback was…** |
| Paraphrase naturally | **What I mean is… / More specifically,… / It's a kind of…** |
| Describe a continuing effect | **Since then, I've… / It has helped me… / I still find myself thinking about…** |
| Reflect or finish | **Looking back,… / What has stayed with me is… / That's why it means so much to me.** |

**Grammar reminders:** **Through + noun phrase** (“through a friend”); **while + clause or -ing form** (“while I was browsing” / “while browsing”); **although + clause** (“Although I enjoyed it, it was expensive”). After **since then**, present perfect often fits a change continuing to now.

**Use connectors when the relationship needs them.** A personal story usually sounds more natural with **“The part I remember best…”** than a sequence of **“Firstly… Secondly… Furthermore… In conclusion…”**.

### Feelings: Choose the Emotion, Then Explain It

| Situation | Natural options | Developed example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A quiet place or relaxing activity | **calm / at ease / refreshed** | “I felt at ease because there was no pressure to do anything in particular.” |
| A successful challenge | **relieved / proud of myself / more confident** | “I was relieved that I'd finished on time, especially after such a difficult start.” |
| Help or a meaningful gift | **grateful / touched / appreciated** | “I was touched that she'd remembered something I'd mentioned months earlier.” |
| An interesting story or discovery | **curious / fascinated / absorbed** | “I was so absorbed in the story that I lost track of time.” |
| An unpleasant experience | **disappointed / frustrated / uncomfortable** | “I felt frustrated because I couldn't get a clear explanation of what had gone wrong.” |

**Meaning matters:** **Relieved** means a worry has eased. **Proud** relates to achievement or someone you value. **Touched** means emotionally moved. They are not interchangeable replacements for “happy.” **I felt appreciated** means I felt that someone valued me; **I appreciated the gift** means I valued the gift.

## How to Develop an Answer Beyond General Statements

Use **point → specific detail → personal effect** when an answer feels too short. These are development examples, not scored Band 7/8/9 comparisons.

| Starting point | A more developed version |
| --- | --- |
| “She's very helpful.” | “When I was struggling with a presentation, she helped me reorganise the main points and stayed to listen while I practised. I felt much more prepared the next morning.” |
| “The park is beautiful.” | “There are narrow paths beside the water, with trees hanging over the banks. I usually slow down there because I keep stopping to look at the reflections.” |
| “The app is convenient.” | “It lets me save unfamiliar words while I'm reading, so I can review them later without interrupting myself for too long.” |
| “It was unforgettable.” | “I still remember everyone cheering when I finally finished. I'd been close to giving up, so that reaction meant a lot to me.” |

### Grammar Choices That Serve the Story

- **Relative clause:** “It's a place **where I can relax without having to plan anything**.”
- **Past continuous + past simple:** “We **were walking** beside the water when we **noticed** a small bird.”
- **Past perfect for an earlier past event:** “I was nervous because I **had never spoken** to such a large group before.”
- **Concession:** “**Although it was more expensive than I'd expected**, I was pleased with the quality.”
- **Past-to-present connection:** “I first went there in [year], and **I've visited it regularly since then**.”
- **Hypothetical reflection:** “**If I had another chance, I'd allow more time** for the journey.”

Use a structure because it expresses your meaning. A few accurate, well-chosen complex sentences are more useful than forcing a complicated sentence into every line.

## Complete Worked Example — A Peaceful Place: the wetland park

**Practice cue card:** Describe a peaceful place you like to visit. Say where it is, when you first went there, what you do there, and explain why you find it peaceful.

**Practice note:** This example uses generalised details. Locations, dates, relationships and routines are illustrative rather than a personal record.

> A peaceful place I really enjoy visiting is the wetland park in the city. It's a convenient distance from home, so it's somewhere I can go without having to plan a whole day around it. I first visited in [year], when I started working in the city, and it's become one of my favourite places to unwind.
>
> I usually go there on some evenings with my friend. There are waterways, trees and traditional villages, and we often spend time walking around or watching birds. Sometimes we rent a boat, which gives us a different view of the wetland.
>
> What I particularly like is the change of pace. During the week, I spend quite a lot of time thinking about what I need to do next. When I'm there, I find myself paying attention to smaller things, like birds moving between the trees or reflections on the water. Those details make it easier for me to stop thinking about work for a while.
>
> It also tends to be relatively uncrowded when we visit, so we can take our time. I enjoy being able to walk slowly and have a conversation without feeling that we need to hurry somewhere.
>
> For me, the combination of being outdoors and having some unhurried time together is what makes it special. I usually leave feeling calmer, which is why I keep going back.

**Practice timing:** This answer is about 230 words. Record it at your normal pace and adjust it to fit the one-to-two-minute speaking time. IELTS does not prescribe a Part 2 word count.

### Alternative Wording for the Same Answer

- **one of my favourite places to unwind / a place I often go to relax / somewhere I can clear my head**
- **What I particularly like is the change of pace / The slower pace is what appeals to me most / I especially enjoy being able to take my time**
- **It tends to be relatively uncrowded when we visit / There usually aren't too many people around at that time / It generally feels quite peaceful during our visits**
- **I usually leave feeling calmer / I tend to feel more relaxed afterwards / It helps me feel refreshed before the week begins**

The last group offers different possible effects. Select the one that is true for you.

### Why This Is Useful for Practice

- It answers the question immediately and covers the suggested points naturally.
- It develops **specific observations**, rather than listing adjectives such as “beautiful” and “amazing.”
- It shifts appropriately between **past events, present routines, and a connection to the present**.
- It connects the place to a personal reason for visiting.
- It gives you room to vary the wording without changing the central details.

The written answer cannot establish a speaking band: delivery, pronunciation, accuracy in real time, and performance elsewhere in the test still matter.

## Short Review Practice

### A. Build One Answer

Choose one cue card above. Prepare for one minute using only keywords, then speak for up to two minutes. In practice, work towards sustaining a developed answer close to two minutes without padding.

Listen back once and check:

1. Did I clearly answer the exact question?
2. Did I develop at least one specific scene or example?
3. Did I explain why it mattered to me?
4. Were my tense choices and sentence endings clear?
5. Was my speech easy to follow, with meaningful stress and natural pauses?

### B. Practise Flexibility

Retell the same answer using three different `/` options. Then change the cue card slightly: for example, change **“a peaceful place”** to **“a place you would recommend.”** Keep relevant details, and change the explanation to suit the new focus.

If you can only deliver one exact script, return to keywords and retell it in simpler language.

### C. Correct These Sentences

1. I've known her since five years.
2. He gave me an advice that helped me decide.
3. I first have visited the park in [year].
4. The photo reminds me my school days.
5. If I will have enough time next month, I'll visit it again.
6. Although the app is useful, but it can be distracting.

**Suggested answers**

1. **I've known her for five years.** Use **for** with a duration and **since** with a starting point.
2. **He gave me a piece of advice that helped me decide.** Alternatively: **He gave me some advice…**
3. **I first visited the park in [year].** A finished, specified past time takes past simple here.
4. **The photo reminds me of my school days.** Pattern: **remind someone of something**.
5. **If I have enough time next month, I'll visit it again.** Use present simple for the future condition in this sentence.
6. **Although the app is useful, it can be distracting.** Alternatively: **The app is useful, but it can be distracting.**
