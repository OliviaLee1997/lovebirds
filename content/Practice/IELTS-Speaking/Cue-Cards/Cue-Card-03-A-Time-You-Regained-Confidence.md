# Cue Card 03 — A Time You Regained Confidence

**Study note:** Added model answer for practice towards IELTS Speaking Bands 7–9, not an examiner-scored response. First-person stories and altered quotations are anonymised study examples. Grammar notes retain qualifications and transcription uncertainty.

**Additional practice topic:** another way to develop your existing experiences and interests.

## Practice Cue Card

Describe a time when other people helped you regain confidence. Say what the problem was, who helped, what changed, and what you learned.

## Extended Model Answer

A time when other people helped me regain confidence was when I was in high school and struggling with maths. My grades weren't very good, and my maths teacher suggested that I transfer to another school. I found that discouraging. When you are a student, a teacher's opinion can carry a great deal of weight, especially if it concerns a subject you already find difficult. It wasn't easy to separate a judgement about my performance at that point from a much broader judgement about what I might be capable of achieving.

**At first**, the suggestion affected my confidence. I was already aware of the difficulty, so hearing that I should change schools made the situation feel more serious. **However**, some classmates at the same school encouraged me, and their support gave me a different perspective. They didn't have to produce a dramatic speech for their encouragement to matter. The important thing was having people around me who didn't treat my current results as a complete description of my future. That helped me begin to see room for improvement again.

**Over time**, I regained confidence, and I eventually got into a university. I wouldn't want to explain that outcome with a simple claim that encouragement automatically produces good results. Academic progress involves work, circumstances and many individual decisions. **What the support changed**, though, was the way I approached the difficulty. It became easier to think about what I could do next instead of assuming that the situation had already been decided. Feeling that improvement was possible mattered because it made continued effort seem worthwhile rather than pointless.

The experience also changed how I think about advice. **On the one hand**, it is useful to listen when someone identifies a genuine weakness. Ignoring a problem does not make it disappear. **On the other hand**, a person's recommendation is still something you can consider rather than automatically accept as the only possible course of action. I learned to distinguish between recognising that I needed to improve and accepting that I had no realistic way to improve in my current environment. Those are quite different conclusions.

**Looking back**, I am grateful to the classmates who encouraged me. Their support helped me recover a sense of possibility at a time when I needed it. If someone I knew were going through a similar experience, I would try to acknowledge the problem while also helping them identify a next step. **Ultimately**, the lesson was not that every negative comment should be rejected. It was that one difficult period, or one person's assessment, does not have to become the final definition of your ability. That is why this experience has stayed with me.

## Linking Language

**At first / over time / eventually** show development; **however** contrasts; **on the one hand / on the other hand** compare two sides; **looking back / ultimately** reflect. Use the paired contrast only when there really are two different considerations.

## Your Grammar Review

| Reported wording | Correction | Pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “My math teacher doesn't like me.” | My maths teacher **didn't like me**. | Past tense for the past narrative. This is a practice sentence, not a factual claim about a teacher's feelings. |
| “She suggested me to go to another high school.” | She **suggested that I go** to another school. / She **advised me to go**. | **Suggest that…** versus **advise someone to…**. |
| “Don't stuck in one tough situation.” | Don't **get stuck** in a tough situation. | Include **get/be** before adjectival **stuck**. |

## Shorten for the Exam

Keep the maths difficulty, the advice, classmates' encouragement, the university outcome and one lesson. Cut the extended discussion of how advice works. Adapt the emphasis if asked about a challenge, encouragement or advice you did not follow.
