# Cue Card 10 — A Song You Like: Ceng Jing De Ni

**Study note:** Added model answer for practice towards IELTS Speaking Bands 7–9, not an examiner-scored response. First-person stories and altered quotations are anonymised study examples. Grammar notes retain qualifications and transcription uncertainty.

## Practice Cue Card

Describe a song you like. Say what it is, who performs it, when you first heard it, and why it matters to you.

## Extended Model Answer

I'd like to talk about a Chinese rock song called Ceng Jing De Ni, performed by Xu Wei. I've known it since I was young, but my relationship with it has changed as I've grown older. That is one of the reasons I find it interesting. A song can stay exactly the same while the person listening to it changes. Something that once felt like a familiar piece of music can later seem to express an experience you didn't understand very well when you first heard it.

**When I was younger**, I didn't think about its meaning in the same way I do now. **These days**, I connect it with youthful dreams, the wish to travel and the way adult responsibilities can change your priorities. I hear it as an invitation to remember the curiosity and enthusiasm you once had. That doesn't mean I think everyone should abandon their responsibilities. For me, the message is more about keeping a part of yourself alive even when life becomes busier and more complicated than you expected.

**What resonates with me most is** the gap between imagining a future and actually living it. When you're young, a dream can feel wonderfully simple because you haven't yet encountered all the decisions involved. Later, work and ordinary commitments take up time and attention. **Even so**, the fact that your life has become more practical doesn't mean your earlier interests have lost all value. The song makes me think about whether I still make room for things I care about, rather than only doing whatever happens to be urgent.

I also like the fact that a familiar song can create that reflection without requiring a long explanation. **For example**, hearing it at a different stage of life can bring back an earlier mood while also making you aware of how your perspective has changed. Xu Wei gave a concert in my city, but I didn't attend. **Although** that would have been a different way to experience his music, listening to a recording still gives me the personal connection I value. The importance of a song doesn't depend entirely on hearing it performed live.

**Looking back**, I think this is why I would choose it over something I simply find catchy. Enjoying a melody is enough reason to like a song, but this one also gives me something to think about. I don't take it as a set of instructions for living; I see it as a reminder to examine my own priorities. **Ultimately**, it encourages me to keep some enthusiasm for life while accepting that growing older brings change. That mixture of recognition and encouragement is what makes it meaningful to me.

## Linking Language

**When I was younger / these days** contrast life stages; **even so / although** express concessions; **for example** develops; **looking back / ultimately** reflect. **What resonates with me most is…** is a focus structure.

## Your Grammar and Collocation Review

| Reported wording | Correction | Pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “I haven't get the ticket” | I **didn't get a ticket**. / I **haven't got a ticket yet**. | Past simple for a finished concert; present perfect for an open situation. |
| “what your aim and what you want to do has changed” | **Your goals have changed.** | A complete subject and plural agreement; an embedded clause would need **what your aim is**. |
| “make our dreams go through” | **make our dreams come true** | Fixed collocation; this is primarily an expression problem. |

**Uncertain wording:** “I'm in the family” does not prove that you said **I'm a fan** incorrectly. The original intent cannot be established from that fragment.

## Shorten for the Exam

Keep the song and performer, the childhood/adult contrast, one interpretation and its personal meaning. Remove most of the general reflections and the concert detail if time is short.
