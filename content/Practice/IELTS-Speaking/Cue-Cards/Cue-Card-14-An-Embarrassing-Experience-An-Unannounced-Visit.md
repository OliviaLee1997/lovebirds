# Cue Card 14 — An Embarrassing Experience: An Unannounced Visit

**Study note:** Added model answer for practice towards IELTS Speaking Bands 7–9, not an examiner-scored response. First-person stories and altered quotations are anonymised study examples. Grammar notes retain qualifications and transcription uncertainty.

## Practice Cue Card

Describe an embarrassing experience. Say when and where it happened, who was involved, what happened, and what you learned.

## Extended Model Answer

An embarrassing experience I remember involved visiting a former English teacher without contacting him in advance. He had taught me some years earlier, and I remembered the lessons fondly. Several years later, I went to see him at an educational institution. I was looking forward to reconnecting with someone who had been part of an earlier stage of my life.

The problem was that I had assumed the connection would be as immediately obvious to him as it was to me. **When I approached him**, he didn't recognise me at first. I felt embarrassed because I had arrived expecting a familiar response and suddenly realised that he did not have the same context in his mind. **From my point of view**, I was returning to an existing friendship. From his, someone had appeared unexpectedly in a place where he was probably thinking about something entirely different.

We then talked about our school days and two other students. That gave the conversation a shared context and helped connect the person standing in front of him with the time we had known each other. **Even though** we were able to talk, the awkward beginning stayed with me. It showed me how easily a friendly intention can lead to an uncomfortable situation if you don't consider what information the other person has. I had been thinking about the pleasure of the reunion, rather than how the visit would feel from his perspective.

**Looking back**, I could have handled it much more thoughtfully. I could have sent a short message, reminded him who I was and asked whether it would be convenient to visit. That would have given him a chance to remember the earlier connection and decide whether he had time. **More importantly**, it would have shown respect for his circumstances. Being pleased to see someone doesn't remove the possibility that they are busy, and knowing them in the past doesn't mean that an unexpected visit will automatically fit into their day.

The experience didn't make me stop wanting to reconnect with people. **Instead**, it changed how I would go about it. If I wanted to contact a former teacher or an old friend now, I would begin with a message and make the invitation easy to respond to. **In the end**, the lesson was quite simple: warmth and consideration need to go together. Remembering someone fondly is one part of a relationship; giving them the time and context to respond comfortably is another. That is why an awkward moment turned into a useful lesson for me.

## Linking Language

**When** sequences; **from my point of view / from his** contrast perspectives; **even though** concedes; **looking back** reflects; **more importantly** prioritises; **instead** changes direction; **in the end** gives the final lesson.

## Your Grammar Review

| Reported wording | Correction | Pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “The situation was occurred.” | The incident **occurred**. / It **happened**. | No passive for **occur/happen** in this meaning. |
| “made me embarrassment” | It **made me feel embarrassed**. | **Embarrassed** is an adjective; **embarrassment** is a noun. Also: **It caused me embarrassment**. |

**Already grammatical:** **I didn't call him before I went…**. **I hadn't called him before I went…** makes the earlier action explicit, but the past perfect is not compulsory just because two past events appear.

**Added useful pattern:** **I should have contacted him first** expresses a past action you now think would have been better.

## Shorten for the Exam

Briefly identify the teacher, explain the visit and failure to recognise you, mention the conversation, and finish with contacting people in advance. Cut most of the general reflection on relationships.
