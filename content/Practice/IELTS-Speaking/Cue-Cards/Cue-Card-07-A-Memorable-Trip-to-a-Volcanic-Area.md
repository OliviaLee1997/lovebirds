# Cue Card 07 — A Memorable Trip to a Volcanic Area

**Study note:** Added model answer for practice towards IELTS Speaking Bands 7–9, not an examiner-scored response. First-person stories and altered quotations are anonymised study examples. Grammar notes retain qualifications and transcription uncertainty.

## Practice Cue Card

Describe a memorable place you visited. Say where or what kind of place it was, what it was like, what happened, and why you remember it.

## Extended Model Answer

I'd like to describe a trip to a volcanic area that stayed in my memory because of the extreme cold and the unusual atmosphere. It was very different from the kind of destination where you find busy streets, cafés and large groups of visitors. The temperature was around minus forty degrees Celsius, and there were only three tourists there. Those two details explain a great deal about why the place felt so different from the busy destinations that people often associate with travelling.

**To begin with**, the cold was impossible to ignore. When you hear a temperature mentioned casually, it is just a number, but being in very cold surroundings makes conditions a much more immediate part of your experience. You become aware that enjoying the scenery is only one consideration. I remember feeling nervous about the situation, particularly the cold. **At the same time**, the small number of visitors made the area feel unusually quiet. There wasn't the sense of being carried along by a large crowd of other tourists.

That combination of quietness and concern made the trip quite different from a straightforward sightseeing outing. I wasn't simply moving from one attraction to another and deciding where to take a photograph. **Instead**, I kept noticing how unfamiliar the setting felt and how strongly it affected my mood. A place can make a strong impression without a major incident taking place. Sometimes it is the contrast with ordinary life that stays with you: being somewhere that makes you pay more attention to your surroundings and question assumptions you normally take for granted.

**Fortunately**, the trip ended well. Looking back on it now, I would separate that positive outcome from the question of whether every choice was sensible. Everything going well on one occasion doesn't automatically tell you that the conditions were harmless. **If I planned a similar visit again**, I would want reliable information about access, weather and visitor arrangements beforehand. Knowing those things in advance would help me judge whether a visit was suitable and feel more prepared, rather than arriving with assumptions based on what an ordinary tourist attraction is like.

**What makes the memory distinctive** is the mixture of fascination and nervousness. I was somewhere very different from the places I normally spend time, and the environment shaped my reaction as much as the destination itself. It also reminded me that a travel story doesn't have to be a list of attractions. Explaining what a place felt like and why you reacted in a particular way can be more revealing. **In the end**, this was memorable because it made the distance between an ordinary day and an unfamiliar environment feel very real to me.

## Linking Language

**To begin with** opens development; **at the same time** combines contrasting impressions; **instead** redirects the focus; **fortunately** evaluates an outcome; **in the end** introduces the final result or assessment.

## Your Grammar Review

| Reported wording | Correction | Pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “There was only three tourists.” | There **were** only three tourists. | Plural agreement. |
| “temperature is minus forty degree” | The temperature **was minus forty degrees Celsius**. | Past description and plural unit. |
| “In which have many volcanoes” | a region **where there are many volcanoes** | A complete location clause needs its subject and verb. |

**Vocabulary:** **Staff** means employees; **stuff** means things or material. Neither uncertain staffing arrangements nor an actual case of hypothermia should be added as established events.

## Shorten for the Exam

Describe the kind of place, the cold and small visitor group, your feelings, and the positive outcome. Remove the longer discussion of storytelling and planning. In a real answer, supply the location if you remember it accurately.
