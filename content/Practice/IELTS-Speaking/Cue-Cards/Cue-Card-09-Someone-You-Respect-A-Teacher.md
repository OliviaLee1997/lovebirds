# Cue Card 09 — Someone You Respect: A Teacher

**Study note:** Added model answer for practice towards IELTS Speaking Bands 7–9, not an examiner-scored response. First-person stories and altered quotations are anonymised study examples. Grammar notes retain qualifications and transcription uncertainty.

## Practice Cue Card

Describe someone you respect. Say who the person is, how you know them, what qualities they have, and why you respect them.

## Extended Model Answer

Someone I respect is my former teacher. He taught me when I was a child, but the quality I admire most becomes more meaningful when I look at it as an adult. He still works as a teacher and has been teaching for many years, which I find impressive when I think about the commitment involved. That is a substantial part of a working life. What impresses me is the dedication involved in continuing to take the same kind of responsibility seriously over such a long period.

He is passionate about his subject and takes teaching seriously. **By that, I mean** that his work seems to matter to him beyond simply having a job to complete each day. Teaching involves explaining ideas that may feel obvious to an experienced adult but are completely new to a child. A teacher has to keep returning to that beginner's perspective. **Even if** a topic has been taught many times before, the students encountering it for the first time still need someone to explain it patiently and clearly.

**What makes his example stand out** is the length of that commitment. It is quite easy to feel enthusiastic when something is new. Maintaining your interest after years of repetition can be more demanding. **For example**, in almost any profession, there are routine tasks that receive little recognition but still need to be done properly. I think dedication shows in the willingness to keep doing those tasks well. In his case, continuing to care about teaching over so many years is the quality I find especially worthy of respect.

My perspective on this has changed as I have gained work experience myself. I now understand more clearly that a person's attitude affects other people, not just their own results. **In a school**, that influence can reach many different groups of students over time. A teacher may not know which explanation or moment of encouragement a student will remember later. **Nevertheless**, the effort can still have a lasting effect. That makes teaching a kind of work where the importance of what you do is not always immediately visible to you.

I don't think I have to choose the same profession to learn from him. The quality I would like to carry into my own work is the habit of taking responsibilities seriously while staying interested in what I do. **Of course**, many years in a job does not automatically make someone admirable; it is the combination of experience and continuing care that matters here. **For that reason**, I would describe him as a role model for professional dedication. He reminds me that a worthwhile career can be built through sustained commitment, rather than a single impressive achievement.

## Linking Language

**By that, I mean** explains; **even if** introduces a condition; **for example** illustrates; **nevertheless** contrasts; **of course** acknowledges a qualification; **for that reason** gives the conclusion. Use the more formal **nevertheless** sparingly in speech.

## Your Grammar Review

| Reported wording | Correction | Pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “The person who I am respected” | The person **I respect** | The intended meaning is active: **I respect him**. |
| “he still teach” | He still **teaches**. | Third-person singular present simple. |
| “passion on… his class” | a **passion for teaching** | **Passion for + noun/-ing**. |

**Tense choice:** **He has been teaching for many years** describes teaching continuing up to the present reference point. In a retrospective account, **At that time, he had been teaching for many years** is appropriate. Past perfect continuous does not prove that he later stopped.

## Shorten for the Exam

Identify the teacher, state his general teaching experience, explain one quality, and relate it to your own working life. Omit most of the general discussion of teaching as a profession.
