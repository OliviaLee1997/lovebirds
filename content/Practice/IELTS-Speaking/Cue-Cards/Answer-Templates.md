# Cue Card Answer Templates — A Summary of All 22 Topics

**Focus:** Turn the 22 existing topics into reusable spoken answers, with topic-specific details and a flexible plan for IELTS Speaking Part 2.

**Added study material:** These templates synthesise the collection's anonymised model answers. They are practice frameworks, not source quotations, verified personal histories, official questions, or score guarantees. Replace bracketed details with your own examples. Suggested scenes are optional practice material.

## How to Build Your Answer

Use **Direct answer → Background → One developed scene → Personal meaning**. Choose the template that fits the question, then change its emphasis to match every prompt on the card. A description needs observable details; an experience needs actions in time order; an ambition needs a realistic plan.

The scripts below are **starting points, not finished two-minute answers**. Replace each bracket with a relevant phrase or sentence, choose one option wherever alternatives are separated by a slash, develop the example, and time yourself. IELTS Part 2 gives you one minute to prepare and one to two minutes to speak. Practise longer only when developing ideas outside an exam simulation.

During preparation, note **subject · background · two details · example · meaning**. Do not write or memorise a complete script. You do not need a dramatic ending if the examiner stops you.

## 1. An Ambition or Future Plan

**Use for:** Card 01. **Plan:** Goal → Origin → Preparation → Obstacle → Motivation.

> One ambition I have is to [goal]. The idea grew out of my interest in [interest], especially the way it allows people to [specific attraction]. At the moment, it is something I would like to explore rather than a confirmed arrangement.
>
> **Before I could do that**, I would need to [first step]. I would also have to [second step], because [practical reason]. For example, [describe a realistic task you would need to learn]. Being interested in something is different from being ready to do it professionally.
>
> **The main challenge would be** [obstacle]. I would deal with that by [manageable action], rather than trying to do everything at once. My first step could be [small next step].
>
> **What appeals to me most is** [personal motivation]. If I eventually achieved it, I think I would feel [emotion], because [specific reason]. For now, I would be happy to make steady progress and find out whether the reality suits me as much as the idea does.

**Fill it from Card 01:** repair motorcycles and run a workshop; technical problem-solving; proper training and supervised experience; costs and customer communication. Say **I would need…**, not **I am opening…**, unless arrangements exist.

## 2. A Challenge, Achievement or Return to Confidence

**Use for:** Cards 02 and 03. **Plan:** Situation → Difficulty → Support and action → Outcome → Meaning.

> I'd like to talk about a time when I [achievement or change]. This happened [general time and setting], when I was [background]. What made it important was that I had been struggling with [difficulty].
>
> **At first**, I felt [emotion], mainly because [reason]. The turning point was [help or feedback]. [Person or group] helped me by [specific action], which made it easier for me to [your next step].
>
> **With that support**, I began to [action]. One moment I remember clearly was [scene]. I [what you did], and [what happened next]. It showed me that [small, believable sign of progress].
>
> **Eventually**, [outcome]. I was pleased with the result, but I also appreciated [something beyond the result]. Looking back, the experience taught me [specific lesson]. It didn't remove every difficulty, but it changed the way I approached [type of challenge].

**Card 02 version:** Start with winning an award. Develop the technical communication challenge and the classmate's interpreting; credit the team and support.

**Card 03 version:** Start with regaining confidence. Develop the effect of classmates' encouragement and your response; do not claim that encouragement alone caused later academic success.

## 3. A Meaningful Gift or Favourite Photograph

**Use for:** Cards 04 and 15. **Plan:** Object → Origin → Visible detail → Associated moment → Meaning now.

> One thing I have kept for a long time is [object or photograph]. I [received it / took it / had it taken] [when and where]. It is connected with [person or group], so its value to me is mainly personal.
>
> **In terms of appearance**, [two visible details]. The detail I notice most is [feature], because it reminds me of [association]. I keep it [place], and I usually look at it when [occasion].
>
> **I still remember** [associated moment]. At the time, we were [background activity], and [small event]. What made that moment special was [reason]. Since then, [relevant change in your lives].
>
> **Whenever I see it**, it brings back [memory or feeling]. It doesn't have to be expensive or technically perfect to matter. For me, it represents [relationship or stage of life], and that's why I would be reluctant to lose it.

**Card 04 version:** Use **A university souvenir a friend gave me at graduation…** Explain the gift and the friendship; do not invent its colour or material as source facts.

**Card 15 version:** Use **A photograph of four school friends at the school gate…** Describe what the image shows before discussing how the friendships continued. Use present tense for the picture and past tense for the occasion.

## 4. An Activity, Sport, Hobby or Enjoyable Routine

**Use for:** Cards 05, 12, 16 and 18. **Plan:** Activity → Beginning or interest → What it involves → Example → Benefit.

> An activity I [enjoy / find interesting] is [activity]. I first [tried it / became interested in it] [when and how]. What appealed to me was [specific attraction], rather than simply [a superficial attraction].
>
> **What it involves is** [two concrete actions]. [Describe your actual routine, or explain how you would begin if you have not taken it up.] One thing that requires attention is [detail], because [reason].
>
> **To give you an example**, [a real occasion or a clearly hypothetical first session]. [Explain what you did or would do.] The part I [enjoyed / would enjoy] most was [detail], which [effect].
>
> **For me, the main benefit is** [physical, mental or social benefit]. It gives me a chance to [personal result]. I wouldn't say it suits everyone, but it fits my interests because [reason]. That's what makes it [an enjoyable routine / something I would like to explore].

**Choose one truthful form in each pair.** If the activity is hypothetical, change **was** to **would be** in the example and closing as needed.

| --- | --- | --- |
| 05 | Explain pacing, breathing and conserving energy; describe a gradual plan if not a regular runner. | **What interests me is learning to pace myself.** |

## 5. A Useful Website, Interesting Book or Meaningful Song

**Use for:** Cards 06, 13 and 20. **Plan:** Identify → Discovery → Content → Specific example → Personal value.

> I'd like to talk about [title or name], which is [type and brief description]. I first came across it [when and how], and it interested me because [connection with your interests].
>
> **The main focus is** [subject or theme]. One aspect I find especially [useful / interesting / meaningful] is [feature or idea]. For example, [describe a specific discussion, question or theme]. That made me think about [your response], particularly because [personal connection].
>
> **The way I engage with it is** [reading, browsing or listening habit]. I tend to [specific action], rather than [less useful habit]. [Add one limitation or a way your understanding has changed.]
>
> **What I value most is** [benefit]. It has helped me [effect], although [reasonable qualification]. I would recommend it to someone who [relevant interest], because [precise reason].

**Card 06 version:** Describe submitted links, discussions and following original sources. A popular comment is a starting point for checking, not proof.

**Card 13 version:** Name the song and performer; explain how its themes connect with youthful dreams and adult responsibilities. Treat that meaning as your interpretation. Do not invent a concert visit: the existing model says the speaker did not attend.

**Card 20 version:** Give the actual title if you know it; otherwise describe the subject. Introduce a claim with **The author argues that…** and your reaction with **It made me wonder whether…** Keep reading in progress distinct from finishing the book.

## 6. A Close Friend or Someone You Respect

**Use for:** Cards 07 and 09. **Plan:** Relationship → Quality → Evidence → Influence → Value.

> The person I'd like to describe is [role or relationship]. I've known them since [general period], when [how you met]. The quality I particularly [appreciate / respect] is [quality].
>
> **By that, I mean** they [observable behaviour]. [Give an example from your shared experience or a pattern you have genuinely observed.] What stands out is the way they [specific detail], even when [circumstance].
>
> **For me**, that matters because [reason]. It makes me feel [emotion] and has influenced the way I [behaviour or attitude]. We [present contact or shared activity], although [relevant change or limitation].
>
> **What makes this person important to me is** [relationship value or influence]. I don't think [quality] is only about [surface impression]; it also involves [deeper, observable behaviour]. That's something I [value in our friendship / try to bring into my own work].

**Card 07 version:** Emphasise common interests, comfortable conversations and emotional support. Explain **why you get on well**, rather than turning the whole answer into praise.

**Card 09 version:** Emphasise sustained care and commitment to teaching. Explain what dedication looks like; length of service alone is not the whole reason for respect. Do not invent a personal incident and present it as a recorded memory.

## 7. A Language or Skill You Learned

**Use for:** Card 08. **Plan:** Starting point → Methods → Difficulty → Practice → Present perspective.

> One thing I studied was [language or skill]. I started [when and where], because [motivation or opportunity]. Before that, I had [starting knowledge], so [first impression].
>
> **At the beginning**, we worked on [basic material]. We used [learning methods], which helped me [specific ability]. The hardest part for me was [difficulty]. For instance, [explain one sound, structure or task], and why I found it challenging.
>
> **What I came to realise was** that [insight about learning]. [Describe a genuine practice method you used.] If I returned to it now, I would [improved practice plan], because [reason].
>
> **Looking back**, the experience gave me [benefit or perspective]. I wouldn't claim to have mastered it, but it helped me understand [specific lesson]. That is relevant to the way I now approach [current learning goal].

**Fill it from Card 08:** Russian at university; textbooks, tests, poems and Russian speakers; the trilled r; connecting sounds and structures with communication. Keep **what I did** and **what I would try now** separate.

## 8. A Memorable Trip, Good News or Unexpected Event

**Use for:** Cards 10, 11 and 19. **Plan:** Setting → Expectation → Turning point → Reaction and outcome → Reflection.

> An experience that stands out happened [when and where]. I was [background], and I had expected [original expectation]. Instead, [what made it different].
>
> **At first**, I felt [emotion], because [reason]. I remember [specific scene or detail]. [Describe what happened in time order.] The turning point came when [event or realisation].
>
> **What stayed with me most was** [moment]. I [reaction or action], while [another detail]. By the end, [outcome], and my feelings had changed from [initial feeling] to [later feeling].
>
> **Looking back**, I think it was memorable because [specific contrast or personal meaning]. [Explain what you learned, appreciated, or would do differently.] I wouldn't say every experience like this ends the same way, but this one changed how I think about [relevant idea].

| Card | Turning point and details | Keep the question central |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 10 | A volcanic landscape, exceptional cold and very few visitors; the trip ended well. | Describe the setting and mixed feelings. Better preparation is a reflection, not an invented rescue story. |
| 11 | A friend received a course offer after preparing and waiting; the news was sooner than expected. | Explain the news, how you heard it, your reaction and why it mattered to your friend. |
| 19 | A call interrupted holiday plans; a difficult overnight journey preceded a team award. | Develop the unexpected change and the contrast between the tiring start and rewarding result. |

For good news, a natural opening is **Some good news I remember hearing was that…** For the competition, begin **An unexpected change of plan happened when…**

## 9. A Peaceful Place, Regular Shop or Educational Attraction

**Use for:** Cards 14, 21 and 22. **Plan:** Location → First visit or routine → Distinctive features → What you do → Reason to return.

> A place I'd like to describe is [place], which is [location]. I first went there [general time and reason], and [explain whether you return regularly or are describing one visit].
>
> **What stands out is** [distinctive feature]. There are [two concrete details], which make it feel [atmosphere or practical effect]. When I go there, I [main activity]. [Add an example of what you notice, buy or learn.]
>
> **One reason I value it is** [reason]. Compared with [relevant alternative], it [difference]. That matters to me because [connection with your routine or interests]. [Include a limitation if relevant.]
>
> **Overall**, I would describe it as [brief assessment]. It gives me the chance to [benefit], and that is more important to me than [less relevant attraction]. [Finish with a reason to return or a suitable recommendation.]

**Card 14 version:** Lead with waterways, trees, birds and the atmosphere during your visits. **A peaceful place** asks mainly about the surroundings; keep walking as supporting detail.

**Card 21 version:** Describe the supermarket's convenience, range and familiar layout. Explain an ordinary visit and why it saves time; do not borrow peaceful scenery from the park answer.

**Card 22 version:** Identify the Chengdu Panda Base, then explain what visitors can learn about care, habitat and conservation. Distinguish what you remember seeing from information you learned later. Do not invent a guided tour or animal encounter.

## 10. An Awkward Experience or Mistake

**Use for:** Card 17. **Plan:** Intention → Assumption → Awkward moment → Response → Better approach.

> An embarrassing experience I remember was when I [action]. My intention was to [positive intention], and I assumed that [expectation]. This happened [general setting], after [background].
>
> **The awkward moment came when** [what went wrong]. I suddenly realised that [mistaken assumption]. I felt [emotion], because [why the moment was uncomfortable].
>
> **To make the situation easier**, [what you actually did]. We [how the interaction continued], although [remaining feeling]. What I had overlooked was [other person's perspective or missing information].
>
> **Looking back, I could have** [better action]. That would have [likely benefit]. The experience didn't stop me from [positive intention], but it taught me to [specific change]. If I were in the same situation again, I would [clear next step].

**Fill it from Card 17:** visiting a former teacher without warning; not being recognised at first; recalling school days and other students; sending a message with context before a future visit. Do not claim the teacher refused the visit or was angry.

## Reuse the Material Without Losing the Question

| Shared material | First question | Second question | What must change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wetland park | Card 14: describe a peaceful place | Card 18: describe something you do to relax | Shift from the surroundings to your actions and their effect. |
| School or university relationships | Card 07: describe a close friend | Card 04: describe a gift | Shift from the person and relationship to the object and the occasion. Do not assume the two models describe the same friend. |
| Student competition | Card 02: describe an achievement | Card 19: describe an unexpected event | Shift from the accomplishment to the change of plan. These are separate model stories; do not silently merge their events. |
| Swimming | Card 16: describe a sport | A new practice question about learning | Shift from present enjoyment to the learning process. The new question is added practice, not an extra collection topic. |

**Useful organising phrases:** **At first** introduces an initial reaction; **for example** adds evidence; **what stood out was** focuses attention; **as a result** connects a cause with its effect; **looking back** introduces reflection. Choose phrases that express a real relationship between your ideas.

## Recall Practice — Added Study Material

1. Prepare Card 14 and Card 18 using the same park. Give each a different first sentence and central paragraph.
2. Retell Card 19 using five keywords: **call · plans · journey · teamwork · result**. Which part makes it unexpected?
3. Replace three brackets in Template 6 with details about someone you know. Add one behaviour that supports the quality you name.
4. Correct: “If I opened a workshop, I will need training.”
5. Correct: “The photo was taken years ago and showed four friends.” Assume you are describing what is visible in the photograph now.
6. Give two versions of a running answer: one about a hobby you practise and one about a hobby you would like to explore.

### Suggested Answers

1. **A peaceful place I enjoy visiting is a wetland park.** Develop its waterways, trees and atmosphere. **One way I relax is to walk in a nearby wetland park.** Develop the easy pace, company and mental break.
2. The call changed an existing plan; the eventual award contrasted with the initial disappointment and tiring journey. An award alone does not explain the unexpected change.
3. Answers vary. **My friend is dependable: when we arrange to meet, they let me know promptly if their plans change.** This is a newly written practice example.
4. **If I opened a workshop, I would need training.** This presents a hypothetical situation. **If I open a workshop, I will need training** is possible for a future possibility presented as more realistic.
5. **The photo was taken years ago and shows four friends.** Past passive for its creation; present simple for its visible content now.
6. **I go running… / I started… / I usually…** for actual participation. **I find running interesting… / If I took it up, I would…** for a hypothetical plan. Add details that fit the chosen version.
