# Personal Grammar Review

The following **57 selected recorded-expression entries** are grouped by language pattern. Some combine related fragments or include a vocabulary improvement; they are not a numerical measure of how often you made mistakes. Review three patterns at a time, then use them in a fresh spoken answer.

**Priorities:** past versus present; have/has + past participle; modal/did/does + base verb; verb complements; plurals and articles; complete predicates; prepositions and connectors.

Tense consistency means matching the intended time. A past first visit, a current routine and a friendship continuing until now naturally need different tenses.

## Past Events and Time References

| Reported wording | Correction or useful revision | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “I just go outside for eat.” | I just **went outside to eat**. | For the completed outing: went. Express purpose with to + base verb. |
| “I catched…” | I **caught**… | Catch has an irregular past form. |
| “When I was a child... I want to be a scientist.” | When I was a child, I **wanted** to be a scientist. | Use past tense for the childhood wish. |
| “She translate late. Chinese to English for me” | She **translated from Chinese into English for me**. | Past tense and a complete verb pattern. The transcribed word late is uncertain. |
| “…she choose to get a PhD degree…” | She **chose to do a PhD**. | Past decision: chose. PhD degree is redundant, not the main tense problem. |
| “We are best friends in high school.” | We **were** best friends in high school. / We **have been** close friends **since** high school. | Choose a past description or continuing relationship. |
| “we don't have a seat” | We **didn't have any seats**. | Past tense for the finished train journey; none of us had a seat also works. |

## Perfect and Continuous Forms

| Reported wording | Correction or useful revision | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “I have traveling to USA.” | I **have been to the USA**. | Use have/has + past participle; include the in the USA. |
| “It have been raining for a week.” | It **has been raining** for a week. | It + has; present perfect continuous. |
| “AI making life better.” | AI **is making** life better. | Present continuous needs be + -ing. |
| “I have been lived there for five years.” | I **have lived** there for five years. / I **have been living** there for five years. | Do not combine the two structures. Live meaning reside is not passive here. |
| “I have never swam in a river.” | I have never **swum** in a river. | Swim → swam → swum; use the participle after have. |
| “She has been live with her friend…” | She **has been living** with her friend. | Have/has been + -ing. |
| “I haven't get the ticket” | I **haven't got a ticket yet**. / I **didn't get a ticket**. | Participle after have; choose the tense according to whether the situation is open or the concert is over. |

## Modal Verbs, Auxiliaries and Questions

| Reported wording | Correction or useful revision | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “it translate” | It **translates**. | Third-person singular present simple takes -s/-es. |
| “Does the opera is a a kind of art” | **Is opera a kind of art?** | Invert be directly; do not add does. |
| “I didn't saw a woman...” | I didn't **see** a woman. | Did not + base verb. |
| “Everything will goes.” | Everything **will go well**. | Modal + base verb. |
| “I needn't to check every website.” | I **needn't check** every website. / I **don't need to check** every website. | Keep the modal and ordinary-verb patterns separate. |
| “he was my teacher… he still teach” | He was my teacher, and he **still teaches**. | Different times justify different tenses; he teaches needs -es. |
| “it don't have so many people” | It **doesn't get too crowded**. | It + does not + base verb. The crowd expression is also more natural. |
| “The next event will in the [month]” | My next event **will be in [month]**. | Will + be; ordinary month names do not take the. |

## Verb Patterns: What Comes After the Verb

| Reported wording | Correction or useful revision | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “I prefer to alone.” | I **prefer to be alone**. | Infinitival to needs a verb; alone does not replace be. |
| “We teach blind people swim.” | We **teach blind people to swim**. | Teach + person + to-infinitive. |
| “I encourage the people, uh, use bicycles more.” | I **encourage people to use bicycles more**. | Encourage + person + to-infinitive. |
| “She suggested me to go to another high school.” | She **suggested that I go** to another school. | Suggest that + clause; alternatively, advised me to go. |
| “I started a learning Russian.” | I **started learning Russian**. | No article before this verb complement; started to learn is also correct. |
| “so glad to hearing that” | I was so glad **to hear that**. | Glad + to-infinitive. |
| “Let me started to answer…” | Let me **start answering**. | Let + object + base verb. Let me start to answer is also grammatical. |
| “I will … spending time go there” | I'll **spend some time looking around the shop**. | Will + base verb; spend time + -ing. If travel time is intended, use getting there. |

## Countable Nouns, Articles and Agreement

| Reported wording | Correction or useful revision | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “two parrot” | two **parrots** | Use a plural countable noun after two. |
| “We have translator.” | We have **a translation app**. | A singular countable noun needs a determiner. App makes the tool meaning clear. |
| “We have many chance.” | We have **many chances**. | Many + plural countable noun. |
| “Sixty person one class there there are sixty person in one class.” | There **were sixty students in my class**. | Complete there were structure; plural noun for the past class. |
| “They are holding a books.” | They are holding **books**. | No a before a plural noun. Each holding a book is a different, more specific wording. |
| “There are less chance for me to meet her.” | There **is little chance of meeting her soon**. | Little chance = probability. Fewer opportunities/chances = fewer occasions. |

| “a pets / a children” | **a pet / pets; a child / children** | No a with these plural nouns. This row groups two source fragments. |

## Complete Sentences and Active or Passive Forms

| Reported wording | Correction or useful revision | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Don't stuck in one tough situation.” | Don't **get stuck** in a tough situation. | Include be/get with adjectival stuck. |
| “a vowel… that is not exist” | a sound that **does not exist** in my first language | Exist is the main verb; use do-support, not be. |
| “The situation was occurred.” | The incident **occurred**. | No ordinary passive for occur in this use. |
| “I'm easy to get there” | **It's easy for me to get there**. | Evaluate the action with it is easy for someone to do something. |
| “it was happened” | It **happened**. | Happen is intransitive in this meaning. |
| “keyboard is consist of…” | It **consists of**… / It **is composed of**… | Do not combine is with consists. For the physical object: A keyboard has keys for letters, numbers and symbols. |

## Prepositions and Linking Expressions

| Reported wording | Correction or useful revision | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “My hometown is famous as white wine.” | My hometown is **famous for baijiu**. | Famous for a product; famous as a role or identity. Baijiu is a distilled spirit, not white wine. |
| “I didn't wake up earlier because of I went to bed so late yesterday.” | I didn't wake up early **because I went to bed so late last night**. | Because + clause; because of + noun phrase. Earlier itself is valid when a comparison is intended. |
| “As result, as the result” | **As a result**, … | Learn the complete linking phrase. |
| “We learned that on the history class.” | We learned that **in history class**. | In class; in the history class can work for a specified class. |
| “listening the bird sing” | **listening to the birds sing/singing** | The missing to is the clear pattern issue. Both sing and singing can be grammatical. |

| Reported wording | Correction or useful revision | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “I am a software engineering.” | I am **a software engineer**. | Engineer names a person; engineering names the field. |
| “You have no enough personal life.” | You **don't have enough privacy**. | Not enough + noun. Privacy is the intended meaning; personal life has a different scope. |

## Linking Words: Two Patterns Worth Automating

**Because + clause**

> I felt exhausted **because we hadn't slept**.

**Because of / despite + noun phrase or -ing form**

> I felt exhausted **because of the overnight journey**.  
> **Despite feeling exhausted**, we continued with the competition.

**Although + clause**

> **Although we were exhausted**, we continued with the competition.

For ordinary standard English practice, use either **Although X, Y** or **X, but Y**, rather than doubling the same contrast with **Although X, but Y**. This last example is an added practice caution, not a claim that you made that exact error.

**As a result** introduces a consequence. **In the end** introduces a final outcome. **Eventually** means after some time or difficulty; it is not interchangeable with **to sum up**.
