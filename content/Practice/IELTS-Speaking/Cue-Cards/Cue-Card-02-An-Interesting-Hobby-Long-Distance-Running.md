# Cue Card 02 — An Interesting Hobby: Long-Distance Running

**Study note:** Added model answer for practice towards IELTS Speaking Bands 7–9, not an examiner-scored response. First-person stories and altered quotations are anonymised study examples. Grammar notes retain qualifications and transcription uncertainty.

## Practice Cue Card

Describe a hobby you find interesting. Say what it involves, how you learned about it, what skills it needs, and why it interests you.

## Extended Model Answer

A hobby I find interesting is long-distance running. What attracts me is the combination of physical effort and judgement. From a distance, it might look as though a runner is simply repeating the same movement, but keeping going comfortably requires more thought than that. You have to notice your breathing, judge your pace and decide how much energy to use. I became more interested in that side of the activity when I learned about the strategy involved in longer races.

**One point that stood out was** the emphasis on breathing and conserving energy. This explanation made me think about the difference between starting enthusiastically and managing to continue. If you run much too fast at the beginning, the rest of the distance can become unnecessarily difficult. **In other words**, a good start isn't necessarily the fastest start. That is an appealing idea because it makes the activity seem less like a test of natural speed and more like something you can learn to approach intelligently over time.

**If I took it up more seriously**, I would begin with a manageable distance and focus on consistency. I wouldn't want to enter a demanding race simply to prove something to myself. I'd rather establish a routine that fitted around work and increase the challenge gradually. **For example**, I could use a familiar route so that I wouldn't have to think about finding my way, and pay attention to whether I was setting off too quickly. The important thing would be learning from each session rather than expecting an immediate dramatic improvement.

**Another reason** I find running interesting is that progress can be personal. One runner might be trying to improve a race time, while another might simply want to finish a comfortable run after a busy day. Neither person needs exactly the same goal. I like the possibility of judging an activity against your own starting point. **That said**, I wouldn't assume that running suits everyone equally or that it always feels relaxing. The distance, surroundings and physical demands all affect the experience, and those are things I would need to consider.

For me, the most useful lesson from the discussion was the idea of pacing yourself. It applies quite naturally to work and studying as well: using all your energy at the beginning can make a longer task harder to complete. **Of course**, that doesn't mean running and working are identical, but the comparison helps explain why the hobby caught my attention. **Overall**, I find long-distance running interesting because there is more to it than moving quickly. It involves patience, self-awareness and the ability to make small adjustments while keeping a longer goal in mind.

## Linking Language

**In other words** restates; **for example** illustrates; **another reason** adds; **that said** qualifies; **of course** acknowledges a limit; **overall** sums up. The conditional opening **if I took it up…** keeps a proposed routine separate from an actual experience.

## Shorten for the Exam

Keep the hobby, the explanation of pacing, one hypothetical way you would approach it, and your main reason for finding it interesting. Remove the extended comparison with work.
