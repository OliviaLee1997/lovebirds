# Cue Card 04 — A Close Friend

**Study note:** Added model answer for practice towards IELTS Speaking Bands 7–9, not an examiner-scored response. First-person stories and altered quotations are anonymised study examples. Grammar notes retain qualifications and transcription uncertainty.

## Practice Cue Card

Describe a person you are close to. Say who the person is, what they are like, what you have in common, and why you get on well.

## Extended Model Answer

I'd like to talk about a close friend. We get on well and have a lot in common, especially our interest in travelling. She is outgoing, which is one of the qualities I associate with her, but what matters most to me is the sense of support in our relationship. Knowing someone for a long time doesn't automatically mean you feel comfortable talking to them about everything. With my friend, though, there is a familiarity that makes it easier to explain what is on my mind.

**When we were younger**, our conversations were naturally shaped by school and everyday life. As we've grown older, the subjects have changed, because our responsibilities and concerns have changed too. **Even so**, the basic connection is still there. I think that is an interesting part of a close friendship: you can remember an earlier version of a person while also getting to know who they are becoming. We aren't exactly the same people we were at school, and our relationship has had to grow along with us.

Travel is one subject that gives us common ground. You don't need to agree on every destination to enjoy discussing where you would like to go or what kind of trip would suit you. **For example**, a conversation about travel can become a conversation about whether you want adventure, rest or a chance to experience a different way of life. I like that it can reveal something about a person's priorities. It is also a pleasant subject to return to when ordinary routines are taking up most of your attention.

**More importantly**, I associate my friend with emotional support. For me, being supportive doesn't always mean having a solution ready. Sometimes it means listening carefully enough that the other person can work out what they actually think. That is the kind of relationship I value. **Although** a friend may see a situation differently, it helps to feel that they are trying to understand you. I would find it much harder to be open with someone if I felt every conversation was going to turn into a judgement of my choices.

**Looking ahead**, I know that our lives may become busier and that being close will still require effort. Shared history is valuable, but it doesn't replace paying attention to each other now. I would like us to keep making room for conversations, whether they are about a serious decision or something quite ordinary. **That's why** I would choose my friend for this topic: we share interests and memories, but the relationship matters most because it gives me a feeling of security. I feel that there is someone I can turn to and support in return.

## Linking Language

**When we were younger / as we've grown older** organise time; **even so / although** express contrast; **for example** develops an idea; **more importantly** prioritises; **looking ahead** shifts to the future; **that's why** explains the choice.

## Your Grammar and Expression Review

| Reported wording | Correction | Pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “She is on outgoing girl.” | She is **an outgoing person**. | **An** before a vowel sound; **on** may reflect transcription uncertainty. |
| “very similar with me” | very **similar to me** | Learn the adjective and its preposition together. |
| “I go on well with my friend.” | I **get on well with** my friend. | A phrasal-verb choice: **get on/along well with**. |

**Meaning check:** **We used to talk every evening** suggests a changed habit. **We still talk regularly** describes a continuing one. Do not replace one with the other automatically.

## Shorten for the Exam

Keep the description of your friend, one shared interest, and one specific explanation of emotional support. Reduce the general reflections about close relationships and the future.
