# Cue Card 14 — A Peaceful Place: the wetland park

**Study note:** Added model answer for practice towards IELTS Speaking Bands 7–9, not an examiner-scored response. First-person stories and altered quotations are anonymised study examples. Grammar notes retain qualifications and transcription uncertainty.

## Practice Cue Card

Describe a peaceful place you like to visit. Say where it is, when you first went there, what you do there, and why it feels peaceful.

## Extended Model Answer

A peaceful place I enjoy visiting is the wetland park in the city. It's easy to get to, which allows it to become part of my ordinary life rather than somewhere I only visit on a special holiday. I first went there in [year], when I started working in the city. **Since then**, I have come to associate it with taking a break from the pace of work. Its appeal is partly that I can experience a different kind of setting without organising a long journey.

I usually go on some evenings with my friend. There are waterways, different types of trees and traditional villages, and we can rent a boat or spend time watching birds. **What I especially enjoy is** having several simple things to notice without feeling that I need to rush between them. A place like that rewards paying attention. You can look at the water for a while, notice movement in the trees or just take in the surroundings without needing to turn the visit into a tightly planned sequence of activities.

**During our visits**, it usually isn't too crowded, and that makes a considerable difference to how comfortable I feel. I wouldn't claim that the wetland is always quiet at every time of year. I am describing the visits I enjoy and the times I tend to choose. **Compared with** a busy street or a crowded attraction, having more space around me makes it easier to slow down. I don't feel the same pressure to keep moving simply because there are people directly behind or in front of me.

The option of renting a boat is another part of the attraction. It offers a different way to experience the waterways and makes the visit feel distinct from an ordinary walk through the city. **Even without** doing that, though, I can enjoy the birds and trees. I like places where the experience doesn't depend on completing one particular activity. **For example**, if I only have enough energy for a quiet visit, I can still feel that the time was worthwhile. I don't need every outing to be ambitious or full of new experiences.

**For me**, peace is partly about the surroundings and partly about what those surroundings allow me to stop doing. At the park, I can spend less time thinking about schedules and more time noticing what is immediately around me. Going with someone close to me also makes it a pleasant shared routine. **That's why** the place has become important to me: it gives me an accessible change of pace. It is somewhere I can return to and enjoy, rather than a destination that only makes sense as a once-in-a-lifetime trip.

## Linking Language

**Since then** connects first visit and current habit; **compared with** contrasts; **even without** introduces a concession; **for example** illustrates; **for me / that's why** connect personal judgement and reason.

## Your Grammar and Meaning Review

| Reported wording | Correction | Pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “it don't have so many people” | It **doesn't get too crowded**. | **It doesn't + base verb**; the new wording also improves naturalness. |
| “Let me started to answer…” | Let me **start answering**. | **Let + object + base verb**. |
| “buy a boat” | **rent a boat**, if temporary use is intended | Vocabulary and meaning; **buy a boat** is grammatical. |

**Already potentially correct:** **I didn't go there this Sunday** works if that Sunday's visit opportunity is already past. Do not label it wrong without the time context.

## Shorten for the Exam

Keep location, [year], the Sunday routine, two activities, and your reason for finding it peaceful. Omit the longer comparisons and general reflections on outings.
