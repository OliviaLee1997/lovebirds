# Cue Card 11 — Some Good News: A Friend's Course Offer

**Study note:** Added model answer for practice towards IELTS Speaking Bands 7–9, not an examiner-scored response. First-person stories and altered quotations are anonymised study examples. Grammar notes retain qualifications and transcription uncertainty.

## Practice Cue Card

Describe some good news you received. Say what it was, who it concerned, when you heard it, and how you felt.

## Extended Model Answer

Some good news I remember hearing was that a friend had been offered a place on a course she wanted to take. She had spent time preparing her application and waiting for a response, so the decision mattered to her. I was pleased because the opportunity was connected with something she wanted to learn, rather than simply another task she felt obliged to complete. It was a reminder that a short announcement can represent a much longer period of effort and uncertainty for the person receiving it.

**At first**, I was surprised because I hadn't realised the result was due so soon. **Once I had taken it in**, I wanted to hear more about what she would be studying and what she hoped to get from the experience. Her enthusiasm made the conversation enjoyable. It wasn't necessary for me to know every detail of the subject to understand why she was excited. I could appreciate the feeling of having a new possibility open up after spending time working towards it.

**For me**, one of the nicest aspects was seeing preparation lead to an opportunity. Applications can involve work that other people don't notice: reading the requirements, organising information, revising an explanation of your interests and deciding whether the commitment is realistic. None of that guarantees a successful outcome. **Even so**, it can make good news especially satisfying when it arrives, because the result has a connection with actions you took rather than feeling like a completely random event.

We also talked about the practical side of starting a course. An offer is exciting, but it is only the beginning. My friend would still need to make time for studying and work out how the course fitted around other commitments. **What mattered most was** that she had something she wanted to explore and a reason to put in the effort. I thought it would be useful to begin with realistic expectations, so that an ordinary difficult week would not make her feel that choosing the course had been a mistake.

**Looking back**, the conversation stayed with me because it was a simple example of sharing someone else's happiness. I didn't need to turn the news into advice or predict exactly what the course would lead to. I could listen, ask questions and show interest in the next step. **Of course**, being offered a place does not guarantee that everything afterwards will be easy. What made it good news was the chance to begin something meaningful and the encouragement that came with that chance. It was pleasant to be part of that moment.

## Linking Language

**At first / once I had taken it in** show a change in reaction; **even so** introduces a qualification; **for me** marks a personal perspective; **looking back** reflects. **What mattered most was…** focuses attention on the central point.

## Your Grammar Review

| Reported wording | Correction | Pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “received a good news” | received **some good news** | **News** is uncountable; also **a piece of good news**. |
| “She has been live with her friend…” | She **has been living** with her friend. | **Have/has been + -ing**. |
| “so glad to hearing that” | I was so glad **to hear that**. | **Glad + to-infinitive**. |
| “take her friend to home” | **bring her friend home** | No **to** before adverbial **home**. **Take him home** is also grammatical when the direction fits. |

**Tense flexibility:** **I thought they would…** reports a past belief. **I think they will…** states a current one. Both can be correct.

## Shorten for the Exam

Keep the news, its timing, your surprise, and why the opportunity mattered to her. Omit most of the general discussion of planning and supporting a friend.
