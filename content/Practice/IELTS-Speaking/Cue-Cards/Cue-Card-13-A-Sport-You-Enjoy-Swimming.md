# Cue Card 13 — A Sport You Enjoy: Swimming

**Study note:** Added model answer for practice towards IELTS Speaking Bands 7–9, not an examiner-scored response. First-person stories and altered quotations are anonymised study examples. Grammar notes retain qualifications and transcription uncertainty.

## Practice Cue Card

Describe a sport you enjoy. Say what it is, when and how you learned it, who you do it with, and why you enjoy it.

## Extended Model Answer

One of my favourite sports is swimming. I learned it when I was in high school, with a coach at a nearby gym, and it has remained an activity I enjoy. These days, I sometimes go with my friend or some of my colleagues. What appeals to me is the combination of physical activity and a change in mental focus. I am doing something active, but I also get a break from the messages, screens and unfinished tasks that can occupy my attention during the working day.

**At first**, I was afraid of the water and thought swimming would be difficult. That makes my current enjoyment of it feel more meaningful. An activity can seem quite intimidating before you have learned how to approach it. **As I became more comfortable**, I started to enjoy the experience rather than only thinking about the difficulty. I wouldn't describe that as suddenly becoming fearless. It was more a change in familiarity: something that had felt uncertain gradually became an activity I could take part in with greater confidence.

**One particular benefit is** the way swimming limits my usual distractions. When I'm doing some other activities, it is easy to reach for my phone, check a message or start watching a video. In the water, that option simply isn't available in the same way. **As a result**, my attention has somewhere more immediate to go. I can focus on what I am doing instead of constantly switching between the activity and something on a screen. For me, that makes the break feel more complete than a period of rest filled with notifications.

I also prefer the feeling of exercising in water to feeling hot and sticky during some other forms of exercise. That is a personal preference, rather than a claim that people cannot sweat while swimming. **Even when** I am putting in effort, the experience feels more comfortable to me. Going with people I know adds another enjoyable element. **Although** swimming itself requires my attention, the outing can still be something we share. I don't have to choose between doing something for my fitness and spending time with people whose company I enjoy.

**Looking back**, swimming is a good example of why I don't want an initial difficulty to decide whether I will like something permanently. I was nervous when I began, yet it became one of my favourite ways to stay active. I would recommend finding an activity that suits your preferences rather than assuming everyone needs the same exercise routine. **For me**, swimming works because the physical experience is enjoyable and the mental break is valuable. It leaves me feeling that I have done something useful with my time without spending the whole session thinking about work.

## Linking Language

**At first / as I became more comfortable** show change; **as a result** explains a consequence; **even when / although** concede; **looking back** reflects. **One particular benefit is…** introduces a supporting point.

## Your Grammar Review

| Reported wording | Correction | Pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “one of my favourite exercise” | one of my favourite **exercises/sports** | **One of + plural noun**. |
| “It doesn't let me concentrated.” | It doesn't let me **concentrate**. | **Let + object + base verb**. For swimming's positive effect: **It helps me concentrate**. |
| “I enjoy talking… and spend time…” | I enjoy **talking… and spending time…** | Parallel complements after **enjoy**. |
| “I have never swam in a river.” | I have never **swum** in a river. | **Swim → swam → swum**. |

## Shorten for the Exam

Keep the sport, high-school lessons and initial fear, who you go with, and the phone-free benefit. Cut the longer general reflection and comparison with other exercise.
