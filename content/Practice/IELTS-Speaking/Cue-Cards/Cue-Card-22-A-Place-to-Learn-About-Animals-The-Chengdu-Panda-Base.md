# Cue Card 22 — A Place to Learn About Animals: The Chengdu Panda Base

**Study note:** Added model answer for practice towards IELTS Speaking Bands 7–9, not an examiner-scored response. First-person stories and altered quotations are anonymised study examples. Grammar notes retain qualifications and transcription uncertainty.

## Practice Cue Card

Describe a place where you can learn about animals. Say where it is, when you visited, what you can learn there, and why it interests you.

## Extended Model Answer

I'd like to talk about the Chengdu Research Base of Giant Panda Breeding in Sichuan. I first visited it in [year], while I was studying. Giant pandas are so familiar from pictures and videos that it is easy to feel you already know a great deal about them. Visiting a place connected with their care and conservation gives the subject a different kind of reality. For me, the interesting part is the opportunity to think about the animals' needs, rather than only their appearance or popularity.

**One feature that interests me is** the attention given to pandas at different stages of life. A young animal and an adult don't have exactly the same needs, and that makes animal care more complex than simply providing a space and some food. The base's work includes research, care, conservation and public education. **In other words**, it has purposes beyond giving visitors an attractive place to take photographs. That broader role is what I would want someone to understand if I were recommending it as a place to learn about animals.

Pandas depend mainly on bamboo for food, and their wild habitat is limited to parts of China. **Because of that**, protecting them involves thinking about the conditions in which they can live, not just the individual animals people find appealing. I find that a useful way to approach conservation more generally. **For example**, caring about a species can lead to questions about habitat, food and the effects of human activity. An animal's familiar image can be the starting point for learning about a much larger environmental picture.

**At the same time**, popularity can create a narrow view of an animal. People may know the name of a famous panda or enjoy a short video without knowing much about the species. I don't think that enjoyment is a bad thing; it can be what first makes someone interested. **What matters is** whether the interest develops into understanding. A place like the panda base can help connect the appeal of seeing the animals with information about the care they require and the reasons conservation work still matters.

**Looking back on the visit**, that is the aspect I would like to develop when talking about it now. Seeing an animal can make a topic feel more immediate than reading a short statement about protection. **Even though** pandas' conservation status has improved, improvement doesn't mean that the work is finished. I would recommend the base to someone who enjoys animals and is willing to look beyond the most photogenic moments. For me, its value lies in bringing curiosity and learning together, so that a visit can leave you with questions worth exploring afterwards.

## Linking Language

**In other words** explains; **because of that** links cause and result; **for example** develops; **at the same time / even though** qualify; **looking back on the visit** introduces reflection.
