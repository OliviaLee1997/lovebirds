# Cue Card 01 — A Meaningful Gift

**Study note:** Added model answer for practice towards IELTS Speaking Bands 7–9, not an examiner-scored response. First-person stories and altered quotations are anonymised study examples. Grammar notes retain qualifications and transcription uncertainty.

## Practice Cue Card

Describe a gift that is important to you. Say what it is, who gave it to you, when you received it, and why it matters.

## Extended Model Answer

I'd like to talk about a university souvenir that one of my closest friends gave me at our graduation ceremony. We had studied together, so the gift was connected with a period of life we both understood very well. I wouldn't describe its importance in terms of how much it cost. **What made it special was** the person who gave it to me and the circumstances in which I received it. It represented the end of our time as students together and the beginning of two different paths.

My friend is outgoing and easy-going, and she is also very capable when it comes to scientific research. Those qualities are an interesting combination: she takes her work seriously, but she is someone I associate with friendship as well as academic life. **By the time** we graduated, she had decided to pursue a further qualification abroad. I was pleased that she had found a direction she wanted to follow, **although** I also realised that we probably wouldn't be able to see each other very often afterwards.

Receiving the souvenir made that change feel more real. A graduation ceremony is usually associated with celebration, and of course there was a lot to celebrate. **At the same time**, leaving university can mean losing the ordinary opportunities to spend time with people. You can no longer assume that you will see a friend simply because you are studying in the same place. For me, the gift brought those two feelings together: happiness about what we had achieved and sadness that a familiar stage of our friendship was coming to an end.

**Since then**, the souvenir has become a reminder of our friendship. After she moved abroad, meeting again soon seemed unlikely, especially while she was building a new life and concentrating on her studies. That distance made the object feel more meaningful. It was something tangible connected with someone who was now far away. **For example**, when I think about the gift, I also think about what it was like to know her before the move, when our lives were organised around the same university rather than different countries.

**Looking back**, I think that explains why some small gifts stay with us. An expensive present can be enjoyable, but a personal one can hold a whole collection of associations. I don't need the souvenir to remind me of every detail of university; it is enough that it brings that friendship to mind. If someone asked why I value it, I would say that it marks both an achievement and a relationship. **That's why** I see it as a keepsake rather than simply an object I was given.

## Linking Language

**By the time** establishes an earlier event; **although / at the same time** introduce contrasting feelings; **since then** connects past and present; **for example** develops a point; **looking back / that's why** introduce reflection and explanation.

## Your Grammar Review

| Reported wording | Correction | Pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “she choose to get a PhD degree” | She **chose to do a PhD**. | A completed decision needs **chose**. The extra word **degree** is redundant, rather than the main grammar error. |
| “She never back the back to China.” | She **hasn't come back to China**. | For a situation continuing up to the reference time: **hasn't + past participle**; **back** alone cannot express the return action here. |
| “There are less chance for me to meet her.” | There **is little chance of seeing her soon**. | **Little chance** describes probability; **fewer opportunities to meet** describes occasions. |

**Vocabulary distinction:** **In France** means in the country; **in French** means using the language.

## Shorten for the Exam

Identify the souvenir and giver, briefly explain the graduation and move, then develop one reason it matters. Omit most of the general discussion of graduation and gift value. Time the shortened version for the official 1–2-minute long turn.
