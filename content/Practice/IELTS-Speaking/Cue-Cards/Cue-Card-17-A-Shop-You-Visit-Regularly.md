# Cue Card 17 — A Shop You Visit Regularly

**Study note:** Added model answer for practice towards IELTS Speaking Bands 7–9, not an examiner-scored response. First-person stories and altered quotations are anonymised study examples. Grammar notes retain qualifications and transcription uncertainty.

## Practice Cue Card

Describe a shop you often visit. Say what kind of shop it is, how you get there, what you buy, and why you return.

## Extended Model Answer

A shop I often visit is a supermarket where I can buy several things I need in one place. I usually go there by car, which is convenient when I have more to carry than I would want to bring home on foot. It isn't a place I value because it feels unusual or luxurious. I keep going back because it fits into my routine and makes an ordinary task easier. When work is busy, having a familiar place for everyday shopping can be surprisingly useful.

The main attraction is the range of products. **For example**, I can use one visit to pick up food and some household supplies instead of making separate trips for each type of item. I would normally begin with the things I actually need and then look around if I have time. **That way**, shopping doesn't have to become a long, open-ended activity. There is still room to compare products, but I have a reason for being there and a clear sense of what would make the visit worthwhile.

**Another reason** I return is familiarity. In a shop you know, you spend less time working out where things are and more time deciding what to buy. A simple shopping trip can involve quite a few small decisions, from comparing quantities to checking whether a product suits your needs. **When the layout is familiar**, some of that effort disappears. I can imagine the difference most clearly on a busy day: finding what I need without wandering through an unfamiliar place makes the task feel much more manageable.

**That said**, convenience doesn't mean I should buy things without thinking. A large range can make it tempting to add items simply because they are available. I find it more sensible to distinguish between something useful and something that only looks attractive for a moment. **For instance**, a special offer isn't necessarily good value if I won't use what I buy. I would rather leave with a smaller number of suitable products than feel pleased about a discount and later realise that I had spent money unnecessarily.

**Overall**, the supermarket is useful because it brings several practical benefits together: a range of goods, a familiar shopping experience and the ability to transport purchases home by car. I still think smaller shops can have advantages, especially when someone wants a specialised product or personal advice. **For my ordinary shopping**, though, being able to complete several errands in one visit matters more. It saves time and reduces the amount of planning involved. That is the main reason a fairly ordinary supermarket has become somewhere I regularly return to.

## Linking Language

**For example / for instance** illustrate; **that way** explains a practical result; **another reason** adds; **that said** qualifies; **overall** sums up; **though** introduces a spoken contrast.

## Your Grammar Review

| Reported wording | Correction | Pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “I will … spending time go there” | I'll **spend some time looking around the shop**. | **Will + base verb**; **spend time + -ing**. |
| “The main reason I keep going that show…” | The main reason I keep **going to that shop is…** | **Go to + place**; a complete **reason…is…** structure. **Show/shop** may be transcription uncertainty. |

**Meaning check:** the transcribed **buy a car** appears to mean **by car** in the journey description. The notes cannot prove whether this was a spoken mistake or a transcription problem.

**Added pattern:** **It's worth visiting**; **it's well worth visiting** is also correct. Do not replace **many** with **various** unless you mean variety rather than quantity.

## Shorten for the Exam

Keep the type of shop, transport, a truthful example of purchases, and two reasons for returning. Remove the extended discussion of discounts and decision-making.
