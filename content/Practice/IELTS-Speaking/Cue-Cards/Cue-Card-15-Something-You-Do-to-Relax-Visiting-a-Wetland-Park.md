# Cue Card 15 — Something You Do to Relax: Visiting a Wetland Park

**Study note:** Added model answer for practice towards IELTS Speaking Bands 7–9, not an examiner-scored response. First-person stories and altered quotations are anonymised study examples. Grammar notes retain qualifications and transcription uncertainty.

## Practice Cue Card

Describe something you do to relax. Say what you do, where and with whom you do it, when you started, and how it helps you.

## Extended Model Answer

One of my favourite ways to relax is to visit a wetland park near my home and go for a walk. It's nearby, so getting there doesn't need to become a major project in itself. I first visited it in [year], after I started working in the city. Since then, the idea of going there has become associated with stepping away from my work routine. I like having an option that feels different from staying indoors but is still simple enough to fit into everyday life.

When I go with friends, we can walk, look at the trees and watch birds. **What makes it relaxing is** that there isn't a complicated objective. We don't need to finish a particular route in a certain time or see every possible part of the park. **Instead**, we can let the walk develop at an easy pace. For someone who spends much of the day responding to tasks and deadlines, it is pleasant to do something where the activity itself is the point, rather than a step towards another result.

The surroundings also give my attention a different kind of work to do. At a desk, I may be looking at information that needs interpreting or a problem that needs solving. In the park, I can notice the movement of a bird or differences between trees without having to turn the observation into a decision. **For example**, watching something for a few moments can be enjoyable even when there is nothing useful to report afterwards. I think that is part of the relief: not every moment has to produce an answer.

**Another advantage** is that walking with friends allows conversation without making conversation compulsory. We can talk about something ordinary, then be quiet for a while without the silence feeling uncomfortable. **Compared with** meeting somewhere noisy, it can be easier to enjoy each other's company at an unhurried pace. Of course, the park won't be equally quiet on every visit, and my mood will vary too. **Even so**, the combination of movement, company and a change of surroundings is something I usually find helpful after a demanding period at work.

I don't think relaxing always requires a holiday or an expensive activity. Those can be enjoyable, but a routine that is easy to repeat can be valuable in a different way. If an activity takes too much planning, I may postpone it until I have more time. **Because** this park is nearby, the barrier is much lower. **Overall**, walking there helps me by changing my pace and giving my attention somewhere else to go. I return with the sense that I have had a break, even though I haven't travelled very far.

## Linking Language

**Instead** contrasts purpose; **for example** makes an abstract point concrete; **another advantage** adds; **compared with / even so** contrast and qualify; **because** explains; **overall** sums up.

## Your Grammar Review

| Reported wording | Correction | Pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “I'm easy to get there” | **It's easy for me to get there.** | **It is + adjective + for someone + to-infinitive**. |
| “three kilometer” | three **kilometres** | Plural unit after three. |
| “I first visit…” referring to [year] | I first **visited** it in [year]. | Past simple for a finished time. |

## Shorten for the Exam

Keep the activity, nearby location and first visit, what you do with friends, and two reasons it helps. Cut the extended comparison between desk work and observation. Keep the focus on relaxation rather than listing a park's attractions.
