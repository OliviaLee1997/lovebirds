# Cue Card 06 — A Useful Website: Hacker News

**Study note:** Added model answer for practice towards IELTS Speaking Bands 7–9, not an examiner-scored response. First-person stories and altered quotations are anonymised study examples. Grammar notes retain qualifications and transcription uncertainty.

## Practice Cue Card

Describe a website you find useful. Say what it is, how you use it, what information it provides, and why it is useful to you.

## Extended Model Answer

A website I find useful is Hacker News, which is a social news site focused mainly on technology and entrepreneurship. I work in software engineering, so I am interested in developments in programming and artificial intelligence. There is far more material online than I could possibly read, and finding a useful starting point can be a challenge in itself. Hacker News helps with that. It gives me a place to see what other people in the technology community are reading and discussing, without having to visit every possible source separately.

The basic idea is straightforward. Users submit links to articles, blog posts and other material, and other users can vote and leave comments. **As a result**, the front page reflects a combination of what has been submitted and what is attracting attention. It isn't simply a collection of articles written by one editorial team. **For example**, a technical explanation on someone's personal blog might appear alongside a company's announcement or a discussion of a new tool. That variety is part of what makes the site useful as a starting point.

When I browse a site like this, I don't need to read every story in full. I can look through the headlines, choose a subject related to my interests and then open the original source. **If something seems particularly relevant**, I can spend more time on it. A post about an AI tool, for instance, could lead me to think about whether the idea has any connection with the kind of engineering work I do. The value isn't that every link will be useful; it is that the site makes relevant possibilities easier to discover.

The comments can add another perspective. Someone may explain a technical limitation, question an assumption or describe a different experience. **At the same time**, I wouldn't treat a popular comment as automatically correct. People have different levels of knowledge, and a confident explanation can still be incomplete. **For that reason**, I see discussions as prompts for further thought rather than final answers. If a claim mattered for a work decision, I would want to check the original documentation or another reliable source before relying on it.

**What I appreciate most is** the time it can save. Instead of searching broadly with no clear direction, I have an initial selection of topics to explore. It also gives me practice reading the way people explain and debate technical ideas in English. **That said**, it could easily become another way of scrolling endlessly, so the benefit depends on using it with a purpose. For me, a useful visit means finding something worth understanding, not reading the largest possible number of posts. That is why I would recommend it to someone with similar interests.

## Linking Language

**As a result / for that reason** express consequences; **for example / for instance** illustrate; **at the same time / that said** introduce limitations. **What I appreciate most is…** is a focus structure rather than a conjunction.

## Your Grammar Review

| Reported wording | Correction | Pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “The website always provided newest things.” | The website **provides the latest news**. | Present simple for a current regular function; **the latest**. Past **provided** is valid if the function belongs to a past period. |
| “I needn't to check every website.” | I **needn't check** every website. / I **don't need to check** every website. | Two different verb patterns; do not combine them. |

**Vocabulary, not a definite grammar diagnosis:** the reported fragment “To know the trend of the This word” is unclear. Practise **keep up with trends in this field** without assuming every transcribed word was spoken that way.

## Shorten for the Exam

Identify the site, explain submitted links and voting, give one use related to your work, and explain the time-saving benefit. Reduce the discussion of comment reliability to one sentence.
