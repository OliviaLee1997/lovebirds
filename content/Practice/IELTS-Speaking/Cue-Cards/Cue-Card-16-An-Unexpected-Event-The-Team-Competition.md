# Cue Card 16 — An Unexpected Event: The Team Competition

**Study note:** Added model answer for practice towards IELTS Speaking Bands 7–9, not an examiner-scored response. First-person stories and altered quotations are anonymised study examples. Grammar notes retain qualifications and transcription uncertainty.

## Practice Cue Card

Describe something unexpected that happened to you. Say when it happened, what you had planned, what changed, and how it turned out.

## Extended Model Answer

An unexpected experience that stands out happened when I was at university. I was taking part in a student team competition in another region with a team of other students. The work involved building a car, designing circuits and explaining our design. It was a technical challenge, but the part I remember most vividly is not a particular circuit or presentation. It is the way a phone call changed my holiday plans and led to a much more rewarding outcome than I expected at the time.

During the summer holiday, I had already bought a ticket to go home when a teacher called and asked me to return to the university for the next stage. **Until that point**, I had been thinking about the journey home. **Suddenly**, I had to change direction and work out how to get back. I wasn't especially pleased about having my plans interrupted. Once you have mentally moved into holiday mode, it can be difficult to become enthusiastic about an unexpected demand, even when you understand why it matters.

Getting back was difficult because train tickets were scarce. My teammates and I ended up travelling overnight without seats, and we didn't get any sleep. **By the time** we arrived, we were exhausted and not in a particularly good mood. That part of the story is important because the experience didn't feel inspiring while we were going through it. It felt inconvenient and tiring. **At that stage**, I don't think we could have looked at the journey alone and predicted how positively we would remember the whole experience later.

**Despite that difficult start**, our team went on to win an award for the project. The result also encouraged me to keep developing my skills and explore further learning. That gave the achievement significance beyond the competition itself. I was pleased with what we had accomplished as a team, and I also realised that the experience could influence what I wanted to learn next. **What had begun as** an unwelcome change of plan had turned into an experience with consequences I had not anticipated when the teacher first called.

**Looking back**, I wouldn't say that every disrupted plan leads to something good. Sometimes an inconvenience remains an inconvenience. What this experience taught me was more specific: my initial reaction didn't tell me how the story would end. I had felt disappointed and tired, but those feelings were only part of the process. **In the end**, the unexpected return became a meaningful memory because of the contrast between the difficult journey and the result. It reminds me to leave some room for an outcome that is better than my first impression of a situation.

## Linking Language

**Until that point / suddenly / by the time / at that stage** organise a story; **despite** introduces a noun phrase; **looking back / in the end** move to reflection and outcome.

## Your Grammar Review

| Reported wording | Correction | Pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “it was happened” | It **happened**. | No passive **was** with **happen** here. |
| “we don't have a seat” in the train story | We **didn't have any seats**. | Finished journey: past simple. |
| “back to the university” as a full clause | We **went back to the university**. | Include a subject and movement verb. |
| “The event was sudden happen” | It **happened unexpectedly**. | Main verb + adverb. **Suddenly** describes suddenness; **unexpectedly** describes lack of expectation. |

**Already correct:** **We didn't have any choice.** **We had no choice** is an alternative. **Award** versus **reward** is a vocabulary distinction, not a tense error.

## Shorten for the Exam

Keep the illustrative holiday plan, unexpected call, overnight train, award and personal reaction. Reduce technical background and use one sentence for the motivation to continue learning.
