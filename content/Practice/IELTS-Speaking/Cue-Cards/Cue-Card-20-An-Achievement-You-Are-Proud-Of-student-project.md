# Cue Card 20 — An Achievement You Are Proud Of: student project

**Study note:** Added model answer for practice towards IELTS Speaking Bands 7–9, not an examiner-scored response. First-person stories and altered quotations are anonymised study examples. Grammar notes retain qualifications and transcription uncertainty.

**Additional practice topic:** another way to develop your existing experiences and interests.

## Practice Cue Card

Describe an achievement you are proud of. Say what it was, when and where it happened, what challenges were involved, and why it matters.

## Extended Model Answer

An achievement I am proud of is winning an award at the student project competition in another city in [year]. The competition involved technical project work, so it was an opportunity to take academic interests into a setting beyond ordinary coursework. Receiving the award was obviously a rewarding outcome, but the experience matters to me for another reason as well. It showed me that having knowledge and being able to communicate that knowledge are connected abilities, and that I needed to keep developing both of them.

At the time, my English was limited, particularly when I needed to communicate in a demanding situation. A female classmate helped by interpreting from Chinese into English for me. **Because of her help**, I could take part more effectively than I would have managed alone. I am grateful for that support. It is easy to describe an achievement as something you personally accomplished, but doing so can hide the practical help that made it possible. Her contribution is an important part of how I remember the experience, not a minor detail.

**What made the communication challenging** was the technical nature of the subject. Even if you know an idea well in your first language, explaining it in another language requires decisions about vocabulary, structure and how much background the listener needs. **For example**, knowing the correct technical term is only one part of the task. You also have to connect it to a clear explanation. If the listener asks a question, you need enough flexibility to explain the point in another way rather than simply repeat the same sentence.

**Although** I was proud of the result, the experience didn't mean that I wanted to spend my entire career doing biological research. I later realised at university that research work wasn't the direction that interested me most. I don't see that as making the competition less valuable. **On the contrary**, an experience can be worthwhile partly because it helps you understand your interests more clearly. It can show you what you are capable of while also helping you decide which aspects of the work you would like to continue and which you would rather leave behind.

**Looking back**, the award represents achievement, but the broader memory includes cooperation, communication and self-discovery. I now work in a technical field, and discussing technical subjects in English is still something I want to improve. The competition gives that goal a personal meaning: I know what it feels like to have something to contribute but need help expressing it. **That's why** I remain proud of the experience. It was a successful result, and it also gave me a clearer understanding of a skill that could make future opportunities more accessible.

## Linking Language

**Because of** introduces a noun phrase expressing cause; **for example** develops; **although** concedes; **on the contrary** directly counters the preceding negative idea; **looking back / that's why** reflect and explain.
