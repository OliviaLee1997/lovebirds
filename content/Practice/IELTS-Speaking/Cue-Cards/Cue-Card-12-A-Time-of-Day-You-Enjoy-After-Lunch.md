# Cue Card 12 — A Time of Day You Enjoy: After Lunch

**Study note:** Added model answer for practice towards IELTS Speaking Bands 7–9, not an examiner-scored response. First-person stories and altered quotations are anonymised study examples. Grammar notes retain qualifications and transcription uncertainty.

## Practice Cue Card

Describe a time of day you enjoy. Say when it is, what you do, who you spend it with, and why you enjoy it.

## Extended Model Answer

My favourite time of day is the break after lunch. It may not sound like a particularly exciting answer, but I value having a small space between the two parts of the working day. By that point, I have usually spent several hours concentrating, and a change of surroundings can make a real difference to how I feel. I don't need the break to include anything special. What I enjoy is the chance to stop thinking about the next task for a while and pay attention to something else.

**If I have enough time**, I like to go for a walk in a nearby park. Sometimes the walk lasts around an hour, although that depends on the time available. I enjoy listening to the birds and noticing what is around me. **Compared with** sitting in the same place throughout the day, walking gives the break a clearer beginning and end. I have physically stepped away from work, which makes it easier to feel that I am taking a proper pause rather than simply waiting for the next message.

I'm usually on my own during this time, and I like the peace and quiet. Being alone doesn't necessarily mean feeling lonely. In this situation, it means I can choose my own pace and let my attention move naturally from one thing to another. **Occasionally**, I chat with people from different parts of the country, and that can be enjoyable too. The important thing is that the interaction feels unhurried. It is different from a conversation where I need to explain a problem, make a decision or produce an immediate answer.

**On other days**, I might listen to music or watch a few videos. Those activities can provide a change of mood, although I don't think looking at a screen always gives me the same kind of rest as a walk. **For example**, there is a difference between deliberately choosing a song I like and letting a stream of unrelated videos fill all the available time. I would rather finish the break feeling that I had enjoyed it than realise it had disappeared without my really noticing what I was doing.

**By the time** I return to work, I often feel refreshed and more able to focus. I wouldn't claim that a lunch break solves every problem or removes a heavy workload. **Even so**, it can change the way I approach the rest of the day. It gives me something small and pleasant to look forward to, and it reminds me that a working day can contain moments that belong to me. **That's why** I enjoy this period so much: it is an ordinary part of my routine that makes the whole routine feel more manageable.

## Linking Language

**If / although** introduce conditions and limits; **compared with** contrasts; **occasionally / on other days** organise a routine; **for example** illustrates; **by the time** marks a later point; **even so / that's why** connect evaluation and reason.

## Your Grammar Review

| Reported wording | Correction | Pattern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “listening the bird sing” | **listening to the birds sing/singing** | The essential repair is **listen to**. Both perception-verb endings can work. |
| “from different area” | from different **areas** | Plural when several areas are intended. |
| “talk with some peoples” | talk with some **people** | Normal plural of **person**; **talk with** is already correct. |

**Already correct:** **I take a walk** and **I am usually alone**. They do not need grammatical correction.

## Shorten for the Exam

Keep when the break happens, the park walk, whether you are alone, and how you feel afterwards. Remove the extended discussion of screen use.
