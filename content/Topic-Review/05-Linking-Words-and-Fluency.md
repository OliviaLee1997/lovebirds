# Linking Words & Fluency

**Course basis:** Lessons 01, 04, 08–12, 14, 15, and 17–19. Lessons 04 and 15 provide the main connector groups; Lessons 09–12 provide speaking-organisation practice.

**Source distinction:** This document consolidates the existing explanations and adds fresh practice. Model paragraphs are study material, not quotations or personal history. The purpose is to express relationships clearly, not to add a connector to every sentence.

## 1. Organise the Idea Before Choosing the Phrase

Useful shapes from the course include:

- **Description:** identify → detail → example → personal meaning.
- **Story:** setting → change or problem → response → outcome.
- **Opinion:** position → reason → example → qualification.
- **Problem discussion:** situation → cause → possible solution.

Plan with keywords. If two sentences have no clear relationship, a sophisticated linking word will not create one.

## 2. Contrast and Concession

| Expression | Structure | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **but** | clause + but + clause | **The park is small, but it has plenty of shade.** |
| **however** | sentence boundary + however + clause | **The park is nearby. However, it gets crowded.** |
| **although / even though** | introduce a clause | **Although it was crowded, we enjoyed the visit.** |
| **whereas** | compare contrasting clauses | **I prefer paper books, whereas my friend prefers e-books.** |
| **that said** | introduce a qualification | **The app is useful. That said, I sometimes find it distracting.** |

In ordinary written English, avoid **Although…, but…** for the same connection. Avoid joining complete sentences with only a comma before **however**: use a full stop or semicolon.

## 3. Reasons and Results

- **because / since / as + clause:** **I stayed indoors because it was raining.**
- **because of / due to + noun phrase:** **The delay was due to heavy rain.**
- **so + result clause:** **It was raining, so we stayed indoors.**
- **as a result / consequently / thus:** introduce the result with suitable sentence punctuation.

**Because of working late** is a possible **-ing** phrase, though a complete **because** clause can be clearer. **Due to the fact that…** is possible but often unnecessarily long.

**So** is common in conversation; **consequently** and **thus** tend to sound more formal. Use **because** or **so** for one reason-result connection rather than **Because…, so…**.

**Check direction:** **I was tired because I slept badly** is not the same causal claim as **I slept badly because I was tired**.

## 4. Addition, Examples, and Exact Identification

| Function | Useful expressions | Important distinction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Add information | **also / in addition / besides** | Match the tone and sentence structure. |
| Add a noun or activity | **in addition to / besides + noun or -ing** | **In addition to reading, I practise speaking.** |
| Introduce an example | **for example / for instance** | Can introduce a full clause. |
| Introduce example items | **such as** | **Activities such as walking and swimming**. |
| Identify exactly | **namely** | **Two skills, namely listening and speaking**. |

**Beside** means next to. **Besides** means in addition to or introduces an extra point. **Namely** identifies the specific items; it does not merely offer a few possible examples.

## 5. Time, Sequence, and Outcome

**First / next / then / after that** move through steps. **Meanwhile** describes an overlapping activity. **Immediately** means without delay.

- **In the end:** the final outcome of a situation.
- **Eventually:** something happens after time, delay, or difficulty.
- **Finally:** can mark a last point or an event after waiting, depending on context.
- **Overall / to sum up:** a general assessment or summary.

**Eventually** is not a general synonym for **to sum up**. An outcome and a summary perform different jobs.

> **First**, we checked the route. **Then**, we bought tickets. The train was delayed, but **eventually** it arrived. **In the end**, we reached the venue on time. **Overall**, the journey was tiring but manageable.

This is an added illustration of the functions, not a script requiring all five expressions every time.

## 6. Conditions, Precautions, and Hypotheticals

**Lesson 15.**

| Expression | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **if** | condition | **I'll go if the weather is pleasant.** |
| **as long as / provided that** | required condition | **We can visit as long as the path is open.** |
| **in case** | precaution against a possibility | **I'll take a jacket in case it gets cold.** |
| **even if** | outcome holds despite the condition | **I'll keep practising even if progress is slow.** |
| **suppose / supposing that** | introduce an imagined situation | **Suppose you had more free time. What would you learn?** |

**If it rains** and **in case it rains** are not interchangeable. **I'll take an umbrella in case it rains** describes taking a precaution before knowing whether it will rain.

For ordinary likely future conditions, use **if it rains**, not **if it will rain**. The practice rule concerns this use, not every possible meaning of **will** in an if-clause.

## 7. Emphasis and Clarification

**In fact** can add a stronger detail or correct an assumption. **Particularly** highlights one aspect. **Indeed** reinforces a statement and can sound formal. **Obviously** assumes something is clear and may sound dismissive when it is debatable.

> **I particularly like the quieter paths. In fact, I sometimes choose a longer route because it is less crowded.**

Use strong words such as **unquestionably** only when the level of certainty suits the claim.

## 8. Thinking, Repairing, and Keeping the Conversation Moving

| Need | Natural options |
| --- | --- |
| Brief thinking time | **Let me think for a moment. / One example that comes to mind is…** |
| A tentative view | **I'd say… / I suppose… / It seems to me…** |
| Limited knowledge | **As far as I know… / I haven't considered that in detail, but…** |
| A correction | **I mean… / More precisely… / Let me put that another way.** |
| Clarification from the listener | **Do you mean…? / Could you explain that last part?** |

A short pause is normal. You do not need to fill every silence. **To be honest** introduces candour; **actually** often corrects an expectation. They are not empty words to repeat at the start of every answer.

If you notice a small error, correct the relevant words and continue. **“I go—sorry, I went there last week”** is usually easier to follow than repeatedly restarting the whole story.

## 9. Develop Rather Than Pad

**General:** **The place is relaxing.**

**Developed:** **The place is relaxing because there are shaded paths away from the main road. I usually walk slowly there, which helps me stop thinking about work for a while.**

The additional value comes from **a concrete feature and its effect**. Long introductions, repeated reasons, and unnecessary formal conclusions add little.

## 10. Review Practice

1. Correct: **Although it was difficult, but I continued.**
2. Complete: **We stayed home because of ___.** Give a noun phrase.
3. Which means precaution: **if** or **in case**?
4. Is **eventually** a general replacement for **to sum up**?
5. Correct the punctuation: **The shop is convenient, however it is expensive.**
6. Give one example of **namely** identifying exact items.

**Answers:** 1. **Although it was difficult, I continued.** 2. For example, **the heavy rain**. 3. **In case**. 4. **No**. 5. **The shop is convenient. However, it is expensive.** 6. For example, **I practised two skills, namely listening and speaking.**

**Speaking task:** Explain a reading preference using one reason, one example, and one qualification. Use only the connectors your meaning needs.
