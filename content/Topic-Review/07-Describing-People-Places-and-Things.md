## 2. A Flexible Description Structure

**Identify → give relevant background → develop two specific details → explain personal meaning.**

Useful openings:

- **I'd like to describe… / One example that comes to mind is…**
- **I first came across it… / I've known them since…**
- **What stands out is… / What I particularly appreciate is…**
- **For example,… / One detail I remember is…**

Choose one `/` alternative at a time. Avoid giving a long generic introduction before naming your example.

## 3. Describe a Person Through Evidence

**Lesson 11: person → relationship → quality → evidence → importance.**

> **I'd like to talk about [person], whom I've known for [duration]. What I particularly admire is [quality]. For example, [an action that shows the quality]. That matters to me because [personal effect].**

**Added model:**

> A person I respect is a former teacher. She was patient without making the lessons feel slow. If someone struggled with an explanation, she would find a different example rather than simply repeat the same words. That helped me feel comfortable asking questions, and I still value that approach when I'm learning something unfamiliar.

**Useful language:** **get on well with / have a lot in common / turn to someone for advice / remain dedicated to teaching / support each other**.

**Grammar:** **The person I respect**, not **the person I am respected**. **She was my teacher and still teaches** can correctly combine past and present.

## 4. Describe a Place Through Location, Atmosphere, and Use

**Lessons 03, 11, 15, 18, and 19.**

> **A place I enjoy visiting is [place], which is [location]. I first went there [when]. It has [specific features]. I usually [activity], and I particularly like [detail] because [effect].**

**Details to develop:** paths, light, sounds, layout, activities, access, and how a visit feels. **Beautiful**, **interesting**, and **convenient** need support.

**Added model:**

> A place I like is a small park near my home. It's easy to reach, and I usually go when I have time for a slow walk. The trees provide shade, and the paths are set back from the road. I particularly enjoy hearing birds instead of traffic. For me, the appeal is having somewhere nearby where I can stop rushing for a while.

**Meaning checks:** **It's easy for me to get there**; **rent a boat**, if temporary use is intended; **traditional villages** for settlements and **villagers** for people.

For a visitor attraction, distinguish **what you personally saw**, **what the site offers**, and **what you learned later**. Do not turn a general fact about pandas into a memory of seeing a particular animal.

## 5. Describe an Object, Gift, or Photograph

**Lessons 10 and 15.**

| Topic | Develop these points | Natural language |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Gift** | What it is; who gave it; occasion; use; meaning. | **It was given to me… / I use it for… / It has sentimental value.** |
| **Useful object** | Acquisition; features; practical example; limitation. | **The feature I use most is… / It helps me…** |
| **Photograph** | What it shows; when/where/how taken; memories; value now. | **It shows… / It was taken… / It reminds me of…** |

**Photo tense sequence:** **The picture shows four people. We were standing near a gate. I've kept the photo for years.** Each tense serves a different time relationship.

**Added photo model:**

> One photograph I value shows a small group of friends standing outside a school. What I like is how relaxed everyone looks. The picture reminds me of an ordinary part of that period rather than a major celebration. I've kept it because it brings back details that might otherwise be easy to forget.

Do not add **on graduation day** merely because a photo is associated with leaving school. Use only the occasion the source or your memory supports.

## 6. Describe a Website or a Shop Through a Concrete Use

**Lessons 10 and 19.**

**Identify → need → how you use it → example → evaluation.**

- Website: **I mainly use it to [action]. Recently, it helped me [specific result].**
- Shop: **I keep going back because [reason]. I can buy [items] in one place.**

**Convenient** becomes clearer when you specify saved journeys, easy search, a useful range, or a simple process. **Multiple products** describes quantity; **a wide range of products** describes variety.

A useful website answer should explain the user's activity, not just repeat the site's name. A shop answer should identify what makes the particular shop suitable for you.

## 7. Describe an Activity, Time of Day, or Song

| Topic | Useful sequence | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Sport or hobby** | How it began → usual routine → enjoyable aspect → benefit. | Listing benefits without describing what you actually do. |
| **Time of day** | When → routine → atmosphere → why it suits you. | Turning a preference into a rule for everyone. |
| **Song** | Identify → first encounter → musical or lyrical feature → personal response. | Retelling unverified lyrics or assuming a literal factual message. |

**Swimming example:** **Swimming is one of my favourite sports because it helps me concentrate on the activity. I can't keep checking messages while I'm swimming, so it gives me a break from my phone.** This is a personal benefit; it does not mean swimmers never sweat or other sports are inherently distracting.

**Song example:** **What I like most is the steady rhythm and the way the chorus builds. I tend to listen to it when I need encouragement.** This is added illustrative wording, not a claim about a particular recording.

## 8. Describe a Picture: Observation Before Interpretation

**Lessons 08 and 09.**

1. **Overall scene:** **The picture shows…**
2. **Position:** **In the foreground / in the background / on the left…**
3. **Visible action:** **They are holding books / walking / talking.**
4. **Cautious interpretation:** **They might be… / It looks as though…**
5. **Response or wider connection:** **This makes me think about…**

> **The picture shows several people sitting around a table. They are looking at books, and one person appears to be explaining something. They might be studying together. I like this kind of arrangement when everyone has a chance to contribute.**

A smile does not establish its cause. Clothing or a room does not prove someone's occupation, diagnosis, income, or nationality. Use uncertainty where the image does not establish the fact.

## 9. Review Practice

Match each detail to its purpose:

1. **It's a short walk from home.**
2. **She stayed to explain the difficult part.**
3. **The photo was taken outside the school.**
4. **They might be preparing for an exam.**

**Answers:** 1. **Access/location**. 2. **Evidence of a quality**. 3. **Photo background**. 4. **Inference, not direct observation**.

**Speaking tasks:** Describe one person, one place, and one item using four keywords each. Add one concrete detail before giving your overall opinion. For an IELTS Part 2 simulation, use one minute to prepare and speak for one to two minutes; longer practice is an extension exercise, not the exam timing.
