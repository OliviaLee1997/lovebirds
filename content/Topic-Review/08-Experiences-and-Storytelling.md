# Experiences & Storytelling

**Privacy note:** Personal and third-party details have been removed or generalised. First-person stories and altered quotations are anonymised study examples, not a biographical record or verbatim transcript.

**Course basis:** Lessons 05–07, 09, 11, 12, 17, and 18.

**Source distinction:** The story inventory reflects reported course topics. Examples below are edited or newly written practice, not a reconstructed recording. Where the source has uncertain dates, roles, or outcomes, those uncertainties must remain visible rather than being filled with invented details.

## 1. The Story Types Practised

| Story type | Course material | Lessons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Everyday sequence** | Activities happening together, plans, a ride or another completed experience. | 05, 06 |
| **Achievement** | Competition participation, an award, or education and career progress. | 07, 09, 18 |
| **Challenge and encouragement** | A difficult school experience and rebuilding confidence. | 09 |
| **Travel experience** | A recent visit, unfamiliar surroundings, concern and reaction. | 11 |
| **Good news** | Receiving news, understanding the plan, and reacting. | 12 |
| **An embarrassing experience** | An unannounced visit to a former teacher who did not initially recognise the visitor. | 17 |
| **An unexpected change** | Changed holiday plans, a difficult overnight journey, and a competition outcome. | 18 |

These are story purposes, not fixed scripts. An enjoyable experience does not always need a problem, and an embarrassing event does not have to end in a dramatic life lesson.

## 2. Build a Clear Narrative

**Setting → trigger → response → outcome → reflection.**

| Step | Question to answer | Useful language |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Setting | When, where, and who? | **This happened when… / At the time, I was…** |
| Trigger | What changed or mattered? | **Then… / The unexpected part was…** |
| Response | What did you do? | **My first step was… / I decided to…** |
| Outcome | What happened as a result? | **Eventually… / In the end…** |
| Reflection | Why does it stay with you? | **Looking back… / Since then, I've…** |

Choose the details that explain the event. An answer about unexpected news needs the news and reaction; it does not need a full history of every person involved.

## 3. Use Tense Changes to Show Perspective

> **I was getting ready to leave when a friend called. I had already booked a ticket, so I needed to decide whether to change my plans. I checked the options and called back. Looking back, I'm glad we talked before making a decision.**

- **Was getting ready:** background in progress.
- **Called / checked:** main past events.
- **Had booked:** an earlier event viewed from that past moment.
- **I'm glad:** present evaluation.

Past perfect does not require a long time gap. With **before** and **after**, simple past may already make the order clear. Use the tense that expresses the relationship rather than trying to make every sentence more complex.

## 4. Make Feelings Specific

**Weak:** **I felt bad.**

**Developed:** **I felt embarrassed because I had expected him to recognise me immediately.**

| Feeling | Typical reason |
| --- | --- |
| **embarrassed** | An awkward social moment or concern about how you appeared. |
| **disappointed** | The result did not meet your expectation. |
| **frustrated** | Something blocked your effort or progress. |
| **relieved** | A worry or difficulty eased. |
| **proud** | An achievement or something you value. |
| **grateful** | Someone's help or kindness. |

The emotions are not interchangeable substitutes for **happy** or **sad**. Explain the cause with **because**, or describe a reaction that makes the feeling understandable.

## 5. Good News: News, Details, Reaction, Reason

**Lesson 12.**

> **I received some good news [when]. [Person] told me that [news]. At first, I felt [reaction], because [context]. What made the news especially meaningful was [reason].**

**Added anonymous example:**

> Last week, a friend told me she had been accepted onto a course she really wanted to take. She'd spent a lot of time preparing, so I was pleased that her effort had paid off. I also felt relieved for her because waiting for the result had been stressful. It was good to hear her sounding excited about the next step.

**Language checks:** **some good news**, not **a good news**; **glad to hear**, not **glad to hearing**. **She told me she was planning…** and **she told me she is planning…** can both be appropriate depending on whether you preserve a current viewpoint.

## 6. Embarrassment: Explain the Expectation That Failed

**Lesson 17.** The useful core is **relationship → expectation → unexpected reaction → response → reflection**.

> **I went to see someone I hadn't spoken to for a long time. I hadn't contacted them beforehand, and they didn't recognise me at first. I felt awkward because I had assumed the connection would be obvious. Once I explained who I was, the conversation became easier. Looking back, a short message in advance would have been more considerate.**

This is an added generalised version of the narrative pattern, not a replacement factual record.

**Source limits:** The original summary contains inconsistent calendar references and uncertainty about whether the encounter was accidental or an unannounced visit. Use broad, supported time markers rather than inventing a year. **Meet** can be intentional or accidental; add **by chance** only if that is what happened.

## 7. Unexpected Plans and an Achievement

**Lesson 18.**

**Supported sequence:** a ticket already bought → unexpected request to return → changed plans → overnight journey without seats → exhaustion → competition award → reported educational opportunity.

**Flexible framework:**

> **I had planned to [original plan], but [unexpected event]. That meant [practical problem]. We decided to [response], although [difficulty]. In the end, [actual outcome]. What surprised me was [contrast with expectations].**

**Useful expressions:** **at short notice / change my plans / take an overnight train / have no choice but to / win an award / be considered for a programme**.

**Accuracy checks:**

- **We didn't have any choice** is grammatical; **we had no choice** is shorter.
- **Competition / contest** identifies the event; **rivalry** identifies a competitive relationship.
- **An opportunity to be considered for admission** is different from confirmed admission.
- Do not add an award rank, named examination exemption, or official rule absent from the source.

## 8. Challenges and Other People's Advice

**Lessons 07 and 09.** Describe what was said or done, how you reacted, and what changed. Avoid turning one person's discouraging comment into a universal conclusion about teachers, classmates, or education.

Useful language:

- **At first, I lost confidence.**
- **Their encouragement helped me keep going.**
- **I gradually became more confident.**
- **I learned to think carefully before accepting advice.**
- **The result mattered to me because…**

**Pattern:** **suggest doing / suggest that someone do something**. For example, **She suggested trying a different approach**, not **she suggested me to try** in standard English.

An honest outcome can include a mixed result or an unresolved problem. Do not invent a success simply because a template has a final “achievement” slot.

## 9. Keep the Timeline and Facts Reviewable

Before speaking, write four or five keywords in order. Check:

1. Does the order make sense?
2. Have I distinguished the earlier background from the main event?
3. Are exact years or durations actually supported?
4. Am I describing what happened, or my interpretation of why it happened?
5. Does the ending answer the question's emotional or reflective focus?

**Retelling is not the same as memorising a script.** Practise the same event with different wording while preserving its meaning and sequence.

## 10. Review Practice

Correct or assess:

1. The incident was happened unexpectedly.
2. I received a good news.
3. I didn't call before I went.
4. We won a national rivalry.
5. The situation made me embarrassment.

**Answers:** 1. **The incident happened unexpectedly.** 2. **I received some good news / a piece of good news.** 3. **Grammatical; past perfect is optional when it fits the viewpoint.** 4. **We won a student competition / an award for the project**, depending on the actual result. 5. **The situation made me feel embarrassed.**

**Speaking task:** Tell an unexpected experience once from memory. Retell it with a different opening and one clearer detail. Then answer **“What would you do differently now?”** without restarting the entire story.
