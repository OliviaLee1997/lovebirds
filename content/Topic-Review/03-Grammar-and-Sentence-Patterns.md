# Grammar & Sentence Patterns

**Course basis:** Recurring corrections in Lessons 01–03, 05, and 07–19. The patterns below consolidate repeated issues rather than listing every transcript fragment.

**Source distinction:** Rules and examples are edited study material based on the existing notes. A reported correction is not automatically evidence of a repeated personal weakness. Hesitations, uncertain transcriptions, and a tutor's alternative wording require separate treatment.

## 1. Articles, Countability, and Plurals

| Pattern | Example | Course connection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **a/an + singular countable noun** | **a pet / an experience / an engineer** | Lessons 01–03, 19 |
| **number + plural noun** | **two people / three kilometres** | Lessons 02, 18 |
| **one of + plural noun** | **one of my favourite sports** | Lesson 17 |
| **some + uncountable noun** | **some advice / some good news / some information** | Lessons 09, 12, 17 |
| **a unit of + uncountable noun** | **a piece of advice / a piece of news** | Consolidated practice |

Choose **a/an** by sound: **an hour**, but **a university**. Use **a child / children**, not **a children**.

**Work** is normally uncountable for employment or activity: **I worked all day / I had work all day**. Use **a job** or **a piece of work** for the intended countable meaning.

**People** is the ordinary plural of **person**. **Peoples** can refer to distinct populations; it is not the normal word for several individuals in a park.

**Distance versus modifier:** **three kilometres away**, but **a three-kilometre walk**.

## 2. Agreement and Quantifiers

- **The file is not opening. / The files are not opening.**
- **The shop sells groceries. / The shops sell groceries.**
- **The pet industry is growing. / Pet-related businesses are growing.**
- **A number of students are waiting. / The number of students is increasing.**

**Many / several / a number of** normally introduce plural countable nouns. **Much / a great deal of** introduce uncountable nouns. **A lot of** works with both.

**Other / another:** **other people**, **other information**, **another person**, **the other person**, **my other friend**. **Another two days** is also possible for an additional amount.

## 3. Questions: Choose the Right Auxiliary

| Statement | Question |
| --- | --- |
| **She is outgoing.** | **Is she outgoing?** |
| **She likes reading.** | **Does she like reading?** |
| **They visited yesterday.** | **Did they visit yesterday?** |
| **You have finished.** | **Have you finished?** |

Use a base verb after **do/does/did**: **Does she like…?**, not **Does she likes…?** Do not add **does** to **Is opera a form of art?**

**Meaning contrasts:** **What is she like?** asks about character; **What does she like?** asks about preferences. **Where do you live?** and **Which city do you live in?** use different structures.

## 4. Verb Patterns Worth Learning as Whole Chunks

| Pattern | Correct example |
| --- | --- |
| **enjoy + -ing** | **I enjoy swimming.** |
| **want + to + base verb** | **I want to improve my speaking.** |
| **start + -ing / to-infinitive** | **I started learning / started to learn.** |
| **be good at + noun/-ing** | **She's good at explaining things.** |
| **be interested in + noun/-ing** | **I'm interested in learning languages.** |
| **spend time + -ing** | **I spend time comparing prices.** |
| **spend time/money on + noun** | **I spend money on books.** |
| **be worth + -ing/noun** | **It's worth visiting. / It was worth the effort.** |
| **glad + to + base verb** | **I'm glad to hear that.** |
| **suggest + -ing / that-clause** | **She suggested waiting. / She suggested that I wait.** |
| **listen to + sound source** | **I listen to music.** |
| **access + object** | **I can't access my email.** |

**Worth** does not take **to visit** in this pattern. **Time well spent** and **well worth visiting** are valid; the problem is the particular combination **it's well spending**.

**Perception pattern:** **I listened to the birds sing** and **I listened to the birds singing** are both grammatical. The base form can present the event as a whole; **-ing** highlights activity in progress.

## 5. Let, Make, Help, and Consider

| Pattern | Example |
| --- | --- |
| **let + person + base verb** | **Let me explain.** |
| **make + person + base verb** | **The interruption made me lose my place.** |
| **make + object + adjective** | **This makes the task easier.** |
| **help + person + (to) + base verb** | **Practice helps me speak / helps me to speak clearly.** |
| **consider + object + adjective** | **I consider the example useful.** |

**Let me concentrate**, not **let me concentrated**. **Make me feel embarrassed** is natural; **make me embarrassment** does not fit the intended structure.

## 6. Ease, Difficulty, and Complete Clauses

**Lesson 18:**

- **It's easy for me to get there.**
- **I can get there easily.**
- **The park is easy to reach.**

**I'm easy to get there** does not express that travel is easy for me. However, **I'm easy to contact** is valid: contacting me is easy for other people.

Include a verb where needed: **When I am on the boat…**. That is still a dependent clause; finish it: **When I am on the boat, I feel relaxed.**

For neutral complete sentences, avoid accidental repeated subjects: **My friend is a teacher.** Conversational structures such as **My friend, she's a teacher** are possible, so “one sentence can only have one subject” is not a reliable universal rule.

## 7. Active and Passive Meaning

| Intended meaning | Form |
| --- | --- |
| Something happened. | **The event happened. / The incident occurred.** |
| A system has several parts. | **It consists of several parts. / It is composed of several parts.** |
| Someone took a photograph. | **The photograph was taken near the entrance.** |
| A process is changing jobs. | **Some jobs are being changed by technology.** |

**Happen** and **occur** are intransitive here: not **was happened / was occurred**. **Is consist of** is not a valid verb form. Passive voice is useful when the verb supports it and the affected thing is the focus.

## 8. Comparisons, Word Forms, and Parallel Structure

- **clean → cleaner**, not **more cleaner**.
- **beautiful → more beautiful**, not **more beauty**.
- Compare equivalent things: **The price here is lower than the price there.**
- **similar to**, **different from**, and **have things in common with** are useful whole phrases.

**Shared pattern:** **I enjoy reading, swimming, and spending time outdoors.** All activities depend on **enjoy**.

**Separate clauses:** **I enjoy swimming, and I spend time outdoors at weekends.** This is also correct. An earlier **-ing** form does not require every later verb to end in **-ing**.

## 9. Small Prepositions and Fixed Expressions

**At home / go home / return home**; **go to school / return to the university**; **in class**; **by car**; **on foot**; **depend on**; **remind someone of something**.

**Back** needs a movement verb in **went back to the university**, but it can be a verb meaning support in **I back your proposal**.

**Hope and wish:** **I hope you have a good time / Have a good time / I wish you a pleasant trip.** Do not repair **I wish you have…** by merely deleting **have** without considering the intended pattern.

## 10. Review Practice

Correct only what needs correction:

1. It's worth to visit.
2. One of my favourite sport is swimming.
3. Let me explained.
4. The event was happened last year.
5. I spent an hour reading.
6. I need another information.
7. The number of visitors are increasing.
8. I'm easy to contact.

**Answers:** 1. **It's worth visiting.** 2. **One of my favourite sports is swimming.** 3. **Let me explain.** 4. **The event happened last year.** 5. **Correct.** 6. **I need some other information / another piece of information.** 7. **The number of visitors is increasing.** 8. **Correct.**

**Speaking practice:** Give a short answer about a hobby using **one of**, **enjoy**, **helps me**, and **worth**. Check the structure after each expression before adding more detail.
