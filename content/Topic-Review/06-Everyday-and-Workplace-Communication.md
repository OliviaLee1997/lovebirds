# Everyday & Workplace Communication

**Privacy note:** Personal and third-party details have been removed or generalised. First-person stories and altered quotations are anonymised study examples, not a biographical record or verbatim transcript.

**Course basis:** Lessons 01–07, 16, and 19.

**Study purpose:** Handle practical conversations: introductions, clarification, workplace problems, requests, shopping, and arrangements.

**Source distinction:** These scenarios draw on the course themes. Dialogues are reconstructed or newly written study material, not recordings of real exchanges. Personal names, employers, and detailed schedules are omitted from the practice examples.

## 1. Introductions and Learning Goals

**Lesson 01.** A useful answer gives a clear fact and one relevant detail.

| Question | Flexible answer |
| --- | --- |
| **What do you do?** | **I work in [field]. My role mainly involves [an -ing phrase].** |
| **Why are you learning English?** | **I'd like to [goal], especially when [situation].** |
| **What do you find difficult?** | **I find [activity in the -ing form] challenging because [reason].** |
| **How long have you been learning?** | **I've been learning for [duration], although [relevant context].** |
| **What do you do in your free time?** | **I usually [activity]. It helps me [benefit].** |

**Added example:** **I'm learning English so I can take part in conversations more comfortably. Reading gives me time to think, but live conversation requires a quicker response.**

Describe your own experience. A sentence repeated during class does not automatically establish your job, travel history, or qualifications.

## 2. Ask for Clarification Instead of Guessing

**Lessons 01 and 02.**

- **How do you pronounce this word?**
- **How do you say that in English?**
- **Could you repeat the last part, please?**
- **Could you speak a little more slowly?**
- **Did you say you can access it or you can't?**
- **Do you mean the file won't open, or that you can't find it?**

**Repair framework:** identify the unclear part → ask a specific question → confirm your understanding.

> **A:** The file isn't opening.  
> **B:** Is it the attachment in the email?  
> **A:** Yes, that one.  
> **B:** Okay. I'll check whether I can open it.

## 3. Report a Workplace Problem

**Lesson 02.**

| Problem | Natural sentence |
| --- | --- |
| Device failure | **The computer isn't working.** |
| Missing supplies | **The printer is out of paper.** |
| Connection problem | **The internet is very slow today.** |
| System issue | **There's an issue with the system.** |
| Email access | **I can't access my email.** |
| File issue | **The file isn't opening.** |
| Air conditioning | **The AC isn't working properly.** |

**Useful sequence:** **problem → what you already tried → requested next step**.

> **The file isn't opening. I've tried downloading it again, but that hasn't helped. Could you send another copy?**

**Responses:** **I'll check it now / Please try again in a few minutes / I'll contact support / Please let me know when it's fixed.** Give a specific wait time only when you have a reasonable basis for it.

**Issue** and **problem** are both normal workplace words. **Access** takes an object directly: **access the system**, not **access to the system** when **access** is the verb.

## 4. Order Food and Make Polite Requests

**Lessons 05 and 07.**

| Purpose | Expression |
| --- | --- |
| Ask for the menu | **Could I see the menu, please?** |
| Order | **I'd like the vegetable soup, please.** |
| Ask for a drink | **Could I have a glass of water?** |
| Offer something | **Would you like something to drink?** |
| Accept or decline | **Yes, please. / No, thank you.** |
| Ask the price | **How much does it cost?** |
| Ask for the bill | **Could we have the bill, please?** |

**Would you like…?** offers something; **I'd like…** states your own request. Asking the price and requesting the bill are different actions.

> **Server:** What would you like to order?  
> **Customer:** I'd like the soup and a salad, please.  
> **Server:** Would you like something to drink?  
> **Customer:** Yes, a glass of water, please.  
> **Customer, later:** Could we have the bill?

**I want…** is grammatical but can sound blunt in this situation. Ordinary polite forms do not require an unusually formal setting.

## 5. Shopping and Returns

**Lessons 03 and 19.**

| Need | Added expression |
| --- | --- |
| Explain a preference | **I like this shop because it has a wide range of products.** |
| Explain convenience | **I can buy most of what I need in one place.** |
| Describe travel | **I usually go there by car.** |
| Ask about an item | **Do you have this in a different size?** |
| Describe a mismatch | **The item doesn't match the description.** |
| Ask about a return | **Could you explain the return policy?** |
| Request an exchange | **I'd like to exchange this for a different size.** |

**Meaning checks:** **By car** is transport; **buy a car** is a purchase. **Shop** and **show** are different words. **A refund** and **an exchange** are different requested outcomes; do not assume a particular store must provide either in every situation.

## 6. Plans, Availability, and Following Up

**Lessons 01, 05, 06, 07, and 16.**

- **I'm available on [day] after [time].**
- **I'm meeting a friend tomorrow.**
- **I'm hoping to have some time off next month.**
- **My next event will be in [month].**
- **Could we move the lesson to another day?**
- **I'll send you the details later.**
- **Please remind me to check the time.**

The scheduling and follow-up examples are added practice, not newly confirmed arrangements.

Use **a day off** for a day without work; **a holiday** may refer to a public holiday or a holiday period, depending on context and dialect. **Will be in [month]** needs **be**, and the month normally has no **the**.

## 7. Give a Short Update About Your Day

**Lessons 04, 05, 16, and 19.**

**Pattern:** current situation → recent reason → effect or next step.

> **Work has been busy recently because we're preparing for a major event. I worked late yesterday, so I'm keeping this evening fairly quiet. I might read for a while and get an early night.**

Useful options:

- **I've been under pressure / It's been a busy week / I have quite a lot to finish.**
- **I need a break / I'd like some time to unwind / I'm looking forward to a quieter evening.**
- **I worked all day / I finished late / I had the day off.**

These options have different meanings. **Finished late** does not necessarily mean worked longer than usual; **worked all day** and **had the day off** describe opposite situations.

## 8. Cross-Cultural Explanations

**Lessons 02, 03, 07, and 19.** Start with a concrete description, give context, and avoid turning one person's experience into a rule about an entire country.

> **Pinyin uses Latin letters to represent Mandarin pronunciation. With a Pinyin input method, you type a pronunciation and choose the characters you mean.**

> **Some restaurants allow pets, but the policy depends on the business and local rules.**

For clothing, food, or customs, **In my experience… / In some places… / The practice varies…** helps keep the scope accurate. Explain unfamiliar terms instead of assuming the listener already knows them.

## 9. Role-play and Review

1. Report a file problem, explain one attempted solution, and ask for help.
2. Order a meal, decline a drink politely, and request the bill.
3. Ask about returning a product that does not match its description.
4. Explain when you are available and propose another time if necessary.
5. Explain one familiar cultural practice in three clear sentences.

**Quick corrections:**

- **I can't access to my email.** → **I can't access my email.**
- **Would you like a drink?** → **Already correct as an offer.**
- **I go there buy a car.** → **I go there by car**, if transport is intended.
- **My break will in [month].** → **My break will be in [month].**

**Self-check:** Did I make the purpose clear? Did I ask for the exact help I needed? Did I confirm important details? Was my tone appropriate to the situation?
