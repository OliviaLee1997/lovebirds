## 2. Develop a Position with a Clear Connection

**Position → reason → example or consequence → qualification.**

> **I think [position], mainly because [reason]. For example, [specific illustration]. This can [consequence]. However, [a relevant limit or condition].**

You do not need all four parts in every answer. The explanation should show why the reason supports the position; merely adding **because** is not enough.

**Added model:**

> I think libraries benefit from having separate areas for different activities. Quiet rooms help people concentrate, while other spaces can support discussions or events. For example, a student revising for an exam has different needs from a reading group. The challenge is organising the space so that one activity does not make the other impossible.

## 3. Match the Structure to the Question

| Question type | Useful structure | Starter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Why?** | cause → mechanism → consequence | **One possible reason is that…** |
| **Compare** | same dimension → A → B → suitability | **One important difference is…** |
| **Advantages and disadvantages** | benefit → drawback → context | **A benefit is…, although…** |
| **How to solve it?** | actor → action → mechanism → limitation | **One practical step would be to…** |
| **Who is responsible?** | role → capability → cooperation | **Schools can…, while families can…** |
| **What might happen?** | qualified prediction → reason → uncertainty | **I would expect…, particularly if…** |
| **Is it always true?** | condition → contrasting cases → judgement | **Not necessarily. It depends on…** |

**It depends** needs a deciding factor, such as cost, access, the person's needs, or the local context.

## 4. Technology, Online Habits, and Business

**Lessons 05, 07, and 19.**

Possible benefits include easier access, saved time, and new ways to reach customers. Possible difficulties include distraction, errors, unequal access, financial pressure, and changes to existing jobs.

**More precise claims:**

- **Online shopping can put pressure on some physical shops; others combine online and in-person sales.**
- **An online lesson can complement classroom learning or compete with it, depending on the service.**
- **Streaming changes viewing options without eliminating every reason to visit a cinema.**
- **Running a business can give someone more control, but it may also involve longer hours and uncertain income.**

For the pet economy, distinguish **a possible motivation** from a statement about all owners. Companionship, personal preference, and willingness to spend on care may matter in different ways. Local pet-entry or registration policies require local evidence.

## 5. Environmental Problems: Explain How a Solution Works

**Lessons 09 and 10.**

**Situation → cause → specific action → likely effect → trade-off.**

> **One way to reduce short car journeys would be to improve safe walking and cycling routes. That could make alternatives practical for people who currently drive because the route feels unsafe. It would not meet every travel need, so public transport and accessibility would still matter.**

This is an added argument, not a confirmed account of a particular city's policy.

**Precision checks from the course:**

- Recycling, reusing materials, and recovering energy from waste are different processes.
- A laboratory finding about an organism breaking down plastic is not proof of a ready solution to all plastic pollution.
- Electric vehicles can change emissions, but a complete comparison also depends on electricity, manufacturing, and use.
- Technical claims about water treatment, discharge, or radiation require reliable evidence and careful definitions.

Avoid relying on invented percentages or a memorable example whose facts you cannot explain.

## 6. Culture, Celebrities, and Identity

**Lesson 12.**

**Cultural preservation:** Discuss what is valued, how it is passed on, and whether a practice causes harm. Shared identity and cultural diversity can coexist. A common language need not erase other languages.

**Celebrity discussion:** Separate interest in someone's professional work from access to their private life. Professional achievement does not establish expertise in unrelated fields.

**Added model:**

> I think cultural traditions are worth understanding, but preserving them should involve discussion rather than automatic acceptance. A craft or celebration may strengthen a sense of belonging. If a practice causes harm, people can question or adapt it while still respecting their community's history.

**Useful language:** **cultural heritage / a sense of belonging / pass knowledge on / respect privacy / distinguish public work from private life**.

## 7. Equality, Prejudice, and Careful Comparisons

**Lessons 07, 13, and 14.**

- **Prejudice:** a preconceived judgement, often unfair, about people or groups.
- **Discrimination:** unequal treatment based on a person's group or characteristics in the context being discussed.
- **Stereotype:** a generalised belief about a group that may overlook individual differences.
- **Colourism:** bias or unequal treatment based on skin tone, including within a racial or ethnic group.

Do not present superiority of one racial group as a fact. Attribute and assess claims: **Some people wrongly believe…**. Legal improvements do not prove discrimination has disappeared, and regional stereotypes do not establish that racism is absent from an entire country.

**Language focus:** **discriminate against someone / equal rights / judge people by their character / challenge a stereotype**. Compare equivalent things and specify the group, time, or context you mean.

## 8. Reading, Education, and Mental Health

**Lessons 08 and 18.**

**Children's books:** A preference for realistic stories can be explained without claiming that imaginative stories are useless. Consider age, comprehension, themes, and how an adult discusses the story. Listening to a book read aloud is different from reading independently.

**Paper and digital reading:** Compare the same purpose: sustained reading, annotation, portability, search, cost, or comfort. Digital readers can support notes, and distractions depend on the device and settings. Personal eye comfort is not a universal medical rule.

**Libraries:** Distinguish observations about particular libraries from a general claim about all libraries. Quiet study and community activity may both be legitimate uses.

**Mental health:** Stress, environment, and traumatic experiences can be contributing factors, not a complete explanation of every condition. A picture alone cannot establish a diagnosis. Use **may contribute to** when causation is uncertain.

## 9. Conservation, Numbers, and Sources

**Lesson 19.** Separate **personal observation**, **reported classroom information**, **verified general facts**, and **interpretation**.

For panda conservation:

- Wild range includes parts of **Sichuan, Shaanxi, and Gansu**.
- The approximately **1,860 wild pandas** cited in the lesson's WWF reference is tied to **2014**, not a fresh global total.
- Wild and captive counts are different populations.
- **Vulnerable** and **Endangered** are distinct formal conservation categories.
- A rare panda colour or a famous individual should not be described as something personally seen without evidence.

A useful spoken alternative is **Giant pandas have a limited wild range and still need conservation support**. For any population comparison, state the year and geographical definition if the exact number matters.

## 10. Review Practice

Improve these statements:

1. **Technology always makes life easier.**
2. **Fairy tales are useless.**
3. **The government should do something about traffic.**
4. **All libraries are noisy now.**
5. **There are 1,860 pandas in the world today.**

**Possible answers:**

1. **Technology can make some tasks easier when it suits users' needs.**
2. **I prefer realistic stories for some purposes, although imaginative stories can support other kinds of discussion.**
3. **Local authorities could improve bus frequency on routes used by commuters, making an alternative to driving more practical.**
4. **Some libraries I've visited have busy social areas; I prefer a separate quiet space.**
5. **The figure cited in the course refers to an estimated wild population around 2014, not today's worldwide total.**

**Speaking task:** Give an opinion about one theme, then answer **“Would that work for everyone?”** Refine the idea in response rather than repeating your first answer.
