# Tenses & Time Relationships

**Privacy note:** Personal and third-party details have been removed or generalised. First-person stories and altered quotations are anonymised study examples, not a biographical record or verbatim transcript.

**Core course basis:** Lessons 03–06, 11, 12, 15, 17, and 18. Related application appears in Lessons 01, 07–09, 14, and 19.

**Source distinction:** This is an edited synthesis of the existing course notes. The examples and review tasks are consolidated or added study material, not verified classroom quotations or new claims about personal experiences.

## 1. Choose the Time Relationship Before the Verb Form

| What you mean | Useful form | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A habit or current fact | **present simple** | **I usually read in the evening.** |
| An activity now or around now | **am/is/are + -ing** | **I'm reading a book about history.** |
| A finished past event | **past simple** | **I bought it last week.** |
| An activity in progress at a past moment | **was/were + -ing** | **I was reading when the phone rang.** |
| Experience or a result connected to now | **have/has + past participle** | **I've read three chapters so far.** |
| Activity over a period leading up to now | **have/has been + -ing** | **I've been reading more recently.** |
| An event earlier than a past reference point | **had + past participle** | **I'd finished the chapter before the call.** |
| Activity leading up to a past reference point | **had been + -ing** | **I'd been reading for an hour when she called.** |

Tense consistency means keeping these relationships clear. It does not mean keeping every verb in one tense.

## 2. Past Simple: Finished Events and Main Story Steps

**Lessons 03, 07–09, and 18.** Use past simple for an event located in a finished period or for the main sequence of a past story.

> **I checked the timetable, bought a ticket, and took the train.**

In questions and negatives, use **did/didn't + base verb**:

> **Did you buy a ticket? / I didn't buy one online.**

| Base | Past simple | Past participle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| be | **was/were** | **been** |
| go | **went** | **gone** |
| see | **saw** | **seen** |
| eat | **ate** | **eaten** |
| drink | **drank** | **drunk** |
| swim | **swam** | **swum** |
| sing | **sang** | **sung** |
| buy | **bought** | **bought** |
| catch | **caught** | **caught** |
| drive | **drove** | **driven** |
| spend | **spent** | **spent** |
| win | **won** | **won** |

**Corrections:** **didn't see**, not **didn't saw**; **caught**, not **catched**; **have swum**, not **have swam**.

## 3. Present Perfect: A Connection to Now

**Lesson 04, reinforced in Lessons 01, 11, 14, and 15.**

- **Experience:** **I've visited that museum before.**
- **Present result:** **I've lost my keys, so I can't open the door.**
- **Continuing situation:** **We've been friends for several years.**

**For + duration:** **for two years**.  
**Since + starting point:** **since [year] / since I left school**.

Neither expression mechanically determines the tense:

> **I lived there for two years, then moved away.** — finished period.
>
> **I've lived here for two years.** — continuing to now in this context.

**A finished event time:** **I visited the park in [year].**  
**A continuing connection:** **I've visited it regularly since [year].**

**No stated time** does not automatically require present perfect: **I opened the door** is natural within an established past narrative. Words such as **just**, **already**, and **yet** also vary with context and dialect.

**Useful speaking sequence:** **Have you ever tried it? → Yes, I have. → When did you try it? → I tried it last summer.**

## 4. Continuous Forms: View an Activity in Progress

**Lesson 05.**

- **Now:** **We are discussing the question.**
- **Around now:** **I'm working on a new project this week.**
- **Past background:** **We were waiting when the train arrived.**
- **Overlapping activities:** **While I was speaking, my partner was taking notes.**

The difference is viewpoint, not literal duration. **“I worked for ten hours yesterday”** is past simple despite the long duration. **When** and **while** do not automatically select a tense. A phone ringing during reading does not prove that the reading stopped.

**Include the auxiliary:** **It is growing**, not **it growing**.

## 5. Perfect Continuous: Activity Leading to a Reference Point

**Lessons 04, 06, and 11.**

> **I've been practising every week.** — a period leading to now.
>
> **I'd been practising for several months when I entered the event.** — a period leading to a past event.

The present perfect continuous can describe repeated activity, not only an uninterrupted action. **“I've been driving for four years”** does not mean driving nonstop.

**Form contrast:** **I've lived here… / I've been living here…** are both possible. **I've been lived here…** does not express residence correctly.

## 6. Past Perfect: Earlier Than a Past Point

**Lessons 06, 17, and 18.**

> **When I arrived, the lesson started.** — it began at or around my arrival.
>
> **When I arrived, the lesson had started.** — it began earlier.

Use **had + past participle**: **had gone**, not **had went**.

The reference point can be a time: **By eight o'clock, I'd finished.** A second action is not required. Neither a long time gap nor a cause-and-effect relationship is required: **I'd just sat down when the phone rang.**

**Both can be correct:**

> **I didn't call before I went. / I hadn't called before I went.**

**Before** already establishes the order. Past perfect adds an explicit earlier viewpoint; it is not a compulsory upgrade for every earlier action.

**Course-story pattern:** **I had already bought a ticket when my teacher called.** The purchase precedes the call; the subsequent story can continue in past simple.

## 7. Future Meaning: Arrangement, Intention, Prediction, or Offer

**Lesson 05, reinforced in Lessons 16 and 19.**

| Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Arrangement | **I'm meeting a friend tomorrow.** |
| Intention | **I'm going to practise more often.** |
| Decision at the moment of speaking | **I'll try that exercise.** |
| Promise or offer | **I'll send you the notes.** |
| Prediction | **The weather will probably improve.** |
| Evidence-based prediction | **Look at those clouds. It's going to rain.** |
| Timetable | **The class starts at eight.** |

These forms overlap. **Will** is not restricted to spontaneous decisions, and an arrangement does not require proof of a booking. Context can supply future time without repeating **tomorrow** in every sentence.

**Form checks:** **will spend**, **will be**, **will be spending** are valid patterns; **will spending** is incomplete.

## 8. Past Habits and Reported Plans

**Lessons 11 and 12.**

- **We used to meet every week.** — normally suggests a changed past habit.
- **We still meet regularly.** — the habit continues.
- **We've always been close.** — continuity from the past to now.

**Reported plan:** **She told me she was planning to move.**  
**Still-current plan:** **She told me she is planning to move.**

Backshift is common, but keeping the present can be appropriate if the information remains true. Compare **I thought it would help** with **I think it will help**: the viewpoint changes.

## 9. Review Practice

Complete or assess:

1. I ___ the park in [year]. *(visit)*
2. I ___ here since [year]. *(live; continuing now)*
3. I didn't ___ him yesterday. *(see)*
4. We ___ when the teacher arrived. *(talk; already in progress)*
5. I ___ already ___ a ticket when the plan changed. *(buy)*
6. I ___ every week recently. *(practise; emphasise the activity)*
7. Does **I didn't call before I went** necessarily need correction?
8. Correct **I'll spending time reading**.

**Answers:** 1. **visited**. 2. **have lived / have been living**. 3. **see**. 4. **were talking**. 5. **had…bought**. 6. **have been practising**. 7. **No**. 8. **I'll spend time reading / I'll be spending time reading**, depending on the intended viewpoint.

**Speaking task:** Describe a hobby using **how you started → what you do now → what has changed → what you plan next**. Let the time frame justify each tense change.
