# Vocabulary & Word Choice

**Privacy note:** Personal and third-party details have been removed or generalised. First-person stories and altered quotations are anonymised study examples, not a biographical record or verbatim transcript.

**Course basis:** Word-choice work across Lessons 01–03 and 06–19, with especially useful comparisons in Lessons 06, 07, 11, 17, 18, and 19.

**Source distinction:** This review groups the corrected explanations in the existing notes. Examples are consolidated or newly written study material, anonymised examples, not audio-verified quotations. A word discussed in class is not necessarily a word the student used incorrectly.

## 1. Memory: Choose the Action You Mean

**Lessons 06, 15, and 17.**

| Word | Meaning or pattern | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **remember** | Retain or bring something to mind. | **I remember that day.** |
| **remember doing** | Recall a completed experience. | **I remember locking the door.** |
| **remember to do** | Not forget a task. | **Remember to lock the door.** |
| **remind someone of/about something** | Cause a memory or bring something to attention. | **The photo reminds me of school.** |
| **remind someone to do** | Prompt someone about a task. | **Remind me to call tomorrow.** |
| **recall** | Remember; often more formal. | **I recall meeting her.** |
| **memorise / memorize** | Deliberately learn something for later recall. | **I memorised the key terms.** |
| **memory / memories** | Ability to remember; a remembered experience or experiences. | **a good memory / happy memories** |
| **recognise / recognize** | Identify something as familiar. | **I recognised his face but couldn't remember his name.** |

Do not reduce these distinctions to “voluntary versus involuntary.” A smell can remind you of a place, and a memory can arise without deliberate effort.

## 2. Hearing, Meeting, and Movement

**Lessons 11, 14, and 17.**

- **Listen to** focuses on attention: **I listened to the instructions.**
- **Hear** focuses on perception: **I heard a noise / I can hear you.** Hearing is not always accidental.
- **See** can mean perceive visually or visit someone: **I saw her across the room / I'm seeing her tomorrow.**
- **Meet** can describe an arranged meeting or an unexpected encounter: **We arranged to meet / We met by chance.**
- **Run into / bump into** clearly signals an unexpected encounter.
- **Approach** takes a direct object: **I approached the teacher / the building.**
- **Go up to** works with people and things: **I went up to him / the window.**

For a planned but unannounced visit, say **I visited without contacting them beforehand**. Do not automatically describe it as a chance encounter.

## 4. Feelings, Qualities, and Word Forms

| Intended meaning | Useful language |
| --- | --- |
| My reaction | **I felt embarrassed / relieved / grateful / proud.** |
| Something causing embarrassment | **an embarrassing moment** |
| The noun for the feeling | **The mistake caused embarrassment.** |
| Positive satisfaction | **proud of an achievement / take pride in your work** |
| An assumption of superiority | **arrogant / arrogance** |
| Ability to understand and learn | **intelligent** |
| Engagement with thought and ideas | **intellectual interests / an intellectual** |
| Arriving at the agreed time | **punctual** |
| Feeling extremely cold | **I'm freezing.** |
| Getting along easily with others | **friendly / easy-going** |
| Excitement about something | **enthusiastic about a project** |

**Lessons 05, 07, 11, 12, and 17.** **Proud** is not automatically negative, and **enthusiastic** does not simply mean friendly. **Convenient** means practical; **compatible** or **a good match** describes people who suit each other.

## 5. Competition, Rewards, and Precise Alternatives

**Lessons 07, 09, 10, and 18.**

- **Competition / contest:** an organised event in which people compete.
- **Rivalry:** a competitive relationship, as in **a rivalry between two teams**.
- **Award / prize:** formal recognition or something won.
- **Reward:** a benefit or payment for an action, effort, or service.
- **Stage / phase:** a part of a process. **Round** fits a contest round only when that is the actual format.

> **We entered a competition, reached the next stage, and won an award.**

Replacing **competition** with **rivalry** here would change the meaning. After introducing an event, **it**, **the event**, or a more specific description may reduce repetition without forcing a synonym.

**Collocations:** **take part in a competition / give a presentation / get into a university / regain confidence / take someone's advice / learn from an experience**.

## 6. Quantity Is Different from Variety

**Lessons 05 and 19.**

| Expression | Main use | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **many** | Large countable quantity. | **many products** |
| **a number of** | Several or a quantity of countable items; not necessarily very many. | **a number of options** |
| **a great deal of** | Large uncountable amount. | **a great deal of time** |
| **various** | Several different examples or types. | **various products** |
| **diverse** | Significant variety or differences. | **a diverse customer base** |
| **multiple** | More than one. | **multiple items** |

**A wide range of products** describes variety. **Everything I need in one place** describes convenience. **Multiple** alone does not mean one shop supplies all the items.

**Buy / purchase / get:** **Purchase** is more formal. **Get** is broader than buying. **Frequent**, as a verb, means visit regularly but is relatively formal; **I often go there** remains natural.

## 7. Useful Phrases by Topic

| Topic | Expressions |
| --- | --- |
| Work | **an issue with the system / access my email / improve work efficiency / be under pressure** |
| Relationships | **get on well with / have things in common / turn to someone for advice / support each other** |
| Relaxation | **switch off / unwind / clear my head / take a break from my routine** |
| Places | **a tourist attraction / within walking distance / rent a boat / avoid the crowds** |
| Reading | **get absorbed in a book / write notes in the margins / search for a phrase / concentrate for longer** |
| Culture | **cultural heritage / a sense of belonging / pass traditions on / cultural exchange** |
| Society | **prejudice / discrimination / equal rights / regional stereotypes** |
| Business | **keep a pet / pet-related services / financial risk / physical retailers / domestic products** |

**Meaning checks:** **Rent** is temporary paid use; **buy** is ownership. **Domestic** refers to a country; **local** may refer to a smaller area. **Prejudice** and **bias** overlap, but **bias** also has technical uses. **History** as a subject differs from a particular **story** or several historical accounts.

## 8. Review Practice

1. The photo ___ me of school. *(remembers / reminds)*
2. I ___ his face but couldn't remember his name. *(recognised / memorised)*
3. The restaurant's ___ prepared the meal. *(cooker / chef)*
4. We won a ___ in the contest. *(prize / rivalry)*
5. The website gives a great deal of ___. *(information / products)*
6. Is **I met her by chance** incorrect because **meet** must be intentional?
7. Explain the difference between **many products** and **various products**.

**Answers:** 1. **reminds**. 2. **recognised**. 3. **chef**. 4. **prize**. 5. **information**. 6. **No; meet can describe chance encounters.** 7. **Many emphasises quantity; various emphasises different kinds or examples.**

**Application:** Choose three pairs and write a situation in which replacing one word with the other would change the meaning.
